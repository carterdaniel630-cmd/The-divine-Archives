Record, tradition, uncertainty

The text. The Zhouyi, or Changes of Zhou, is the core stratum of the work now called the Yijing. The received book joins sixty-four hexagram statements to line texts. Dates, compositional stages, and original uses remain debated; no single legendary attribution should be presented as settled history.

The commentarial tradition. Later interpretive works known collectively as the Ten Wings became inseparable from the classic in the received tradition. Traditional accounts connect them with Confucius; modern scholarship does not treat that authorship as established.

The coins. Three-coin casting is a later, compact method for generating values 6, 7, 8, and 9. It gives changing yin and changing yang equal odds, unlike the reconstructed yarrow-stalk procedure. This exhibit uses local pseudorandomness; it makes no claim to supernatural causation.

How to read. The named hexagram, its traditional Judgment and Image, and any changing-line text belong to a long interpretive practice. They are offered here as the tradition presents them: material for reflection, not verified prediction. Translation is never neutral; the full public-domain Legge edition is linked below.
