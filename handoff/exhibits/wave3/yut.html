The Lunar New Year game of Korea · a family race of sticks and stars

Yutnori is Korea’s beloved folk board game, played by families from Seollal (Lunar New Year) through the first full moon — named a National Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Rules of the game

ATTESTEDTHE THROW

  • Four chestnut-wood sticks, each with one flat side and one round side, are cast like dice.
  • Count the flat sides facing up: 도 do (pig) = 1 · 개 gae (dog) = 2 · 걸 geol (sheep) = 3 · 윷 yut (cow) = 4, throw again · 모 mo (horse, all round) = 5, throw again.

ATTESTEDTHE BOARD · 말판 MALPAN

  • 29 stations: an outer circuit of 20, with two diagonals crossing at the center station (방, bang).
  • One throw moves one mal (token); a throw is never split between mals.
  • Landing on a rival’s mal captures it — back to the start — and earns another throw.
  • Landing on your own mal stacks them; the stack travels as one.
  • A mal that lands on a corner may take the diagonal shortcut on its next move. At the center, choose your direction.
  • Exact count to bear off past the start. First side to bring every mal home wins.

SIMPLIFIEDIN THIS EXHIBIT

  • 2 mals per side — traditional teams play 4.
  • Entering a mal at the start uses up the throw (a common casual rule; house rules vary).
  • Sticks are perfectly fair (50/50 each) — real carved sticks vary.
  • The back-do (one marked stick sending a mal one step back) is omitted.
  • The center always offers the direction choice on arrival; some house rules only allow it when landing exactly.
Rules after the standard malpan as described by the Cultural Heritage Administration via Korea.net, the Korea JoongAng Daily, and the Yunnori articles on Wikipedia. Early roots (possibly the Baekje game jeopo) are debated by historians — we present only what is documented.
Every rule above is labeled attested (documented in the sources) or simplified (our exhibit choice). Nothing here is a guess dressed as fact — where scholars disagree, we say so.
