Rules & sources

AttestedThe board & the armies

A lattice of 9 × 5 points — 45 in all — with diagonals drawn through alternating squares. Points where diagonals meet are strong; the rest are weak, and pieces travel only along the lines. Each side fields 22 stones; every point is occupied except the center. Bone (white) moves first.

AttestedThe two captures

  • Approach: step toward the enemy so you land beside them — every unbroken line of enemy stones beyond your landing point is taken.
  • Withdrawal: step away from adjacent enemy stones along the same line — every unbroken line of them behind your starting point is taken.
  • If one move offers both, you choose — you may never take both at once.

AttestedCompulsion & the chain

  • A capture, when open, must be taken — the quiet paika step is only for turns with no capture.
  • After a capture, the same stone may keep capturing, but it may not revisit a point it has already touched this turn, nor capture twice in a row in the same direction.
  • The game is won by capturing every enemy stone.

Our choicesWhat this exhibit reconstructs

  • Free choice among captures. Some traditions demand the longest capture; here you choose freely, as the literature permits.
  • Ebony, the house player, is a hand-built heuristic — greedy capture chains weighed against your reply — honest, but built to be beaten.
  • Draws. The living game has elaborate draw conventions; here only a true deadlock is called a draw.
  • Undo takes back your whole last round (your move and the house reply).
