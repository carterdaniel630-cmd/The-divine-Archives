Kōnane

The old Hawaiian game of capture — black lava stone against white coral, on a papa dark as a tide pool at night. Every move must take. The player who cannot take, loses.

How to play

  • AttestedThe papa is filled in an alternating pattern of black and white stones. (Boards of many sizes are recorded; 8×8 is used here.)
  • AttestedOpening. Black lifts one black stone from the center (or a corner); White lifts a white stone orthogonally adjacent to that hole. Black then moves first, by capturing.
  • AttestedEvery move is a capture. Jump orthogonally over an adjacent enemy stone into the empty point immediately beyond; the jumped stone is removed. No diagonal jumps. There are no non-capturing moves in the traditional game.
  • AttestedChains. A turn may continue jumping with the same stone, but only in the same direction — you may stop after any capture. At least one capture per turn.
  • AttestedWinning. The player who has no capture on their turn loses. The count of taken stones does not decide the winner.
  • Our choiceOpening used here: the black stone on c6 and the adjacent white stone on d6 — one black, one white, from the central 2×2. You play Black (first move).
  • Our choiceThe opponent is a heuristic engine (2-ply, prefers long chains). Its skill is an approximation — not tradition.
