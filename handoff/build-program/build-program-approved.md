## RELICS

### 1. Treaty of Kadesh tablet
- **What it is:** A clay tablet of the oldest surviving peace treaty — Egypt
  (Ramesses II) and the Hittites (Hattusili III), ~1259 BCE. The visitor
  turns it over and reads both sides.
- **Archive material:** Hittite & Anatolian chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** The original was a SILVER tablet — lost. The text
  survives in two forms: Akkadian cuneiform tablets from the Hittite capital
  Hattusa (Bogazkoy), and Egyptian hieroglyphic versions carved on temple
  walls (Karnak, Ramesseum). Build the Hittite record form: a clay cuneiform
  tablet, wedge-shaped script pressed into sun-baked clay, both faces
  inscribed. Show the Egyptian temple-wall version as a companion panel.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Warm clay tones, raking light so every wedge
  casts a shadow, fine cuneiform detail, worn edges, dust in the grooves.
  Lighting: warm, low, like a museum case.
- **Honesty labels:** "Original silver tablet lost. Text survives in Hittite
  cuneiform copies and Egyptian temple-wall versions." Never present the clay
  tablet as the original silver one.
- **Interaction:** Pick up, turn over, zoom into the script; tap a line for a
  plain-words translation of that clause (peace, borders, mutual aid).

### 2. Crown of the Kandake
- **What it is:** The crown of a Kushite queen who ruled as king (a Kandake —
  e.g. Amanirenas, who fought Rome). The visitor inspects it as a royal object.
- **Archive material:** Nubia & Kush chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** NO single surviving "Crown of the Kandake" object
  exists. Kushite royal crowns are known from temple reliefs (Meroë, Naqa)
  and surviving Kushite gold jewelry (Meroë pyramid treasures). Base the
  crown on those depictions + jewelry: gold, tall form, uraeus cobra at the
  brow, sun-disc and feather details as attested in reliefs, inlaid carnelian
  and lapis in the Kushite palette.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Polished gold with real reflections, deep
  lapis blue, carnelian red, warm candlelight on metal. Fine engraved detail
  on every band and mount.
- **Honesty labels:** "Reconstruction from Meroitic temple reliefs and
  surviving Kushite gold jewelry. No single original crown survives." Never
  present it as one dug-up object.
- **Interaction:** Rotate, zoom into the uraeus and inlays; tap details for
  what each element meant in Kushite kingship.

### 3. Etruscan bronze mirror
- **What it is:** An engraved bronze hand mirror — disc + handle — with its
  figures named in Etruscan. The visitor holds it and reads the engraving.
- **Archive material:** The Etruscans chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** Real Etruscan mirrors (c. 5th–3rd c. BCE, e.g. in
  the Vatican, British Museum, Met): a polished bronze disc with a handle,
  the back engraved with mythological figures, often with Etruscan name
  labels incised beside them. Engraving is fine line-work, not relief.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Aged bronze — warm gold-brown with verdigris
  in the recesses — mirror face catching light, crisp incised line-work,
  worn handle from handling.
- **Honesty labels:** Figures named from the incised Etruscan labels; where a
  label is uncertain, say so. The mirror is a type, not one specific museum
  piece — say which real mirrors it draws on.
- **Interaction:** Turn it over (polished face / engraved back), zoom into the
  engraving; tap a figure for its name and story.

### 4. Scythian gold stag
- **What it is:** A golden stag plaque in the steppe animal style — the
  Scythians' signature art. The visitor inspects it up close.
- **Archive material:** The Scythians & the Steppe chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** The famous gold stag plaque from Kostromskaya
  (Kuban), 6th c. BCE, Hermitage: a reclining stag with folded legs,
  sweeping antlers that curl into the animal-style spirals, made as a shield
  or harness ornament. Gold, with the characteristic stylized, flowing
  animal forms.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Bright worked gold, deep shadow in the
  incised lines, antler curls rendered in full, steppe palette (gold, deep
  green, bone). Light that makes the gold glow without washing it out.
- **Honesty labels:** "After the Kostromskaya stag plaque tradition. Animal
  style is stylized by design — the curls are the art, not a photograph of
  a deer."
- **Interaction:** Rotate, zoom into antler curls and legs; tap for how the
  animal style works and what the stag meant on the steppe.

## GAMES

### 5. Tabula
- **What it is:** Roman dice-and-board race game, ancestor of backgammon.
  Board game form — must look and play like a board game, with a
  mystical/ethereal feel matching its tradition (standing game-form rule).
- **Archive material:** Rome chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** Described in the 6th-c. epigram of Agathias (the
  Emperor Zeno game). Played with dice and 15 pieces per side on a board of
  three rows. CLAUDE MUST VERIFY the exact board layout against the Agathias
  epigram + scholarly reconstructions before building — do not guess the
  row/point counts. Label the reconstruction plainly.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Roman board — wood, bone, ivory pieces; warm
  lamp light; the board reads as a real object on a table, not a flat diagram.
- **Interaction:** Full game vs. AI or pass-and-play; dice roll, legal-move
  highlighting, undo, tutorial. Rules shown from the Agathias account;
  reconstructed rules labeled as reconstruction.

### 6. Awithlaknannai (Zuni "fighting snakes")
- **What it is:** The Zuni board game — two lines of pieces ("snakes")
  fighting across a grid. Board game form.
- **Archive material:** Native North American chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** Zuni (Ashiwi) game documented in ethnographic
  sources (Culin and others). Grid board with diagonal lines; each side has
  6 pieces; capture by jumping over an enemy piece. CLAUDE MUST VERIFY the
  board layout and rules against the ethnographic sources before building —
  do not guess.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Desert palette — sandstone, turquoise, clay
  red; the board reads as a real object; pieces with weight and shadow.
- **Interaction:** Full game vs. AI or pass-and-play; legal moves, undo,
  tutorial. Present with respect: this is a living people's game. Label the
  sources. No sacred framing it does not have.

### 7. Sort the Seals
- **What it is:** A sorting game: sort Indus seals by their carved animals
  (unicorn, bull, elephant, rhino, tiger). Board/table form.
- **Archive material:** Indus Valley chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** Real Indus stamp seals (Mohenjo-daro, Harappa):
  small steatite seals, ~2–4 cm, carved animals above a line of Indus
  script. The unicorn is the most common animal. The Indus script is
  UNDECIPHERED — this is a hard fact, not a gap to fill in.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Steatite tones — pale stone, worn edges,
  crisp carved animals; warm light; the seals read as small real objects
  you pick up and sort.
- **Honesty labels (binding):** Motifs ONLY — no invented meanings, no
  invented readings of the script. Every seal card says: "Script
  undeciphered. We sort by animal, not by meaning."
- **Interaction:** Drag seals into animal groups; score on correct sorting;
  tap a seal for its site and animal. Difficulty levels. No "decode the
  script" mechanic anywhere.

## IDEAS (walkthrough spaces / experiences)

### 8. The Mithraeum
- **What it is:** Descend into a cave-temple of Mithras — seven grades of
  initiation, the bull-slaying scene (tauroctony) at the end. Walkthrough
  space.
- **Archive material:** Roman mystery cults chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** Real mithraea (e.g. under San Clemente, Rome;
  Carrawburgh, Hadrian's Wall): underground or cave-like halls, central
  aisle, raised benches (podia) along both sides, tauroctony relief at the
  far end. Seven grades: Corax, Nymphus, Miles, Leo, Perses, Heliodromus,
  Pater.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Cave stone, torchlight, deep shadow, the
  tauroctony lit as the focal point. Solemn, hushed — not a haunted house.
- **Honesty labels (binding):** No Mithraic scripture survives. Rites are
  reconstructed from archaeology + iconography — label every reconstructed
  element as reconstruction. Do NOT invent secret doctrine.
- **Interaction:** Walk the hall, sit on the benches, approach the
  tauroctony; the seven grades presented as a progression of learning, not
  a fake initiation. Observational — witness, don't perform.

### 9. The Fire Temple
- **What it is:** Tend the eternal flame (Atash) at the heart of the rite.
  Walkthrough space.
- **Archive material:** Zoroaster chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** Zoroastrian fire temples (agiary): the sacred
  flame kept burning, tended by priests, purity rules around it. The flame
  is the focus of worship — not an idol, but the visible sign of Asha
  (truth/order).
- **Art direction (top tier):** Firelight as the main light source — warm,
  flickering, real shadows; stone, brass, clean lines; the flame itself
  rendered with depth and movement, not a flat glow.
- **Honesty + respect (binding):** Fire temples have restricted access in
  real life — non-Zoroastrians are generally not admitted to the inner
  sanctum. This is a respectful WITNESS, not a simulation of priesthood.
  The visitor tends the flame as an act of care and learning, never as a
  fake priest. No mockery, no spectacle.
- **Interaction:** Tend the flame (fuel, airflow, attention), learn what the
  flame means; quiet, reverent pacing.

### 10. Langar, the Free Kitchen
- **What it is:** Sit, serve, and eat together — everyone equal at the
  table. Walkthrough experience.
- **Archive material:** Sikhism chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** Langar as practiced TODAY in gurdwaras
  worldwide: free kitchen, everyone sits together on the floor (pangat),
  served by volunteers (seva), no distinction of rank, caste, or faith.
  This is a LIVING practice — present it as it is, not as history.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Warm kitchen light, steam, real food detail,
  the hall full of people sitting together; the mood is welcome and
  equality, not ceremony.
- **Honesty + respect (binding):** Living practice — get it right. Show
  seva (serving others) as the heart of it. No mockery, no exoticizing.
  The equality is the point: everyone sits at the same level, eats the
  same food.
- **Interaction:** Join the line, sit, serve, eat; learn what langar means
  from the practice itself.

### 11. Cave painting wall
- **What it is:** Paint with ochre by hand — the first art. Walkthrough /
  hands-on experience.
- **Archive material:** The Paleolithic chapter.
- **Real-world reference:** Paleolithic cave painting (Chauvet, Lascaux,
  Altamira traditions): ochre (red/yellow), charcoal (black), hand
  stencils, animal forms painted on uneven cave walls by firelight.
- **Art direction (top tier):** Cave rock texture, firelight flicker, ochre
  and charcoal pigments with real texture, the wall uneven and alive.
- **Honesty labels:** In the STYLE of Paleolithic cave painting — generic
  tradition, not a copy of one specific famous panel. No invented "meaning"
  for the paintings; what they meant to their makers is unknown — say so.
- **Interaction:** Paint with ochre by hand (finger / brush), hand stencil,
  blow-pigment; the wall keeps your work for the session. Quiet, awed
  pacing — this is the first art humans made.

## REVAMP

### 12. Crown of Thorns (Crown of Jesus of Nazareth) — REVAMP
- **Carter's order:** "It's in the Divine Archives already. That one needs a
  serious revamp in terms of detail and that thing is barely a crown at all."
- **What was found (CORRECTED 2026-10-16 — Carter saw it himself):** The
  rendering EXISTS. It lives in the museum code as entry `CAT.v06` in
  `docs/museum/relics.js`, in the Scholasticism room (Wing VII, High
  Medieval), alongside the Codex Gigas. Same single rendering also
  referenced from the Sacred Kingship and Eastern Orthodoxy rooms. The
  earlier "no rendering found" note was WRONG — it only searched
  Muse-side files and the Vault text entry. This is a REVAMP of the
  existing rendering, not a fresh build.
- **Photo evidence (Carter's screenshot, 2026-10-16):** The photo
  proves it. The crown is a tangle of golden hoops — a thick pale-gold
  torus wrapped with many thin gold rings standing at odd angles, loops
  sticking out sideways. It reads as a pile/coil of hoops, NOT a crown.
  Carter: "that's obviously not a crown."
  The biggest shape is a thick glass ring around the bundle, nearly
  invisible, so the eye sees a ghost ring or just a stack of hoops. Flat
  painted stripes, not woven fiber — no knots, no fraying. One fixed
  camera angle, no way to turn it. Small offsets are random every visit,
  so it stacks slightly differently each time — reads as sloppy, not
  deliberate. The honest part is right: it is modeled as rushes (the Paris
  relic really is a ring of rushes, no thorns) and the label says so.
- **What the revamp must fix:** Real woven structure — strands that
  actually interlace and cross, visible fiber, knots, a band with real
  height, not nine parallel hoops. Thorn barbs woven into the circlet,
  clearly labeled (thorns = Gospel image; Paris relic = rushes — both
  truths shown, never mixed up). The glass ring becomes a quiet display
  case, not the biggest shape. More than one viewing angle, and
  something for it to sit on (cushion or stand) so it reads as an object
  on display, not a floating coil.
- **Archive material:** Vault entry V06 (Crown of Thorns) + Gospels Mark
  15:17, Matthew 27:29, John 19:2.
- **What the real things look like — TWO things, kept distinct:**
  1. **The Gospel crown (the build's primary form):** The soldiers "wove"
     (Greek *pleksantes*) a crown of thorns — a WOVEN CIRCLET of thorny
     branches, a parody of royal insignia, an act of mockery. Build it as
     a woven circlet: interlaced thorny branches, thorns visible along the
     weave, a ring that reads unmistakably as a CROWN — NOT a jeweled
     royal crown, NOT a plain ring, NOT a book. This is the form Carter
     ordered: it must read as a crown.
  2. **The Paris relic (the honest-evidence layer):** What Paris actually
     holds is a circlet of bundled RUSHES bound with gold thread, ~21 cm
     across, with NO thorns (thorns were detached over centuries and given
     as gifts). Sealed in crystal + gold, kept at Notre-Dame (re-enshrined
     Dec 2024, new reliquary). Present this truthfully alongside the
     woven form: a second view / companion panel showing the rush ring as
     it actually is. The hypothesis that the rush ring once supported
     thorny branches is a HYPOTHESIS — label it as such, never as fact.
     The ring has NOT been radiocarbon-dated; age/origin before the 13th
     century is unknown — say so.
- **Art direction (top tier):** The woven thorn circlet: every branch
  individually modeled, thorns sharp and distinct, weave visible, warm
  wood-brown tones, dramatic side light so the thorns cast shadows.
  The rush ring: pale bundled stems, gold thread binding, crystal + gold
  setting. Both lit reverently — candlelight, dark background, hushed.
- **Respect (binding):** Sacred Christian relic. NO mockery, NO gore, NO
  spectacle. Reverent presentation throughout. The mockery in the Gospel
  account is presented as the soldiers' act, not repeated by us.
- **Interaction:** Rotate the woven circlet; switch views (Gospel crown /
  Paris relic); zoom into the weave; read the Gospel text, the documented
  history (Baldwin II → Louis IX 1238 → Sainte-Chapelle → Revolution →
  Notre-Dame 1806 → 2019 fire rescue → 2024 re-enshrining), and the
  evidence labels. The two forms are NEVER conflated — the visitor always
  knows which one they are looking at.
