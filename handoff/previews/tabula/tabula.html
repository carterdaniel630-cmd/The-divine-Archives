“Board of 24 points, 12 a side — the 19th-century reconstruction of Tabula from Agathias’s epigram on Emperor Zeno’s game (played c. 480 AD, recorded c. 530). The three-row board belongs to its ancestor, Ludus Duodecim Scriptorum; Tabula drops the middle row. Three dice, fifteen pieces a side entering from off the board, lone pieces vulnerable — all from the same reconstruction; details such as bearing off are not certain. Point shapes are stylized. No complete Roman tabula board survives; the Gloucester set (found 1983, in an 11th-century pit) is the only complete tables set ever found.”
