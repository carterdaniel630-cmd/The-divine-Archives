THE ARCHIVIST'S TABLE

Five thousand years ago, merchants of the Indus cities pressed small steatite seals into clay to mark their goods — each barely an inch or two across, each carved with an animal above a line of script.

Drag each seal into the tray of its carved animal: the unicorn, the humped bull, the elephant, the rhinoceros, the tiger.

Tap any seal to study it — its site, its animal, its size. Nothing here is invented: no meanings, no readings.
