# Naming & IP Policy — The Divine Archives
**Binding on all builds, content, and copy. Ordered by Carter, 2026-10-13.**

## The rule
Use GENERIC names for everything. Never borrow the commercial identity of
another creator's work — not in titles, not in descriptions, not in "style"
references, not in code, not in marketing.

## What this bans
- **Trademarked names:** Pac-Man, Indiana Jones, Zelda, Mario, Star Wars,
  Disney characters, brand names — never as titles, labels, or references.
- **"X-style" references:** "Pac-Man style game" and "Indiana Jones style
  adventure" are BANNED. Saying "-style" still trades on the mark and still
  invites a claim. Describe the genre in plain words instead.
- **Copyrighted characters, plots, dialogue, lyrics, designs:** no borrowed
  characters, no retold copyrighted storylines, no quoted lyrics or dialogue.
- **Lookalike branding:** nothing that sounds, looks, or reads as a knockoff
  of a commercial property.

## What to do instead
| Banned | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Pac-Man style maze game" | "maze-chase arcade game" |
| "Indiana Jones style adventure" | "torch-lit tomb exploration" |
| "Zelda-like quest" | "open-field mythic quest" |
| Named copyrighted character | Original or mythological figure |

## What's fine
- **Public domain:** mythology, ancient history, folklore, classical texts —
  this is the archive's home ground.
- **Factual reference:** a Vault entry or exhibit ABOUT a trademarked work may
  name it factually (e.g., "the 1980 arcade game Pac-Man") — but never as the
  archive's own title, branding, or selling point, and prefer generic
  phrasing wherever the fact doesn't require the name.
- **Original creations:** Carter's own names, titles, and designs are the
  safest ground there is.

## Scope
Applies to: game/exhibit/room titles, in-exhibit text, video captions,
marketing copy, social posts, code identifiers, file names, and any
user-facing string. Applies to the website, the museum, the arcade, the
vault, videos, and all future builds.

## Enforcement
- This policy is part of the Protection Program (ready line item 1).
- The High Council's consistency auditor checks naming on every review.
- When in doubt, go more generic. There is no penalty for being too plain;
  there is real legal exposure in being too clever.
