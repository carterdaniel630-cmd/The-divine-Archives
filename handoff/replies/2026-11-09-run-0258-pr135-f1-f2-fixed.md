## What changed
**F1 (phone budget): fixed.** Each statue is now a level-of-detail pair (`THREE.LOD`), picked every frame by the
statue's **own** distance to the visitor, as the review suggested:
- **close up (within 3.2 m):** the statue mesh. Shiva (106,936) and Isis (75,000) draw their full mesh.
  Socrates (132,060) is the one statue whose full mesh, plus the Rotunda's own walls and exhibits (up to ~89k in
  some views), would pass 220k by itself: a first sweep with the full Socrates found 2 views at 220,736. So a statue
  over 115k triangles is drawn close up from a copy thinned in the browser to **at most 115k** (Socrates: 114,603),
  by vertex clustering on a millimetre-scale grid. Side-by-side at 1.4 m it is not visibly different
  (`handoff/previews/pr135/fix-near-socrates-390.png` vs the review's screenshot). Its statue page still turns the
  full mesh; nothing on any card or page claims the museum shows the full mesh, and the code header says so.
- **beyond 3.2 m:** a far copy of about 6k triangles (Shiva 6,165; Isis 5,443; Socrates 6,250) made the same way from
  the same mesh, with one grid per statue so the parts still meet. From across the room it reads as the same statue
  (`fix-far-ring-390.png`).
- The copies are made once, when each statue loads (on approach to the Rotunda, as before). Measured in Node on the
  real meshes: far copies 26–71 ms each; Socrates' thinned near copy ~0.66 s (one time, on a desktop CPU; a phone
  will be slower, and it happens while the visitor is still walking in from the hall). Not measured on a real phone.

**F2 (README): fixed.** `docs/assets/rotunda/lib/README.txt` now says the museum loads this folder's GLTFLoader and
BufferGeometryUtils against its own r186 three on purpose, and not to point it at the r160 three.module.

`app.js` changes only the `.then` that stores the counts and the `__MU.statues()` test hook (now also `nearTris`,
`farTris`, `level`). No change to cards, plaques, placement, hit boxes, loading distances or text.
