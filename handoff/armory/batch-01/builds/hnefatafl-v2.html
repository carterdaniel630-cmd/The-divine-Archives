Tournament rules · 11 × 11
  • All pieces march in straight lines.
  • Capture by closing an enemy between two allies.
  • The king escapes at a corner; raiders surround him.

I · The march

Every piece moves like a rook.

Move any number of empty squares north, south, east, or west. No diagonal steps and no leaping over another piece.

II · The capture

Close the shield wall.

Trap an enemy between the piece you moved and one of your own. The sandwiched piece is removed. The throne and corner sanctuaries may serve as the hostile far side.

III · The escape

Lead the king to a corner.

The gold king alone may rest on the central throne or any corner sanctuary. The defenders win the instant he reaches a corner.

IV · The siege

Take the king alive.

Raiders win by surrounding the king on all four sides. Beside the throne or on the board edge, two raiders complete the capture with the hostile boundary or throne.

V · The record

A reconstruction, plainly named.

The Viking-age rules did not survive. This 11 × 11 tournament ruleset descends from Carl Linnaeus’s 1732 description of tablut, a Sámi relative of tafl.

The surviving record

A family of northern games

Tafl games were played across the Norse world: unequal armies contesting a central king. Archaeology preserves boards and pieces, but not a Viking rulebook.

What survives

The 11 × 11 oak board found at Ballinderry, Ireland, dates to the tenth century and is commonly associated with tafl. Gaming pieces are known from Viking-age Birka in Sweden and from sites across Scandinavia and the North Atlantic.

What is reconstructed

In 1732, Carl Linnaeus recorded a living Sámi game called tablut. His account is the clearest historical bridge to playable tafl, though it is centuries later than the Viking Age and leaves ambiguities. This edition uses a modern 11 × 11 tournament ruleset: rook movement, custodian capture, restricted sanctuaries, corner escape, and a strong king.

This edition’s king-capture rule

Four raiders must surround the king in open ground. Beside the throne or on the board edge, two raiders complete the capture, with the hostile throne or boundary closing the trap. The rule is stated here because variants differ.
