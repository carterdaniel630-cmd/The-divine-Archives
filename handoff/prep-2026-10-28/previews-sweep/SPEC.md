## The pieces, in pickup order

### First — the three the loop itself flagged for a sensitivity review

1. **The Fire Temple** (walkthrough space; home room: **Zoroaster, ch06**). The visitor keeps a quiet vigil at an eternal flame in the manner of Yazd's Atash Behram — offers sandalwood, minds the airflow. **Built-in framing:** real fire temples restrict the inner sanctum to Zoroastrians and trained priests; the piece states the visitor is a *guest keeping vigil, not a priest* — witness, not simulation of priesthood. **THE review question, named plainly:** the visitor does tend the flame (the sandalwood offering is interactive). The entry defends it as an act of care and learning; the sensitivity review checks that call before any rebuild — if the review says witnessing only, the rebuild makes the tending observational.
2. **Langar — The Free Kitchen** (walkthrough experience; home room: **Sikhism, ch34**). Threshold etiquette is built in and real: cover head, remove shoes, wash hands; sit in the pangat row at one level with everyone; perform seva (serving others); servers eat last; lacto-vegetarian so all can eat. Living-practice framing on the entry veil ("happening today in gurdwaras around the world — walk it with respect") + five grounded info tabs. Review point: participation vs observation — langar famously welcomes everyone, which is the piece's footing; the review confirms the presentation stays on it.
3. **Awithlaknannai — "Fighting Serpents"** (game; home room: **Native North American, ch62**). A fully playable 3D rebuild of the Zuni (A:shiwi) board game from Stewart Culin's published 1907 ethnography — board topology corrected against Culin's own diagram (Murray's widely-copied version plays wrong; the entry shows the working). Presented as a living people's **game** from public sources, sources named on the page, the gaps in the record (win condition) labelled as reconstruction. Review point: confirm nothing ceremonial or restricted crept in — public-record game material only. Engine is built and unit-tested (20/20); one open design question is recorded in the entry (whether the board's end curves are traversable lines).
