    Rules of this table
    1. The board is two rows of six pits, four seeds in each, with a store at each player's right hand. attested
    2. On your turn, lift every seed from one of your six pits and sow them one by one, moving counterclockwise: across your row, into your own store, across your opponent's row, and back again. You place a seed in your own store but skip your opponent's store. attested
    3. If your last seed falls into your own store, you sow again — the store is a threshold, not an ending. attested in Kalah
    4. If your last seed falls into an empty pit on your own side, you capture that seed and every seed in the pit directly opposite, carrying them all to your store. attested in Kalah
    5. When one side's pits stand entirely empty, the game ends. Whatever seeds remain on the board are gathered into the other player's store. The most seeds in store prevails. simplified
    Honest accounting: this is a documented casual blend, not one single historical ruleset. The 2×6 counterclockwise board and the sowing gesture come from the Oware tradition of Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire; the stores, the extra turn, and the opposite-pit capture come from the Kalah tradition of the Swahili coast and its later standardizations. Oware's own attested capture takes groups of exactly two or three seeds — that rule is not used here; the simpler opposite-pit capture stands in its place. Many traditional forms use no stores at all and end the game differently. No guess here is presented as fact: what is attested is labeled; what is blended or simplified is labeled too.
