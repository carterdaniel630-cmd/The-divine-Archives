# Games Differentiation Brief — for Claude Code's rework pass

Carter's verdict after playing: the games are good, but too many look and feel
similar. Nothing is duplicated (all 25 verified distinct), but 12 of them fall
into 4 mechanical families, and the uniform dark-museum art direction flattens
them further. This brief tells the rework pass how to make each game feel like
its own artifact, not a reskin.

PRINCIPLE: keep the house coherence (they're one archive), but give every game
a dominant MATERIAL IDENTITY and a MECHANICAL SIGNATURE — the one thing the
player feels that no other game in the set does.

## Family 1: Race games (Pachisi, Patolli, Yut, Game of the Goose, + Ur/Senet/Mehen below)

- **Pachisi** — signature: the cowrie-shell throw ritual (7 shells, the cast
  table) + castle strongholds. Material: Mughal imperial — ivory, lapis,
  deep red. 4-player chaos as the marquee mode.
- **Patolli** — signature: SACRED STAKES. This was ritual gambling; players
  wagered real possessions. Lean into danger and devotion, not just racing.
  Material: Aztec — obsidian, turquoise mosaic, codex palette.
- **Yut** — signature: team play (2v2) + piece stacking/carrying + shortcut
  gambles. The only team game in the set — make that the headline. Material:
  Korean folk — warm wood, folk-art color.
- **Game of the Goose** — signature: the hazard narrative as a moral journey
  (well, prison, death). The spiral IS life's path. Material: hand-colored
  Renaissance print, not game-board wood.

## Family 2: Ancient track races (Ur, Senet, Mehen)

- **Ur** — signature: the rosettes + Woolley's royal cemetery + Finkel's
  reconstruction story. The archaeology is the game.
- **Senet** — signature: the afterlife journey — every square a stage of the
  soul's passage. Book of the Dead framing, not a race.
- **Mehen** — signature: the board IS the god — the coiled serpent protecting
  Ra. The spiral form is the selling point; no other board looks like it.

## Family 3: Chess variants (Chaturanga, Makruk)

- **Chaturanga** — signature: the fourfold army as BATTLEFIELD
  (infantry/cavalry/elephants/chariots). Ancient Indian warfare framing.
  Material: sandstone, war-elephant motifs.
- **Makruk** — signature: SLOWNESS as a feature. The grinding endgame, the
  64/32-move counting rules — frame as meditation, the opposite of blitz.
  Material: Thai temple — gold leaf, black lacquer.

## Family 4: Jump-capture (Alquerque, Fanorona, Konane)

- **Alquerque** — signature: compulsory capture chains; the Alfonso manuscript
  courtly origin. Material: medieval Iberian manuscript illumination.
- **Fanorona** — signature: THE DILEMMA. Every capture offers approach vs.
  withdrawal — render both options glowing and force the choice visibly.
  Its "perfect game" reputation (analyzed to a draw) is the story.
  Material: Malagasy rosewood, raffia.
- **Konane** — signature: the opening sacrifice — the first two stones removed
  set the entire game. Capture-only purity. Material: Hawaiian volcanic
  stone, ocean colors.

## Standalones (already distinct — protect their identity in rework)

- **Go**: ink-and-slate minimalism; the only territory game. Keep it austere.
- **Mancala (Oware)**: carved-wood sowing rhythm; the only sowing game.
- **Nine Men's Morris**: the mill as trap; medieval stone.
- **Hnefatafl**: asymmetric siege; Norse iron and frost.
- **Bagh-chal**: 4-vs-20 asymmetry; Himalayan tension.
- **Liubo**: the TLV board is the artifact; lacquered Han wood.
- **Rithmomachia**: arithmetic as combat; manuscript illumination.
- **Oracle Bones / I Ching / Astrolabe / Antikythera / Alchemist / Library
  Rescue**: instruments and oracles, not board games — already distinct.

## Hard rule for rework

If two games in the same family still feel interchangeable after rework, the
rework failed that pair. Test: describe each game in one sentence without
naming its culture — if the sentences collide, differentiate further.
