# Carter's ruling — 2026-11-16 ~02:25 UTC: the ground at your feet, alive

Given live in session ..01HFWzTkBr97DH9bJRZ5VnYJ, after his first walk of the live VR museum in his Quest 2.

His words: "the floor ecosystems are not alive and operating autonomously and it should feel as your walking on the
ground not just above it. the floor feels like it sits above the scenery in the ground."

Asked two questions with options; his picks:
1. **The ground: "Ground at your feet"** (recommended option) — raise each room's landscape to floor level and drop
   the glass: the visitor walks on the earth, plants at the ankles, water just below. **Same on phones/computers and
   in VR** (one build), not VR-only.
2. **Alive: all three** — "More creatures" (more animals/insects moving on their own, near the visitor, **true to
   each room's place and era**), "React to you" (plants part, water ripples, creatures scatter as you walk through),
   "Day and weather" (wind, light and weather change over time).

## How it is to be built (loop-side reading; Carter can change it)
- New build → per item: each piece goes to its own PR and merges only on Carter's yes.
- Pilot rooms first (ch02 Egypt, ch03 Mesopotamia, their corridor; the Rotunda has no pit), walked by Carter in the
  headset, then rolled out to the other rooms by the build loop.
- Accuracy floor holds: every added creature and plant is era- and place-true with a source logged (as `fauna.js`
  and `world.js` BIOME already are); nothing invented to fill a scene.
- Comfort holds: no falling (Comfort §a.6: water stays shallow underfoot, no drops), nothing startles within 2 m
  (§e.22 — creatures move away from the visitor, they never jump at them), flashes ≤ 3 Hz (§d.19).
- Every-Screen rule: phones keep the lighter tier; the phone budget (`tools/museum-baseline.js`) must hold.
