# Relic Fix Specs — for Claude Code

Specs only. Do NOT rebuild from this file alone — Claude does the rebuilds,
so the work is done once. Nothing here merges to main without Carter's
explicit go-ahead.

## Balance rule (Carter 2026-10-16)

Some of these are CREATIVE RENDITIONS. A relic may be given a capability it
never had so it is engaging — do NOT strip engaging capabilities for pure
accuracy. Stay as true as possible AND keep the thing fun to use. Where a
rendition departs from the real thing, label it plainly on screen instead of
silently faking it.

## General standard

Relics are HIGHLY DETAILED 3D. The bar is "hold it in your hand" — a relic
must read as a real object you can pick up, turn over, and inspect, with
true depth, materials, and detail. Flat front-view drawings do not meet the
bar (see the general note at the bottom).

## 1. Emerald Tablet — TWO DIFFERENT RENDERINGS, DO NOT CONFUSE THEM

- **Video still:** Slab form, green stone, rough chipped edges, thickness.
  The form is CORRECT — a tablet, not a book. What is wrong: the carving
  is all shapes and diagrams. The legends say the tablet was covered with
  *writing* — lines of text. Ours has no rows of readable writing at all.
  Fix: keep the slab exactly as it is. Add carved rows of writing across
  the face. Label wherever it appears: no original survives — it is known
  from texts only.
- **Museum rendering: WRONG FORM (Carter's photo proves it).** The museum
  renders the Emerald Tablet as a BOOK — one photo shows it closed (brown
  cover, cream page block), another shows it open with two pages of tiny
  text. The file search missed this because the museum builds relics with
  generic procedural shapes and the Tablet entry uses the book shape.
  Fix: the museum Emerald Tablet must become a green stone SLAB with
  carved writing rows, NOT a book. No cover, no spine, no pages, no page
  block. Same slab treatment as the video still: thick stone, rough
  chipped edges, carved writing rows, labeled "no original survives —
  known from texts only." The video still stays as-is.

## 2. Ring of Solomon

- **What is wrong:** Ours is all gold. The legend says the ring was two
  metals — brass *and* iron, one for good spirits, one for evil. The legend
  also says four jewels were set in the ring; ours has none.
- **Fix:** Build the two-tone metal (brass + iron) and the four set jewels
  in. OR label plainly on screen why ours is all gold.
- **Stays:** Turnable signet form, domed band, stepped head, 56 border
  beads, raised pentalpha star in true star order, engraved holy names,
  hidden inner-sleeve inscription. The Ring is the bar other relics are
  held to.

## 3. Wheel of the Year

- **What is wrong:** The back has a 48-tooth gear ring and an axle — it
  reads like a machine, not a ritual wheel. Real wheels have no gears. The
  gem-like enamel and jewels are fancier than real carved or painted wood.
- **Fix:** Drop the gear ring and axle (plain back). Soften the jewels
  toward carved/painted wood. OR label it plainly as a grand ceremonial
  museum relic, not a real altar wheel.
- **Stays:** Spin/snap interaction, eight festival wedges, medallions,
  calendar ring, gold today-bead, brass pointer, hub.

## 4. Witch's Grimoire

- **What is wrong:** Too thin and too neat — the page block reads as one
  solid slab. Brass corners only on the two front corners. Clasps read as
  small blocks, not straps. No headbands. The ribbon is two stiff flat
  bars. The turning leaf is too thin and floaty.
- **Fix:** More thickness, a slight swell, covers that overhang the pages
  a little, visible gathered leaves. Matching brass on the back corners
  too. Clasps as visible straps reaching around the edge to catch on the
  back. Headbands at the top and bottom of the spine. Ribbon that drapes
  and bends. Thicker leaf that sits inside the book.
- **Stays:** Open/close, turn leaves, all seven exhibit sections,
  atmospheres. Closest of its group — the main anatomy is right.

## 5. Ouija board

- **What is wrong:** The alphabet bends into two curved rainbow arcs. The
  real board has two *straight* rows (A–M, then N–Z), and the curved arcs
  are the first thing the eye sees. The pointer is a teardrop with no
  feet; the real pointer is heart-shaped with three small legs. The board
  is too thick, the decoration too busy (zodiac ring, triangles,
  moon-phase row, branding). Letters are raised gold blocks; real boards
  have printed flat letters.
- **Fix:** Straighten the alphabet into two straight rows. Heart pointer
  with three small feet underneath. Thinner board. Calmer decoration —
  OR label it plainly as the Archives' ornate version, not the standard
  talking board. Flatten the letters if aiming for the real thing.
- **Stays:** The fortune-board answer engine, séance pacing, GOOD BYE
  closing, session journal. The engine is the engaging capability — do
  not touch it.

## 6. Gematria volvelle

- **What is wrong:** No centre pin — an empty hole where the pin should
  be, and the pin is the one thing that makes a volvelle a volvelle. Flat
  picture: no thickness, side edge, back, or handle. The answer reads on
  separate number drums below instead of on the disc. Corner gears spin
  but connect to nothing; real volvelles have no corner gears.
- **Fix:** Show the centre pin/rivet with a small knob. Give it a body —
  visible side edge, thickness, back. Move the answer onto the disc (a
  pointer or window on the disc itself). Fix or drop the corner gears.
  Decide what it is and name it truly: flat = call it a page volvelle;
  handheld = needs a rim, grip, or handle.
- **Stays:** Three-letter-ring dialing, tap-to-dial, presets, and the
  honesty line (no historical gematria machine existed).

## 7. Oracle Bones of Anyang

- **What is wrong:** The crack is a branching tree. The real crack is one
  main straight line with one side branch. The crack is shown on the back;
  it belongs on the FRONT face. The ritual order is wrong: ours has you
  carve first, then heat. The real order is hollows → heat → crack →
  carve. Only 5 hollows in one column; writing too sparse. The outline is
  too smooth and complete — the real specimen is a broken fragment.
- **Fix:** One straight crack + one branch on the FRONT face. Order:
  hollows → heat → crack → carve. If carve-first is kept for
  playability, say so plainly on screen. More hollows in rows. Denser
  columns of writing. Roughen the edge to show a fragment, OR label the
  outline as completed for display.
- **Stays:** Ox shoulder-blade shape, ridge + socket, front/back flip,
  named-specimen basis.

## 8. Astrolabe

- **What is wrong:** The top bracket is a nub, not a real bracket + ring.
  Only 9 star pointers; old instruments show ~15–20. No zodiac band. No
  centre pin + wedge clip. No front pointer arm — and no label saying
  which style it is. The body reads as a flat coin.
- **Fix:** Real bracket + hanging ring (loop). ~15–20 star pointers.
  Labeled ring of twelve zodiac signs around the star wheel. Centre pin +
  little wedge clip on the back. Add the front pointer arm (and label
  the instrument European-style) OR label it as an Islamic-style
  instrument that does not use one. Raised rim + visible edge of the
  stacked plates inside, so it reads as a dish, not a flat coin.
- **Stays:** Face-on readable disc, engraved look, sighting bar,
  calendar/degree/shadow scales.

## 9. Antikythera Mechanism

- **What is wrong:** Flat bronze plate — no wood at all. The Moon-phase
  ball is too big and sits in the centre; the real one is a small ball
  riding on a pointer arm. X-ray gears have printed numbers on them;
  real gears were never stamped. No fragments view. The crank floats
  beside the plate.
- **Fix:** Wooden box, 34 × 18 × 9 cm proportions (taller and narrower
  than now), wood sides and frame, bronze dials set into the front and
  back faces. Smaller Moon ball on a pointer arm; leave the centre as
  the axle. X-ray view: gears meshed together in a train inside the
  box, no printed numbers. Add the fragments view — the corroded green
  lumps as they look today — beside the clean reconstruction. Crank
  attached to the side of the box.
- **Stays:** Dial layout (two front rings, two back spirals with the
  right turn counts and cell counts, small games dial), crank-drag
  interaction, attested gear tooth counts.

## 10. Alchemist's Magnum Opus

- **What is wrong:** The tall neck is wrong — a long neck belongs to a
  retort, not an alembic; ours is a hybrid of two vessels. The cap is
  not a full helmet. The spout is too short. The furnace is a small
  box; a real athanor is a tall brick tower. The flask is not connected
  to the furnace heat — the fire heats nothing and the flask is heated
  by nothing. Only one vessel. The mercury pool and crystals are
  unlabeled decoration.
- **Fix:** Remove the tall neck — seat the dome cap directly on the
  boiling flask. Full rounded helmet cap. Longer spout sloping clearly
  down into the receiver. Tall brick tower furnace, clearly larger
  than the flask, fire mouth at the bottom, top opening. Flask sitting
  in a sand/water bath on top of (or inside) the furnace — one working
  setup. Add at least the retort and crucible (and ideally the pelican)
  as separate vessels. Label the mercury pool and crystals as
  decoration, or replace them with real tools (tongs, bellows, fuel).
- **Stays:** Interactive still, transmutation gameplay.

## 11. Mehen — the Egyptian coiled-serpent game

- **What is wrong:** The track is 49 separate ovals — it reads as a
  trail of glowing beads, not a snake. The head is a plain gold blob.
  No side tab. No marbles.
- **Fix:** One continuous snake body with segments marked across it.
  Scales. Tapering tail. A real head shape — ears, snout, eyes. Side
  tab or animal-head bump that real boards have, plus a holding place
  for spare pieces. Add the small round marbles that belong with the
  lion pieces.
- **Stays:** Spiral-to-centre layout, 3 pieces a side, 4 throwing
  sticks, "rules are lost" honesty.

## 12. Senet — the Egyptian game of 30 squares

- **What is wrong:** Pieces are tall chess-pawn shapes; real pieces are
  short cones or spools. The board is a uniformly dark grid; real fine
  boards are boxes — light ivory and dark wood, drawer, little legs.
  Special squares carry generic line icons. The names Horus / Isis /
  Nephthys on squares 28–30 are not settled.
- **Fix:** Short cone/spool pieces, light and dark. Box look: light
  ivory and dark wood squares with clear contrast, a drawer line,
  little legs. Real Egyptian marks (the good-sign mark on 26, water
  lines on 27) with plain-words labels. Check or soften the 28–30
  names.
- **Stays:** 30-square S-path layout, 5 pieces a side, 4 throwing
  sticks, reconstruction honesty note.

## 13. Hnefatafl — the Viking siege game

- **What is wrong:** Pieces are flat discs lying on the board; real
  pieces were standing pegs. The king is a flat crown cutout, not
  clearly taller. Corners and throne not bold. No plain label that the
  11×11 board is the modern tournament version. The king-capture rule
  is not stated on the board.
- **Fix:** Standing peg pieces with real height and thickness,
  turned-wood look. King clearly taller and bigger. Bold corners and
  throne. Label: "11×11 tournament version — the Vikings left no
  rulebook." State the king-capture rule on the board.
- **Stays:** 11×11 board, 24 attackers, 12 defenders + king, cross
  formation, movement / sandwich-capture / corner-escape rules.

## 14. I Ching — the Book of Changes

- **What is wrong:** No yarrow stalks — the older, original tool; a
  visitor never sees or touches them. Coins not always readable as cash
  coins. Line values (6, 7, 8, 9) not visible. The short paragraphs on
  the reading are not labeled as modern summaries on the reading
  itself.
- **Fix:** Add yarrow-stalk mode: a bundle of 50 thin stalks, offered
  alongside coin mode, with one line that stalks came first and coins
  later. Cash coins clearly round, bronze, square hole, characters
  around it, at every size. Show line values 6/7/8/9 so visitors learn
  why a line changes. Label the reading's short paragraphs as modern
  summaries on the reading itself; real Judgment and line texts live in
  the linked translation.
- **Stays:** Coin mode, bottom-up line build, changing lines, 64
  figures, session journal, reflection-not-prediction honesty, link to
  the public-domain translation.

## 15. Royal Game of Ur

- **What is wrong:** The non-rosette squares do not follow the real
  5-5-5 pattern (5 eyes, 5 ringed dots, 5 five-dot). The dice are flat
  triangles; real dice are little pyramids with 2 marked corners. The
  pieces are flat printed discs. The board reads as a flat printed
  grid, not an inlaid box.
- **Fix:** 5 eyes, 5 ringed dots, 5 five-dot squares. True pyramid
  dice with depth and shading, 2 marked corners each. Domed pieces
  with a little height, shell vs. dark stone. Inlaid-box read: shell
  plaques set into wood with blue and red stone inlay, visible rim,
  hollow-box feel.
- **Stays:** 20-square shape with bridge, 5 rosette squares, 7 pieces
  a side, Finkel reconstruction credit.

## 16. Library Rescue

- **What is wrong:** Straight-down view flattens it into a maze / game
  board. Scrolls are loose bundles on the floor; real scrolls were
  stored upright or lying in pigeonholes and chests, with labels. No
  reading furniture. Unclear whether it is a room you visit or a board
  you play.
- **Fix:** Tilted or eye-level view: shelves stand up, scrolls show as
  rows of roll-ends with labels, the player reads as a person walking,
  not a dot on a floor plan. Scrolls stored properly — upright in
  pigeonholes or lying in labeled rows on shelves. Add reading
  furniture: desks, stools, a catalog chest, wall labels. Decide
  room-vs-board and say so plainly.
- **Stays:** Fire-and-rescue framing, honesty note (no single night
  when all of Alexandria burned), three levels. Hybrid renderer stands
  (WebGL room + 2D actors) — UPSCALE: bigger, more detailed, fills the
  screen.

## 17. Debate Hall table

- **What is wrong:** It could not stand up in real life — a stone slab
  that big (~2.5 tonnes) balances on one central cone, each end
  sticking out nearly 4 feet past the pedestal with nothing under it.
  Nothing says "carved" — plain boxes and a smooth cone; the carving
  is surface paint and gold trim, not shape. No knee room, no chairs.
  The "compare well" is a flat gold ring lying on the surface, not a
  hollow.
- **Fix:** Legs, trestles, or a much wider base. Real carved shape:
  carved legs, carved relief, shaped edge, apron. Knee room + chairs.
  Compare well as an actual hollow you could drop something into.
- **Stays:** Black marble + gold trim, world map top, walk-up-and-gather
  framing, debate interactions.

## 18. Grand Directory

- **What is wrong:** Every room is the same size and shape — it reads
  as a diagram, not a true plan. Walls are single thin lines with no
  thickness. No door swings, stairs, windows, scale bar, or compass.
- **Fix:** Vary room sizes (some big, some small). Wall thickness.
  Door swings. Stairs. Windows. Scale bar. Compass direction.
- **Stays:** Floor-plan wayfinding, you-are-here dot, gold journey
  route, whispers key.

## 19. Bagh-chal — GAMEPLAY ERROR, not a drawing error

- **What is wrong:** The board draws both diagonals in every square, so
  pieces can move diagonally anywhere. The real board has diagonals
  only through alternating points. Our own code even calls the
  full-diagonal version "the standard board" — that claim is wrong.
  This changes which moves are legal.
- **Fix:** Diagonals only through alternating points. Correct the
  "standard board" claim in code/comments. Verify all legal moves
  against the real board.

## General note — flat pieces that need 3D rebuilds

Oracle Bones, Astrolabe, Antikythera, and Alchemist's pieces are flat
front-view drawings, not objects you can pick up and turn. Where the bar
is "hold it in your hand," those four get 3D rebuilds as specified
above (bone depth and fragment edge; astrolabe dish + rim + plates;
Antikythera wooden box; alchemist vessels with real depth and a
connected furnace).

## MUSEUM RENDERINGS — photo audit (Carter's screenshots, 2026-10-16)

Carter sent 16 museum screenshots in two batches. His eyes were right:
the file searches missed these problems because the museum builds relics
with generic procedural shapes. Every entry below is from what the
photos actually show. Bar for all of them: TOP TIER detail, color, tone,
lighting, shading — grounded in Archive information.

### M1. Generic-book problem (applies to the whole museum)

- **What is wrong:** The museum uses ONE book shape for everything
  text-ish — same flat brown cover, same cream page block, same plain
  pages. Every book reads as the same generic box.
- **Fix:** Real books (Codex Gigas, Zohar, Gospel of Judas, Nag Hammadi,
  Codex Sinaiticus) KEEP a book form but each gets its own true binding,
  size, page layout, and texture — no two alike. NON-books (Emerald
  Tablet, Shroud, True Cross, Black Stone) must NOT use the book shape
  at all — see their entries below.

### M2. Codex Gigas ("Devil's Bible")

- **Photo shows:** A large closed book, reddish-brown cover, five gold
  dome bosses on the cover, gold corner plates, cream page block.
- **What is wrong:** Book form is correct, but it reads as a toy box —
  flat brown cover with no leather grain, no tooling, no clasps or
  straps, no spine detail, page block a solid cream slab with no page
  lines.
- **Fix:** Top-tier book build: tooled leather with real grain, raised
  spine bands, metal clasps + engraved corner fittings, individually
  lined page edges, the Devil portrait as a real page. Keep the true
  scale (92 cm — the largest surviving medieval manuscript).

### M3. Crown of Thorns

- **Photo shows:** A tangle of golden hoops — a thick pale-gold torus
  wrapped with many thin gold rings standing at odd angles, loops
  sticking out sideways. It reads as a pile/coil of hoops, NOT a crown.
- **What is wrong:** No weave, no thorns, no band height. (Full revamp
  spec is item 12 in build-program-approved.md — this photo is the
  evidence for it.)
- **Fix:** Real woven interlaced strands with fiber, knots, and height;
  thorn barbs woven in and labeled (thorns = Gospel image; Paris relic =
  rushes); glass becomes a quiet display case; cushion/stand; multiple
  viewing angles.

### M4. The Zohar, First Printings

- **Photo shows:** A plain closed book, brown cover, cream pages. No
  title, no ornament, no texture — it could be any book.
- **What is wrong:** Generic-book problem at its worst. Nothing says
  Zohar.
- **Fix:** Keep book form, make it specific: 16th-century Mantua/Cremona
  printing — Hebrew title lettering on cover/spine, period leather
  binding with blind tooling, page edges, worn corners. Label: first
  printings 1558–1560; the Zohar itself first circulated in Castile in
  the 1280s.

### M5. The Shroud of Turin

- **Photo shows:** A long flat pale strip/ribbon, diagonal across the
  frame, faint triangle marks, one pale oval stain.
- **What is wrong:** It reads as a ribbon, not a 14-foot linen cloth. No
  weave, no body image, no blood marks, no herringbone texture.
- **Fix:** Real linen cloth — 4.4 m × 1.1 m proportions, herringbone
  weave texture, faint front-and-back body image (head-to-head, dorsal +
  frontal), blood marks at wrists/feet/side, burn holes + water stains
  from the 1532 fire, patches. Present flat or gently draped, never as
  a ribbon. Keep the honest labels: radiocarbon 1260–1390 (1988,
  contested); documented from c. 1354.

### M6. The True Cross

- **Photo shows:** A rectangular box/lid with a gold border and a grid
  of colored panels (blue/red/green rectangles with tiny white figures).
- **What is wrong:** It reads as a board game or a box lid. There is NO
  CROSS SHAPE AT ALL.
- **Fix:** Build it as what it is — a cross-shaped reliquary
  (staurotheke): a cross form holding wood fragments, gold/jeweled
  frame, inscription. Or show the wood itself: dark wood fragments in
  cross arrangement. Never a colored-panel box. Label: fragments
  venerated worldwide; no single verified cross.

### M7. The Black Stone of the Kaaba

- **Photo shows:** Seven or eight shiny black spheres/balls piled in a
  shallow silver bowl/frame.
- **What is wrong:** It reads as black marbles in a dish. The real stone
  is a dark stone set in a wide silver frame, embedded in the Kaaba's
  eastern corner — not a pile of balls, not a bowl.
- **Fix:** A single dark stone (fragmented, dark reddish-black, worn
  polished surface) set in its wide silver frame/border, mounted as in
  the Kaaba corner — the silver frame is a BORDER around the stone, not
  a bowl. No pile of balls.

### M8. The Emerald Tablet (museum) — see §1 above

- **Photo shows (3 photos):** Closed book (brown cover, cream page
  block); open book with two pages of tiny text; close-up of pages.
- **What is wrong:** It is a BOOK. The Emerald Tablet is a slab/tablet.
- **Fix:** Green stone SLAB with carved writing rows. No cover, no
  spine, no pages. See §1 (museum rendering). Video still stays as-is.

### M9. The Gospel of Judas (Codex Tchacos)

- **Photo shows:** An open book, close-up of two pages covered in dense
  tiny text, with black triangle artifacts (rendering glitches) scattered
  on the pages.
- **What is wrong:** Book form is correct for a codex, but the pages are
  flat cream planes with smudged text, black triangle rendering
  artifacts, no binding, no leather cover, no page edges.
- **Fix:** Keep book form, rebuild top-tier: leather-bound codex, Coptic
  text pages with real legible layout, page edges, binding. Fix the
  black-triangle artifacts (geometry/texture bug). Label: Codex Tchacos,
  radiocarbon-dated 220–340 CE, Greek original before c. 180 CE, Coptic
  Museum, Cairo.

### M10. The Nag Hammadi Codices

- **Photo shows:** An open book, two cream pages with dotted text lines.
- **What is wrong:** Book form is correct (they ARE codices), but it is
  one generic plain open book — no leather binding, no cartonnage, no
  sense of THIRTEEN books.
- **Fix:** Keep book form, top-tier: leather-bound codex with papyrus
  pages, Coptic text, and show it as one of thirteen (a set/shelf hint),
  cartonnage detail. Label: bound after the 340s CE (dated cartonnage),
  texts older, Coptic Museum, Cairo.

### M11. Codex Sinaiticus

- **Photo shows:** An open book, two cream pages with text columns,
  brown cover.
- **What is wrong:** Book form is correct, but generic — flat pages,
  smudged text, no vellum texture, no four-column layout (the real
  Sinaiticus is famous for FOUR columns per page), no binding detail.
- **Fix:** Keep book form, top-tier: vellum pages with the real
  four-column layout, Greek uncial text, leather binding, page edges.
  Label: c. 330–360 CE, oldest complete New Testament, split across the
  British Library / Leipzig University Library / St Catherine's
  Monastery / National Library of Russia.

### M12. The Holy Lance ("Spear of Destiny")

- **Photo shows:** A long thin spear lying flat — brown shaft, small gold
  sleeve, tiny point. It reads as a flat stick.
- **What is wrong:** Almost no detail — no blade shape, no texture, no
  socket, no inscription.
- **Fix:** A real lance: leaf-shaped blade with a center ridge, socket,
  wooden shaft with grain, the gold sleeve with detail, displayed on a
  stand or mount, with the Vienna lance context. Label: Vienna lance,
  early medieval (c. 7th–8th century); other claimants in Rome (St
  Peter's), Etchmiadzin (Armenia); copy in Kraków.

### M13. The Mawangdui Silk Texts

- **Photo shows:** A long folded silk strip, diagonal, pale with faint
  markings and black triangle artifacts.
- **What is wrong:** It reads as a ribbon, not silk manuscripts. Flat, no
  silk sheen, no text detail, black triangle artifacts again, no sense
  of manuscripts.
- **Fix:** Real silk — sheen, weave, ink text (Chinese characters) in
  columns, the folded/rolled manuscript form, laid on a surface, with
  the Han tomb context. Fix the triangle artifacts. Label: buried in
  Tomb 3, Mawangdui, in 168 BCE; excavated 1973; Hunan Museum, Changsha.

### M14. Siberian & Arctic Shamanism room (Wing I, Prehistory)

- **Photo shows:** A room diorama — a small owl figure on a stump in a
  sandy pit under a glass floor, sparse grass tufts, a rock, plain walls,
  wooden paneling.
- **What is wrong:** The owl is a tiny toy-like figure (smooth, no
  feather detail); the room is bare and flat; the diorama feels empty.
- **Fix:** Top-tier room detail — shamanic objects with real materials
  (drum, costume, antler), the owl with feather texture, richer ground
  (not a bare sand pit), proper lighting and atmosphere. The room needs
  its lived-practice layer (shamanic rite, honestly labeled).

### M15. The Paleolithic room (Wing I) — bear figure

- **Photo shows:** A standing bear figure on a brown pedestal — smooth,
  pale, spotted, toy-like (round ears, dot eyes, blocky limbs). It looks
  like a plush/craft bear.
- **What is wrong:** It is clearly a toy, not a rendition of Paleolithic
  art or a real bear.
- **Fix:** Decide what it is and build it truly — if it is a Paleolithic
  figurine, model it as carved bone/ivory with tool marks; if it is a
  real bear, model real anatomy and fur. Either way: top-tier detail, NO
  toy look. The pedestal/room needs cave-art context (paintings, ochre,
  stone tools) with real texture.

### M16. The Upanishads room (Wing IV, Axial Age) — BIRDS UNDER THE GLASS

- **Photo shows:** A dirt/soil diorama under a black glass grid floor;
  in the dirt, small white mushroom shapes and green grass tufts; BELOW
  the glass, small bird shapes (white bird silhouettes) sit in the dirt.
  A torch stands in the corner.
- **What is wrong:** Birds belong in the AIR / sky, not buried under the
  floor in the dirt. (Carter named this one directly.)
- **Fix:** Birds fly above the floor / in the sky, with shadows and
  grounding. Nothing living sits under the glass except what is meant
  to be buried.
- **PLACEMENT RULE (whole museum):** Audit EVERY room for animals
  sitting under the glass. Birds go in the air. Fish go in water. Land
  animals go on the ground above the glass, with shadows. No animal
  sits under the floor unless the room's story says it is buried.
