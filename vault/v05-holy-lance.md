# The Holy Lance: Four Spears and a Myth

*Vault entry V05 · Relics & Sacred Objects · Rival relics in Vienna (Imperial Treasury, Hofburg), Rome (St Peter's), and Vagharshapat, Armenia (Etchmiadzin); a copy in Kraków · Recently added — pending full review.*

In a glass case in the **Imperial Treasury** of the Hofburg palace in Vienna lies an iron spearhead about half a metre long. Its blade was broken long ago and has been bound together with a sleeve of **silver**, and over that, a sleeve of **gold**. Down its centre, set into an opening in the blade, is a slender iron pin, venerated as a **nail from the Cross**. On the gold sleeve, added for the emperor Charles IV in the fourteenth century, is an inscription: **LANCEA ET CLAVVS DOMINI**, "the lance and nail of the Lord." For a thousand years this object was part of the regalia of the **Holy Roman Empire**. It was carried before German kings, invoked in battle, and treated as the spear that pierced Christ's side on the cross.

It is **not the only claimant**. Rome, Armenia and the memory of the First Crusade each hold, or once held, a "Holy Lance." Then, in 1972, a popular occult book gave the Vienna spear a new name and a new legend: the **"Spear of Destiny"**, supposedly the object of Adolf Hitler's obsession. This entry separates the four spears from one another, and all of them from the myth.

## The text behind every lance

Only one Gospel mentions the lance. **John 19:34**:

> **ἀλλ᾽ εἷς τῶν στρατιωτῶν λόγχῃ αὐτοῦ τὴν πλευρὰν ἔνυξεν, καὶ ἐξῆλθεν εὐθὺς αἷμα καὶ ὕδωρ.**
> But one of the soldiers pierced his side with a lance, and at once blood and water came out.

The soldier is **unnamed** in John. The name **Longinus** (probably formed from the Greek *lonchē*, "lance") appears later, in the apocryphal **Acts of Pilate (Gospel of Nicodemus)** and in later tradition. That tradition turned him into a convert and a saint. **Everything after the Gospel sentence is tradition, not scripture.**

## Four relics

**1. The Vienna lance (the Holy Lance of the Holy Roman Empire).**
- **What it is.** A **winged spearhead of Carolingian type**. **Scientific examination** has placed its making in the **early medieval period**, not the first century. The metallurgist **Robert Feather**, who was allowed to remove the sleeves in **2003**, dated it to around the **seventh century**. More recent imaging studies reported in 2025 point to the **eighth century**.
- **Its history.** It is documented among the insignia of the **Ottonian** kings in the tenth century, and **Otto I** is said to have carried it at the **Battle of Lechfeld (955)**. Over time it was identified with the **lance of St Maurice**, the lance of **Constantine** and finally the **lance of Longinus**. The **gold sleeve** was added under **Charles IV** (mid-fourteenth century). It is the object of a documented cult, but **its identification with the Gospel lance is a medieval tradition, not a finding**.
- **Kraków.** The cathedral keeps a **copy** of the Vienna lance. By tradition it was a gift from **Emperor Otto III to Bolesław I of Poland** around **1000**.

**2. The Vatican lance.**
- A lance-point venerated at **Constantinople** was described by pilgrims for centuries. Parts of it reportedly went to Paris with King **Louis IX's** purchases of Passion relics in the thirteenth century; those parts were **lost in the French Revolution**.
- The remaining portion passed to the **Ottomans** after 1453. In **1492** Sultan **Bayezid II** sent it to **Pope Innocent VIII**, as part of the diplomacy over the sultan's brother **Cem**, who was then in papal custody. It is kept in **St Peter's Basilica** and has been shown during Lent.
- The Church has **made no pronouncement on its authenticity**. Its documented history begins in Byzantine Constantinople, not in Jerusalem.

**3. The Armenian lance (Geghard).**
- A spearhead kept at **Holy Etchmiadzin**, the seat of the Armenian Apostolic Church. Armenian tradition holds that the apostle **Thaddeus** brought it to Armenia. It is first attested in a **thirteenth-century** Armenian text.
- For centuries it was kept at the monastery of **Geghard**, whose very name means "lance". It was moved to Etchmiadzin in the **eighteenth century**. **No scientific dating** of it has been published.

**4. The Antioch lance (lost).**
- In **1098**, during the **First Crusade**, the besieged crusaders at **Antioch** were starving. A poor pilgrim, **Peter Bartholomew**, reported **visions of St Andrew** revealing where the lance lay buried. On **14 June 1098**, men dug in the Church of St Peter and **a spearhead was found**. The discovery rallied the army, which broke the siege.
- **Doubt was immediate.** The papal legate **Adhemar of Le Puy** was reportedly sceptical. In **1099** Peter underwent an **ordeal by fire** to prove his truthfulness and **died of his injuries** days later. The relic's later history is **uncertain**.
- This is a documented case of a relic **found by vision in a crisis**. Historians discuss it as **a study in how relics function**, not as evidence of the Gospel lance.

## The myth: "The Spear of Destiny"

In **1972** the British writer **Trevor Ravenscroft** published ***The Spear of Destiny***. It claimed that the Vienna lance carried an occult power, that whoever held it controlled the world's destiny, and that the young **Adolf Hitler** had been possessed by this idea in pre-war Vienna.

- **The documented part.** After annexing Austria in **1938**, the Nazi regime **moved the imperial regalia, including the lance, to Nuremberg**, the city of the Nazi party rallies, for political symbolism. American forces **recovered** them, and they were **returned to Vienna on 6 January 1946**.
- **The unsupported part.** Ravenscroft's **occult narrative** relied on claims said to come from an informant and from **"clairvoyant" methods**. Reviewers at the time found **no hard evidence** for it, and historians of Nazism do not treat it as reliable. The name "Spear of Destiny" and the story of Hitler's obsession come **from this book**, not from any earlier source. They were later amplified by films, games and novels.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**The inscription as claim.** The gold sleeve's **LANCEA ET CLAVVS DOMINI** is a **statement of identity** written directly onto the object. An imperial workshop added it to fix the lance's meaning at a time when its identification was still shifting between Maurice, Constantine and Longinus. It is real and legible. **It records what was believed in the fourteenth century, not what is true of the first.**

**The nail within the lance.** The iron pin set into the blade makes the Vienna lance a **double relic**: lance and nail. Crowns, reliquaries and holy lances of the period often **combined** Passion relics in this way. The combination multiplied the object's sacred power in the eyes of its keepers.

**Blood and water.** John's "blood and water" became a major **sacramental symbol**. Church Fathers read it as the **Eucharist and baptism** flowing from Christ's side, and as the birth of the Church from the side of the "new Adam". That is a documented line of interpretation. **It concerns the Gospel text, not any surviving spearhead.**

**Kingship.** Carried before rulers, the Holy Lance worked as **regalia**. It signified God-given rule and victory, in the same visual language as the imperial crown and orb.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- The Gospel of **John (19:34)** alone mentions the lance. The name **Longinus** comes from later apocryphal tradition.
- The **Vienna lance** is an **early-medieval** (c. seventh–eighth-century) spearhead with a nail-pin and fourteenth-century gold sleeve inscribed **LANCEA ET CLAVVS DOMINI**. It was part of the imperial regalia, was **moved to Nuremberg in 1938**, and was **returned to Vienna in 1946**.
- The **Vatican lance** reached Rome from **Bayezid II in 1492**. The **Armenian lance** is attested from the thirteenth century. The **Antioch lance** was "found" in **1098** after Peter Bartholomew's visions, and he **died after an ordeal by fire** in 1099.

**What is not supported**

- That **any** surviving lance has been shown to be the one described in John's Gospel. The Vienna lance is **scientifically dated centuries later**.
- The **"Spear of Destiny"** occult narrative and **Hitler's alleged obsession** as told by Ravenscroft (1972). It lacks documentary evidence.

**What's genuinely open**

- The **date and origin** of the **Vatican** and **Armenian** lances, which have **not been scientifically examined** in published studies.
- What the **Antioch** spearhead actually was, and what became of it.
