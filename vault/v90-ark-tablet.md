# The Ark Tablet: Building the Boat in Babylon

*Vault entry V90 · Texts & Tablets · Private collection; published by Irving Finkel of the British Museum*

A clay tablet about the size of a mobile phone, with **sixty lines** of Old Babylonian cuneiform, was written in Babylonia around **1900–1700 BCE**. After the Second World War an RAF serviceman, **Leonard Simmons**, brought it home to Britain among other objects he had picked up in the Middle East. His son **Douglas** later brought it to **Irving Finkel**, the British Museum's curator of cuneiform, who worked on it from **2009**. (Accounts differ on when Finkel first saw it: see the evidence section.) It tells part of the flood story, and it stops to say **how to build the boat**: how much rope, what ribs, how much bitumen. The boat it describes is not the long ark of Bible pictures. It is **round**: a giant **coracle**, the round river boat of Mesopotamia, scaled up far beyond any real one. Finkel has called the tablet "one of the most important human documents ever discovered."

## From a serviceman's souvenirs to the British Museum

- **Leonard Simmons** served with the RAF and came home after the Second World War with a small collection of antiquities. His son **Douglas Simmons** inherited them and brought the tablet to Finkel. Most reports give **2009**; one account says Finkel first saw it in **1985** and could only study it fully in 2009. (The family name is spelled "Simmonds" in some accounts.)
- Finkel worked on the text for several years and published it in **January 2014**, when the tablet went on display at the British Museum on loan. The tablet is **privately owned**, so it has no British Museum collection record; Finkel's book *The Ark Before Noah* publishes a photograph, the text and his translation, and is the edition this entry follows. This entry does not say where the tablet is shown today.
- It was shown **beside the Babylonian Map of the World**, a clay tablet whose text describes islands beyond the ocean at the edge of the known world. Finkel's reading of that text says that on one of them are **the remains of the ark**.

## What the tablet says

- **The warning.** As in the other Babylonian flood stories, the god **Enki** (Ea) speaks to the **reed wall** of the house of **Atrahasis**, "exceedingly wise", and Atrahasis overhears. He is told to pull down his house, build a boat, give up his possessions and save life.
- **The shape.** In Finkel's translation: "Draw out the boat that you will make on a circular plan; let her length and breadth be equal." The round boat is the Mesopotamian **coracle**, a basket of rope and reed sealed with bitumen, a type of boat that was still used on the rivers of Iraq in the twentieth century. The tablet does not invent a new kind of boat. It takes one its hearers knew and makes it enormous.
- **The size.** The base is to cover about **3,600 square metres**, roughly half a football pitch, a circle some 70 metres across.
- **The build.** The hull is to be made of coiled **palm-fibre rope** in stated quantities, strengthened with **wooden ribs**, and waterproofed **inside and out** with **two kinds of bitumen**. The quantities are the point: this is a flood story told for people who knew how boats were made.
- **The animals.** In the damaged closing lines the wild animals of the steppe go aboard **"two by two"**. It is the earliest known appearance of the detail now attached to Noah's ark.

## Building it

In **2014** a television team had a round boat built to Finkel's reading of the tablet, by boatbuilders in **Kerala**, India, for a documentary (shown in the United States as the NOVA programme *Secrets of Noah's Ark*). It was **scaled down**: about **40 feet across**, a fifth of the tablet's size by the documentary's own count, though some accounts say a third. It **floated**, but water came in through its seams from the moment it was launched, and the crew had to plug the gaps and set pumps working. The documentary's own conclusion is that the team never managed to reproduce Babylonian bitumen, but that the structure was sound. The test shows that the boat type works at that size. It does not show whether the full-size boat the tablet describes would have worked. This entry tells the build the same way as this archive's Flood chapter (ch01), which follows the same sources.

## What the tablet is, and is not

Finkel's own view is a sceptic's. He has said he is **"107% convinced the ark never existed."** He reads the tablet's practical detail as a storyteller's skill: a narrator, speaking to people who built and caulked boats, gave them the convincing details they were able to judge. On that reading the tablet is not a record of a real ship. It is evidence of how a sacred story was kept alive, generation after generation, by people who retold it for their own audience. Its date in the Old Babylonian period and its hero, **Atrahasis**, place it in the same stream as the ***Atrahasis*** epic, from which the flood in *Gilgamesh* (the Flood Tablet, V89) was later adapted.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**The circle.** The epic's cube stands like a building, and the ark of Genesis is a long vessel. The coracle has no front and no back. It does not need to sail anywhere: it only has to rise with the water and stay afloat. One reading of the shape, offered here as interpretation, is that survival in this telling is not a voyage but staying afloat until the water goes down.

**The building list as sacred text.** In this telling, the boat that saves life is not only commanded by a god; it is measured, supplied and built by human hands to a specification. The Mesopotamian flood stories keep answering the question "but how would it work?" The tablet's rope counts and bitumen measures are part of its story, not a side note to it.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- The tablet exists and its text is **published**: sixty lines of Old Babylonian cuneiform, translated by Irving Finkel in *The Ark Before Noah* (2014), from work he did on it from **2009**. That the owner's father brought it home after serving with the RAF is consistently reported.
- The text **orders a round boat**, equal in length and breadth, with a base of about **3,600 square metres**, built of palm-fibre rope and wooden ribs and sealed with bitumen, and its closing lines bring the wild animals aboard **"two by two"**.
- The round **coracle** is a real Mesopotamian boat type, still in use on Iraq's rivers in modern times.

**What is not supported**

- That the 2014 television build was **full size**, or that it simply succeeded. It was a scaled-down boat that floated but leaked and needed pumps.
- That the tablet is a **shipwright's record of a real vessel**. Its own translator is clear that no such ark existed.
- That the **remains of the ark** can be found on a mountain or anywhere else. The tablet ends before the flood itself and says nothing about where the boat came to rest.

**What's genuinely open**

- **When Finkel first saw the tablet.** Most reports say the owner brought it to him in 2009; the Wikipedia article on the 2014 replica says he first encountered it in 1985 and could not translate it fully until 2009. This entry does not choose between them.
- The tablet's **exact date** within c. 1900–1700 BCE. It is dated by its script and language. Where it was found is not recorded.
- How this instruction-form telling relates to the ***Atrahasis*** epic itself: an extract from it, a scribe's expansion, or a separate retelling.
- Whether a full-size boat, built exactly to the tablet with ancient materials and skills, would have kept the water out. The 2014 build does not decide it.
- The **scale** of the 2014 build: the documentary says a fifth, other accounts a third.
