# The Golden Plates: A Record No One Can See

*Vault entry V75 · Relics & Objects · Not held: returned, by Joseph Smith's account, to an angel · Recently added — pending full review.*

Before dawn on **22 September 1827**, **Joseph Smith**, a twenty-one-year-old farmer's son of Manchester, near Palmyra in western New York, went up a hill near his family's farm. He said that an angel named **Moroni** had shown him a stone box buried in its side four years earlier, and that this night, at last, he was permitted to take what was in it: a book of thin **metal plates** that looked like gold, bound with three rings and engraved in an unknown script. From them, over the next two years, he dictated the **Book of Mormon**, the founding scripture of the Latter Day Saint movement. The plates themselves, he said, were then taken back by the angel.

## The story as Smith and his witnesses told it

- **1823:** Moroni first appears to Smith and tells him of the buried record.
- **1827:** Smith receives the plates, with two seer stones set in a frame, the "interpreters". He keeps them covered and says he is forbidden to show them.
- **1828:** **Martin Harris**, a farmer who financed the book, takes a sheet of characters copied from the plates to the classicist **Charles Anthon** in New York. Harris said Anthon confirmed them; Anthon later wrote that he had told Harris they were a **hoax**. That summer the first **116 pages** of the dictated translation, lent to Harris, are **lost**.
- **1829:** With **Oliver Cowdery** as scribe, Smith dictates most of the text in about three months. **Three Witnesses** sign a statement that an angel showed them the plates; **Eight Witnesses** that Smith showed them and they "did handle with our hands".
- **1830:** The Book of Mormon is printed at Palmyra by **E. B. Grandin**.

## The text

Smith said the plates recorded, in "**reformed Egyptian**", the history of peoples who came from the Near East to the Americas, above all the **Nephites** and **Lamanites**, and the visit of the risen **Christ** to them. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and other Latter Day Saint churches hold it to be scripture alongside the Bible.

## What survives

About **28 per cent** of the original dictated manuscript survives, damaged by years in the cornerstone of the Nauvoo House; the **printer's manuscript** copied from it is nearly complete. In **2017** the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints bought the printer's manuscript from the **Community of Christ** for **$35 million**, the highest price then paid for a manuscript. A sheet of characters, the "**Caractors**" document, also survives.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**Reformed Egyptian.** The characters on the Caractors document do not match any known writing system. Latter-day Saint scholars have compared them with Egyptian scripts; Egyptologists and linguists have not accepted any match.

**Seer stones.** Witnesses described Smith dictating with his face in a hat, looking at a **seer stone**. The Church has published photographs of one such stone.

**The witnesses' statements.** Printed in every edition, the eleven testimonies are the book's own **evidence** for the plates. Latter-day Saint historians point out that none of the witnesses formally withdrew his testimony, though several later broke with Smith. What they experienced is debated.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- **Smith's account** of receiving plates in **1827**, the **witnesses' statements** of **1829**, and the publication of the Book of Mormon in **1830**.
- The survival of the **manuscripts** and the **Caractors** document.

**What is not supported**

- That the plates were **seen or examined** by anyone outside Smith's circle, or survive today.
- That the peoples of the Book of Mormon are attested by **archaeology**, **linguistics** or **DNA** studies of Native Americans, which point to Asian origins. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints has acknowledged the DNA findings in an official essay and holds that they do not settle the question.

**What's genuinely open**

- What the **witnesses** saw and handled.
- Whether the plates existed as physical objects: a matter of **faith** for believers and of debate for historians.
