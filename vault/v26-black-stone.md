# The Black Stone of the Kaaba

*Vault entry V26 · Relics & Sacred Objects · Set in the eastern corner of the Kaaba, the Sacred Mosque, Mecca · Recently added — pending full review.*

Pilgrims circling the **Kaaba** during the Hajj pass one corner again and again. Set into the wall there, about a metre and a half above the ground and held in a **silver frame**, are the dark, polished fragments of a stone: the **Black Stone** (*al-Ḥajar al-Aswad*). Many pilgrims try to **kiss or touch it** as they pass, as the Prophet Muhammad is reported to have done. When the crowds make that impossible, as they usually do, pilgrims **raise a hand towards it** from a distance at the start of each circuit.

This entry describes the stone **with the restraint its sacredness asks for**. It is a living object of worship for over a billion people, and it has **never been scientifically examined**.

## In Islamic tradition

- **Origin.** Hadith and tradition describe the stone as **coming from Paradise**, originally white and **blackened by human sins**, and as set in the Kaaba by **Abraham and Ishmael**. **These are articles of faith, not historical claims.**
- **Muhammad and the stone.** A well-known account places the Prophet at the **rebuilding of the Kaaba around 605 CE**, before his prophetic mission. The tribal clans quarrelled over who should have the honour of setting the stone back in place. Muhammad resolved it by placing the stone on a **cloak** and having a representative of each clan **lift a corner**, then set it in place with his own hands.
- **Not an object of worship.** Islamic teaching insists that the stone is **not worshipped**, and that the Kaaba and the stone are a **focus**, not a god. The caliph **ʿUmar** is reported (in Sahih al-Bukhari) to have said to it:

> I know that you are a stone that can neither harm nor benefit. Had I not seen the Messenger of God kiss you, I would not have kissed you.

## The documented history

- **930 CE.** The **Qarmatians**, a radical Ismaili movement based in eastern Arabia, **sacked Mecca** during the pilgrimage season, killed pilgrims, and **carried off the Black Stone** to their capital.
- **952 CE.** The stone was **returned**, apparently in fragments, after more than twenty years. It had been **broken**, and later damage broke it further.
- The fragments are **held together** in a **silver casing**, which has been renewed several times over the centuries.

## What it is made of

**No one outside the tradition knows, and no sample has ever been analysed.** Proposals include:
- a **stony meteorite**, a popular idea because of the heavenly origin in tradition;
- an **impactite**, glass formed by a meteorite striking sand. Some point to the **Wabar craters** in the Arabian desert;
- **basalt**, **agate** or another terrestrial rock.

Reported physical descriptions, for example claims that a fragment **floated** in water when it was recovered, are **themselves traditions** and cannot be tested. **The question is open and likely to remain so.**

## Symbology

**The hand of God.** A tradition describes the stone as God's **"right hand on earth"**, so that touching it is like pledging allegiance. This is **metaphor within the tradition**. It gives the gesture of **touching or saluting** the stone the meaning of **covenant**.

**Continuity with Abraham.** Islam reads the Kaaba and its stone as restoring the **monotheism of Abraham**. The rites of the Hajj retrace events from the lives of **Abraham, Hagar and Ishmael**. The stone is part of a **sacred geography** that links Islam to the prophets before it (ch21).

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- The Black Stone is set in the **eastern corner of the Kaaba** in a **silver frame**, in **fragments**. It was **taken by the Qarmatians in 930** and **returned in 952**. It is **central to the rites of the Hajj** and was venerated by the Prophet according to hadith.

**What is not supported**

- Any **scientific identification** of its material. **No analysis has been made.**

**What's genuinely open**

- **What the stone is**: meteorite, impactite or terrestrial rock.
- Its **history before Islam**, beyond the traditions.
