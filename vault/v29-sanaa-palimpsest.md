# The Sana'a Palimpsest: The Qur'an Beneath the Qur'an

*Vault entry V29 · Manuscripts · Dar al-Makhtutat, Sana'a (DAM 01-27.1) · Recently added — pending full review.*

In **1972** heavy rain brought down part of the **Great Mosque of Sana'a**, one of the oldest mosques in Yemen. Restorers working between the ceiling and the roof found a **mass of old parchment and paper**: thousands of fragments of worn-out Qur'an copies. They had been put there because a damaged copy of the Qur'an is not thrown away. From the 1980s a Yemeni–German project led by scholars including **Gerd-R. Puin** sorted and photographed the fragments. In **1981** one group of leaves was recognised as a **palimpsest**, a manuscript written over an earlier text that had been scraped or washed away. Under a Qur'an there was **another Qur'an**.

## Two texts on one parchment

- **Ṣanʿāʾ 1**, as scholars call it, has about **80 leaves** in Sana'a, plus **four leaves** that left Yemen and were sold at auction in the West.
- **The upper text** is a Qur'an that follows the **standard ʿUthmānic text** and the standard **order of suras**.
- **The lower text**, the one erased, is also a Qur'an. It is **not** the standard text. Its wording often differs in small ways: different words, word order, extra or missing phrases. Its **suras run in an order found in no known tradition**, although the suras themselves are recognisably the same ones.
- A **radiocarbon** test on one of the detached leaves, published by **Behnam Sadeghi and Uwe Bergmann** in 2010, dated its parchment, and so its lower text, to **578–669 CE** at 95% probability. They reported a high probability of a date before about 646.

## What scholars make of it

In 2012 **Sadeghi and Mohsen Goudarzi** published an edition of much of the lower text. They called it "at present the most important document for the history of the Qur'an" and argued that it is **the only surviving copy of a textual tradition other than the ʿUthmānic**. They linked it to the kind of **Companion codices** that Islamic sources say existed before ʿUthmān standardised the text, such as those attributed to Ibn Masʿūd and Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, while noting that it matches neither exactly.

**Asma Hilali** (2017, Oxford University Press / Institute of Ismaili Studies) published a new edition of both layers. She stressed signs that the lower text may have been a **working or teaching copy** rather than a finished codex. **Éléonore Cellard** (2021) studied how the leaves were made and assembled, and argued that the lower text was once part of a **complete Qur'an codex**. These readings are argued in the specialist literature and not settled.

## Why erase a Qur'an?

Parchment was expensive, and reusing it was common in the ancient and medieval world. Christian scribes did the same: St Catherine's Monastery, the home of the Codex Sinaiticus (V18), also yielded the famous Syriac Sinaiticus, a Gospel text under a later one. Scribes may have erased the lower text because it **no longer matched the standard text** that had become authoritative. They may simply have needed the parchment. The manuscript itself does not tell us which.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**Layers as history.** A palimpsest is a physical record of change: one text, then another. Here the change runs **from a non-standard to the standard text**, the direction Islamic tradition describes when it tells of ʿUthmān's recension and the retirement of other copies.

**The paper grave.** Worn copies of the Qur'an are not discarded. They are buried, placed in a *genizah*-like store, or, as at Sana'a, set aside inside a mosque. The Jewish practice of the Cairo Genizah preserved texts in a similar way.

**Reading instructions.** Imaging has revealed small marks in the lower text that may be **early reading or recitation instructions**. They point to the Qur'an as a text that is **recited**, of which the written copy is only one part.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A **palimpsest Qur'an** from the **1972** Sana'a find, with an **upper standard text** and an **erased lower text** that differs in **readings and sura order**.
- **Radiocarbon** on a detached leaf dates its parchment to **578–669 CE** (95%).
- The lower text is the **only known manuscript witness to a non-ʿUthmānic Qur'an text** in substantial extent.

**What is not supported**

- Claims that the palimpsest shows the Qur'an to be **fundamentally different** from the text Muslims read. The lower text's differences are mostly **small variants** within recognisably the same suras.
- Claims that it proves **no variation ever existed**. The lower text is itself evidence of early variation.

**What's genuinely open**

- **What the lower text was**: a Companion-type codex, a local tradition, or a teaching copy.
- **Why** it was erased, and exactly **when** each layer was written.
