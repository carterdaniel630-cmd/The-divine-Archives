# The Copper Scroll: A Treasure List Hammered into Metal

*Vault entry V10 · Texts & Tablets · The Jordan Museum, Amman · Recently added — pending full review.*

Most of the Dead Sea Scrolls (V02) are skins or papyri. In **1952**, archaeologists in **Qumran Cave 3** found two rolls of something else: **copper**, corroded so badly that they could not be unrolled. They had once been a single sheet about **2.4 metres long**, inscribed by punching letters into the metal. For three years nobody could read them. In **1955–56**, at the **Manchester College of Science and Technology**, the engineer **Henry Wright Baker** built a device to cut the rolls into **23 curved strips**. The philologist **John Allegro** was involved in the project. When the strips were read, the text turned out to be unlike anything else from Qumran. It was a **plain list of places where treasure was hidden**.

## The object and its text

- Catalogued as **3Q15**, the fifteenth item from Cave 3. It is now in the **Jordan Museum, Amman**.
- The text lists about **64 locations**: in ruins, cisterns, tombs, under steps and beside aqueducts. Each gives a depth in cubits and a quantity of **silver, gold, vessels or scrolls**. The totals run into **many tonnes** of precious metal.
- The **Hebrew** is closer to the later **Mishnaic** language than to the literary Hebrew of most scrolls. The engraving is inexpert, and the text has **spelling oddities** that complicate reading.
- **Seven** of the entries are followed by **groups of two or three Greek letters**, recorded in the editions as **ΚΕΝ, ΧΑΓ, ΗΝ, ΘΕ, ΔΙ, ΤΡ, ΣΚ**.

The first entry, in a working rendering after the published translations:

> In the ruin in the Valley of Achor, under the steps that go eastward, forty cubits: a chest of silver and its vessels, a weight of seventeen talents.

## What was it?

Every explanation has serious defenders and serious problems:
- **Real treasure of the Jerusalem Temple**, hidden before the Roman siege of **70 CE**. That would explain the quantities, but the amounts are **enormous**.
- **Treasure connected with the Bar Kokhba revolt** (132–135 CE), if the scroll is later than the other Cave 3 finds.
- **A folkloric or literary list**, along the lines of legends about hidden Temple vessels. The deadpan administrative style makes this seem less likely to many readers.
- **Not connected with the Qumran community at all.** Its language and material stand apart from the other scrolls.

**No treasure from the list has ever been found.** Many place-names cannot be identified with confidence, and searches since the 1960s, including Allegro's own expedition, found nothing.

## Symbology and encoding

**The Greek letter-groups.** Seven entries are tagged with Greek letters in an otherwise Hebrew text. They have been read as **abbreviations of names**, perhaps of the people responsible for each cache (for example ΔΙ for a name like *Didymos*), as **numerals**, or as **cues** for a companion document. The scroll itself refers to **"another writing"** giving further details. One popular book claimed the letters spell out the name of the Egyptian pharaoh **Akhenaten**. **That reading has no scholarly support.** The letter-groups are **real and deliberate**. **Their meaning is unknown.**

**Metal as permanence.** Choosing **copper** over leather for this one document looks like a deliberate choice of **durability**. The list was meant to outlast the people who made it.

## Connections

The Dead Sea Scrolls (V02), Second Temple Judaism (ch10), the Ark of the Covenant (V07), whose later traditions also imagine hidden Temple treasures.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A copper scroll found in **Qumran Cave 3 (1952)**, cut open at Manchester in **1955–56**, listing about **64 hiding places** of silver, gold and objects in Mishnaic-style Hebrew. **Seven Greek letter-groups** tag certain entries.

**What is not supported**

- That any of the treasure has been **located**. Claims that the Greek letters encode **Akhenaten** or other hidden messages are also unsupported.

**What's genuinely open**

- Whether the treasure was **real**, whose it was (the **Temple's**, the **Bar Kokhba rebels'**, or someone else's), and **when** the scroll was made.
- What the **Greek letter-groups** mean.
