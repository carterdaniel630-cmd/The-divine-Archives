# The Ark of the Covenant: The Object Described in Every Detail and Found Nowhere

*Vault entry V07 · Relics & Sacred Objects · No surviving object is verified; the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church holds that it is kept at Aksum · Recently added — pending full review.*

It may be the most precisely described object in the Bible. **Exodus 25** gives its measurements to the half-cubit, its wood, its gold, its rings and poles, and the two golden **cherubim** facing each other across its lid with their wings spread. Priests carried it into the Jordan and around Jericho. Philistines captured it, and it was brought back. David danced before it. Solomon installed it in the innermost room of the Temple. Then, somewhere between Solomon and the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem in **586 BCE**, it **drops out of the record**. When the Roman general Pompey forced his way into the Holy of Holies of the rebuilt Temple in 63 BCE, the Roman historian **Tacitus** says he found **the shrine empty**. **Josephus** describes the innermost room of that Temple as holding **nothing at all**.

This entry sets out what the texts say the Ark was and did, what the traditions say became of it, and what archaeology can and cannot say. It includes the claim, held with great seriousness by the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, that it rests today in a chapel in **Aksum**.

## The text: how it was to be made

**Exodus 25:10**, in Hebrew:

> **ועשו ארון עצי שטים אמתים וחצי ארכו ואמה וחצי רחבו ואמה וחצי קמתו**
> They shall make an ark of acacia wood: two cubits and a half its length, a cubit and a half its width, and a cubit and a half its height.

The rest of **Exodus 25:10–22** specifies:
- the box is to be **overlaid with pure gold inside and out**, with a gold moulding;
- **four gold rings** hold **two acacia poles overlaid with gold**, and **the poles are never to be removed** (25:15);
- a lid of **pure gold**, the ***kapporet*** (traditionally "mercy seat");
- **two cherubim** of hammered gold at its ends, facing each other, their wings overshadowing the lid;
- the **"testimony"** is to be placed inside, meaning the tablets of the covenant.

God says: "**There I will meet with you, and from above the kapporet, from between the two cherubim**, I will speak with you" (25:22).

**Its dimensions in modern terms.** A cubit was a forearm's length, and ancient cubits varied. Using a **common cubit of about 44.5 cm**, the Ark measures roughly **111 × 67 × 67 cm**. Using a **longer "royal" cubit of about 52.5 cm**, it measures roughly **131 × 79 × 79 cm**. Either way it is a **chest about the size of a large trunk**, not a monumental object. The exact cubit intended is **uncertain**.

## What it contained

The texts do not fully agree:
- **Deuteronomy 10** and **1 Kings 8:9**: "There was **nothing in the ark except the two tablets of stone**."
- **Hebrews 9:4**, a first-century Christian text: the Ark held the tablets, **a golden jar of manna** and **Aaron's rod that budded**. This draws on traditions in Exodus 16 and Numbers 17, where those items are placed *before* the testimony rather than inside it.

## What it did, in the narrative

- It **led the march** through the wilderness and **stopped the Jordan** so Israel could cross (Joshua 3). It was carried **around Jericho** (Joshua 6).
- It stood at the sanctuary at **Shiloh**. It was **captured by the Philistines** at the battle of Ebenezer (1 Samuel 4). In their temple the image of **Dagon fell before it**, and plague followed it from city to city until the Philistines **sent it back on a cart** (1 Samuel 5–6).
- It rested for years at **Kiriath-Jearim** (1 Samuel 7). **David** brought it to Jerusalem. When **Uzzah** touched it to steady it he was struck dead, and David **danced** before it (2 Samuel 6).
- **Solomon** installed it in the **Holy of Holies** of the Temple (1 Kings 8).
- **Last mentions:** **2 Chronicles 35:3** (under Josiah, late seventh century BCE). **Jeremiah 3:16** looks to a time when "they shall no longer say 'the ark of the covenant of the LORD'… it shall not come to mind… **nor shall another be made**". That passage has been read as evidence that it was already gone.

## Where did it go? The traditions

- **Taken or destroyed by Babylon (586 BCE).** The list of Temple spoils in **2 Kings 25** does **not mention** the Ark. Some scholars take that silence to mean it had already vanished; others think it was simply destroyed.
- **Hidden by Jeremiah on Mount Nebo.** **2 Maccabees 2:4–8** (second or first century BCE) says the prophet **Jeremiah** hid the tent, the Ark and the incense altar in a **cave on Mount Nebo**, to stay hidden "until God gathers his people together again".
- **Hidden beneath the Temple.** Rabbinic sources, including the **Talmud (Yoma 53b–54a)**, record a debate. One opinion holds that the Ark was **hidden in its place**, beneath the Temple Mount, and another that it was **taken to Babylon**.
- **Taken to Ethiopia.** The ***Kebra Nagast*** ("Glory of the Kings") is an Ethiopian work compiled in its present form in the **fourteenth century**. It tells how **Menelik I**, son of **Solomon and the Queen of Sheba**, brought the Ark to Ethiopia. The **Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church** holds that it is kept in a chapel beside the **Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion in Aksum**. A single **guardian monk** keeps it and does not leave the chapel precinct. **No outsider is permitted to see it**, so **the claim cannot be examined**.
- **Modern "discoveries".** Various individuals have claimed to have found the Ark, most famously under Golgotha in Jerusalem. **None has produced verifiable evidence.**

## What archaeology can and cannot say

**No object has ever been found that can be identified as the Ark.** Archaeology contributes context instead:
- **Portable shrines carried on poles** are well attested in the ancient Near East and Egypt. The best-known example is the **Anubis shrine from Tutankhamun's tomb**, a chest on carrying poles. The comparison shows the Ark's **type** was real in that world. It does not show that the Ark existed.
- At **Kiriath-Jearim**, where 1 Samuel says the Ark rested, excavations from **2017** led by **Israel Finkelstein and Thomas Römer** uncovered a large **eighth-century BCE platform**. They proposed that it was a **northern Israelite cult centre** connected with the Ark traditions. Their interpretation, including the idea that the Ark story reflects later political history, is **debated**.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**A throne, not a box.** In the Hebrew Bible God is "**enthroned upon the cherubim**" (1 Samuel 4:4; 2 Samuel 6:2). The Ark with its winged lid is best understood as God's **throne or footstool**: an **empty seat** of an invisible king. That fits the prohibition on images. The holiest object in Israel's sanctuary was a **place where God was not depicted**.

**The kapporet and atonement.** The lid's name, ***kapporet***, shares its root with ***kipper***, "to atone". On the **Day of Atonement** the high priest sprinkled blood **on and before the kapporet** (Leviticus 16). The object's name encodes its ritual function, and the English "mercy seat" (from Tyndale's translation) tries to capture that.

**Gradients of holiness.** Gold covering, then gold alone, then the cherubim: the Ark sits at the centre of a **graded architecture of holiness**, from the outer court to the Holy of Holies. The tabernacle and Temple are laid out as a **theological map**.

**The tabot.** Every Ethiopian Orthodox church is consecrated with a **tabot**, a tablet representing the tablets of the covenant, kept in the sanctuary and wrapped in cloth when carried in procession. At the feast of **Timkat** (Epiphany), tabotat are carried through the streets. **The church is not holy without it.** This is a living **ritual encoding** of the Ark.

**Christian typology.** Early Christian writers read the **Virgin Mary as the new Ark**, the vessel that carried God's presence. They noticed echoes between **Luke 1** (Mary's visit to Elizabeth) and **2 Samuel 6** (the Ark's journey to Jerusalem). **Revelation 11:19** shows the Ark in heaven. These are **documented interpretations of the texts**. They are not historical claims about the object.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- The Ark is **described in detail** in Exodus 25 and appears throughout Joshua, Samuel and Kings. Its **type** of object, a portable shrine carried on poles, is **well attested** in the ancient Near East and Egypt.
- **The Ark disappears from the textual record** before or at the Babylonian destruction of 586 BCE. **Josephus** and **Tacitus** both report the Second Temple's Holy of Holies as **empty**.
- The **Ethiopian Orthodox Church's claim** to hold the Ark at Aksum is **documented as a tradition**, grounded in the fourteenth-century *Kebra Nagast* and expressed in the **tabot** in every church.

**What is not supported**

- That the Ark has been **found**. **No verifiable evidence** supports any modern discovery claim.
- That the Aksum object **is** the Ark. It **cannot be examined**, so the claim can be neither confirmed nor refuted.

**What's genuinely open**

- Whether the Ark existed **as described**, and **when** the traditions about it took shape. The Kiriath-Jearim interpretation is one debated reconstruction.
- **What happened to it**: destroyed, taken, hidden, or never the single object the later texts describe.
