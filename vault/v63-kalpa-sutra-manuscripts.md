# The Kalpa Sutra Manuscripts: Fourteen Dreams in Gold

*Vault entry V63 · Manuscripts · Jain temple libraries (bhandars) in India; museums worldwide · Recently added — pending full review.*

Each year at the end of summer, during the eight-day festival of **Paryushana**, Shvetambara Jain monks in western India read aloud from a book that the community holds among its most sacred: the **Kalpa Sutra**. On the fifth day the reading reaches the **birth of Mahavira**, the twenty-fourth Jina. Before his birth his mother, **Queen Trishala**, dreamed **fourteen dreams**, and in temples silver replicas of the dreams are shown to the congregation and families make offerings to honour them. For centuries the book was copied by hand and **illustrated**, in brilliant **red, blue and gold**, and gifting a copy to a temple library was an act of religious merit.

## The text

- Tradition attributes it to the teacher **Bhadrabahu**, traditionally of the fourth century BCE; the text was fixed, with the rest of the Shvetambara canon, at the **Council of Valabhi** in the fifth century CE.
- Three parts: the **lives of the Jinas** (above all **Mahavira**, then **Parshva**, **Nemi** and **Rishabha**); a list of the **elders** who handed on the teaching; and **rules for monks** during the rainy-season retreat.
- The Kalpa Sutra is a **Shvetambara** text. The **Digambara** Jains do not accept it, and their tradition counts **sixteen** dreams, not fourteen.

## The manuscripts

- The oldest copies are on **palm leaf**. From the late **fourteenth century** Jain scribes in **Gujarat** and **Rajasthan** began to use **paper**, and the number of illustrated copies grew rapidly.
- The pages are long and narrow, imitating palm leaves, often with a painted dot or hole where a string once passed through.
- The paintings are in the **Western Indian style**: flat red grounds, figures in profile with a **projecting far eye**, and lavish use of **gold** and **ultramarine**. Wealthy merchants commissioned them.
- Thousands survive in the temple libraries, the ***bhandars***, and in museums worldwide.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**The fourteen dreams.** An **elephant**, a **bull**, a **lion**, the goddess **Shri**, a **garland**, the **moon**, the **sun**, a **banner**, a **vase**, a **lotus lake**, the **ocean**, a **celestial palace**, a **heap of jewels** and a **smokeless fire**. Interpreters told the king that they announced the birth of a universal ruler or a Jina. The interactive cards above show all fourteen.

**The transfer of the embryo.** In the Kalpa Sutra, Mahavira was first conceived by a **Brahmin** woman, **Devananda**, and the god **Harinaigamesin** moved the embryo to the womb of the **Kshatriya** queen Trishala. The episode is often painted. The Digambaras reject it.

**Books as objects of worship.** Jains honour **knowledge** as sacred: on the festival of **Jnana Panchami** manuscripts are cleaned and venerated. Copying and donating books earned merit, which is why so many were made and preserved.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A **Shvetambara** Jain scripture, read at **Paryushana**, containing the **lives of the Jinas** and the **fourteen dreams** of Trishala.
- A large tradition of **illustrated paper manuscripts** from western India, mostly from the **fourteenth to sixteenth centuries**.

**What is not supported**

- That the text in its present form goes back unchanged to **Bhadrabahu**. It was transmitted orally and fixed centuries later.

**What's genuinely open**

- How much of the text is **early** and how much was added when the canon was fixed.
- The dates of the earliest illustrated copies, since many are **undated**.
