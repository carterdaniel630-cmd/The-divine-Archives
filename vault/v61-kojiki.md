# The Kojiki: The Shinpukuji Manuscript

*Vault entry V61 · Manuscripts · Ōsu Kannon (Shinpukuji), Nagoya, Japan · Recently added — pending full review.*

In **1371 and 1372**, a monk named **Ken'yu** at the Buddhist temple of **Shinpukuji** in Owari province copied out, in three volumes, a Japanese book that was already more than six hundred years old. It began: *"At the time when heaven and earth first opened..."* His copy is the **oldest surviving manuscript** of the **Kojiki**, the "Record of Ancient Matters", Japan's oldest surviving book. It is kept today at the temple of **Ōsu Kannon** in Nagoya and is a **National Treasure** of Japan.

## The book

- According to its preface, the Emperor **Tenmu** (r. 673–686) ordered the old genealogies and legends to be corrected. A court attendant, **Hieda no Are**, memorised them.
- Years later the courtier **Ō no Yasumaro** wrote down what Hieda no Are recited, and presented the book to **Empress Genmei** in **712**.
- Three volumes: the **Age of the Gods**; the legendary early emperors; and the emperors down to **Empress Suiko** (d. 628).
- Written in **Chinese characters**, some used for their meaning in Chinese style, some for their **sound** to spell Japanese words, names and songs.
- Eight years later, in **720**, the court produced a second, longer history in formal Chinese, the ***Nihon Shoki***, which tells many of the same myths in variant forms.

## The myths

- The first deities appear alone in the **Plain of High Heaven** and hide themselves.
- The divine pair **Izanagi and Izanami** stir the ocean with a jewelled spear and create the islands of Japan and many gods. Izanami dies giving birth to fire; Izanagi follows her to the land of the dead, **Yomi**, and flees in horror.
- Washing himself, Izanagi gives birth to the sun goddess **Amaterasu**, the moon god **Tsukuyomi** and the storm god **Susanoo**. Offended by Susanoo, Amaterasu hides in a **cave** and the world goes dark until the gods lure her out.
- Amaterasu's grandson **Ninigi** descends to earth, and his great-grandson becomes **Jimmu**, the first emperor.

## A book with a later life

For centuries the Kojiki was less read than the Nihon Shoki. The scholar **Motoori Norinaga** devoted over thirty years to its study, completing his ***Kojiki-den*** in **1798**; he treated it as the pure record of ancient Japan. From **1868** the Meiji state used its account of the **divine descent of the emperors** in **State Shinto**. After **1945** the emperor renounced claims to divinity, and scholars read the Kojiki as a political and literary work of the early court, as well as a treasury of myth.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**Writing sounds with meanings.** The Kojiki's mixed script, using Chinese characters for sound (the system later called *man'yōgana*), is the root of Japanese writing. The names of the gods are often long compounds whose parts carry meaning, such as *Ame-no-minakanushi*, "Lord of the Centre of Heaven".

**The imperial regalia.** The myths explain the **three sacred treasures** of the emperor: the **mirror** used to lure Amaterasu from the cave, the **jewels**, and the **sword** Susanoo took from the tail of an eight-headed serpent. They are still kept at shrines and in the palace and are **not shown** to the public.

**Purity and pollution.** Izanagi's washing after returning from Yomi is the mythic model of **purification** (*misogi*), central to Shinto ritual.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A book presented at court in **712** by **Ō no Yasumaro**, surviving in manuscripts of which the oldest is the **Shinpukuji copy of 1371–72**.
- It records the **myths of the gods** and the **descent of the imperial line** as the court of the eighth century told them.

**What is not supported**

- That the early "emperors" of the later volumes, such as **Jimmu** (traditionally 660 BCE), are **historical** as described. Historians regard the early reigns as legendary.

**What's genuinely open**

- How much of the myth is **old oral tradition** and how much was **shaped by the court** to support its rule.
- A minority of scholars has argued that the **preface**, or even the whole book, is a later **forgery**. Most reject this, but the debate has not fully closed.
