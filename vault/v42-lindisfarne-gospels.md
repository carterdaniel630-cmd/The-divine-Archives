# The Lindisfarne Gospels: A Book for a Saint

*Vault entry V42 · Manuscripts · British Library, London (Cotton MS Nero D IV) · Recently added — pending full review.*

In **687** **Cuthbert**, monk, hermit and bishop, died on the rocky islet of Inner Farne. He was buried at the monastery of **Lindisfarne**, the Holy Island off the Northumbrian coast. In **698** his coffin was opened and his body was found, it was said, **incorrupt**, and his cult took hold. Around the same years, a monk of Lindisfarne began one of the most ambitious books ever made in early medieval Europe: the **four Gospels**, written and painted in honour of God and **St Cuthbert**. Two hundred and fifty years later another member of the community wrote in the back of the book who had made it.

## The book

- **259 leaves** of vellum, made around **715–720**, containing the four Gospels in **Latin** (Jerome's **Vulgate**), with prefaces and **canon tables**, the concordance of parallel passages.
- Each Gospel opens with an **evangelist portrait**, a **carpet page** of pure ornament built on a cross, and a great **decorated initial page**. Matthew's account of Christ's birth has a **Chi-Rho** page.
- It was probably written and painted by **one person**, a feat of concentration that took years. That person is traditionally **Eadfrith**, bishop of Lindisfarne (698–721).
- Its pigments came from local minerals and plants, with some that may have been traded from afar. Modern analysis has argued that fewer exotic imports were used than once thought.

## Aldred's colophon and gloss

In the **mid-tenth century** the community, driven from Lindisfarne by Viking raids, was settled at **Chester-le-Street**, carrying Cuthbert's body and the book. There a priest named **Aldred** did two things:

1. He wrote an **interlinear gloss**: an **Old English** translation of the Latin, word by word, in small letters between the lines. It is the **oldest surviving English version of the Gospels**, and a major source for the **Northumbrian dialect** of Old English.
2. He added a **colophon** naming the book's makers: **Eadfrith** wrote it, **Æthelwald** bound it, **Billfrith** the anchorite made its jewelled cover, and Aldred glossed it.

Aldred wrote about 250 years after the book was made, and scholars take his colophon seriously but not as certain. It may record community tradition rather than documents.

## Journey and return

The book travelled with Cuthbert's relics to **Chester-le-Street** and then to **Durham**. It was removed from Durham at the **dissolution of the monasteries** in the sixteenth century, its jewelled cover lost, and entered the collection of **Sir Robert Cotton**, which passed to the nation and then to the **British Library**. In the north-east of England there have been repeated calls for its **return**, and it has gone back to Durham and Newcastle for exhibitions.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**The carpet page as cross.** Each carpet page hides a **cross** within dense interlace. The eye has to **find** the cross in the knotwork, an act often compared to prayer or meditation.

**The evangelists' symbols.** A man (angel) for **Matthew**, a lion for **Mark**, an ox (calf) for **Luke**, an eagle for **John**, from Ezekiel and Revelation. Matthew's portrait is labelled in Greek letters, *O AGIOS MATHEUS*, "Saint Matthew", a mark of learned devotion.

**The book as relic.** Made for Cuthbert and kept with his body, the Gospels were a **contact relic** and a treasure of the saint's shrine as well as a text.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A Northumbrian **Gospel book** of c. **715–720**, in the **British Library**, with an **Old English interlinear gloss** by **Aldred** (mid-tenth century), the oldest surviving English Gospels.
- Aldred's **colophon** names **Eadfrith**, **Æthelwald**, **Billfrith** and himself.

**What is not supported**

- That its decoration encodes **secret doctrine**. Its symbols are those of mainstream early medieval Christianity.

**What's genuinely open**

- **Exactly when** it was made and whether **Eadfrith** was its single artist-scribe, as the colophon indicates.
- The **source** of some pigments.
