# The Staff God of Tiwanaku: The Gateway of the Sun

*Vault entry V65 · Relics & Objects · In place: Tiwanaku, Bolivia*

At almost **3,850 metres** above sea level, on the high plain near the southern shore of **Lake Titicaca** in Bolivia, a single block of **andesite** stands in the ruins of **Tiwanaku**. It is a gateway about **3 metres** high and **4 metres** wide, cut from one stone, cracked through the top. Above the doorway, in the centre of a carved frieze, a figure faces the viewer. It stands on a stepped platform and holds a **staff** in each hand, and its head is ringed with **rays** ending in animal heads. On both sides, rows of smaller **winged figures** turn toward it. The Spanish and later travellers called it the **Puerta del Sol**, the **Gateway of the Sun**. Its builders' name for it, and for the figure, is lost.

## Tiwanaku

- A great city and ceremonial centre that flourished from about **500 to 1000 CE**, with monumental platforms and sunken courts, and influence across the southern Andes.
- The gateway stands in the **Kalasasaya**, a large walled platform, but it was found **broken** and is probably **not in its original position**. It was later re-erected.
- Tiwanaku declined around **1000–1100 CE**, perhaps with drought. By the time of the **Inca**, centuries later, it was already an ancient ruin, and the Inca treated it as a sacred place of origin.
- It became a **UNESCO World Heritage Site** in **2000**.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**The Staff God.** A frontal figure holding two staffs appears across Andean art for **two thousand years**: on the **Raimondi Stela** of **Chavín de Huántar** in Peru, many centuries earlier; at Tiwanaku; and in **Wari** art. Scholars call this the **Staff God** or "Staff Deity". It seems to have been a major deity or a model of divine power, but its names and myths in each culture are **unknown**.

**Viracocha.** The figure is popularly called **Viracocha**, the creator of Inca religion, whose myths say he rose from **Lake Titicaca** and created the sun and the peoples of the world at Tiwanaku. The link is **attractive but late**: the Inca myths were recorded by the Spanish, a thousand years after the gateway was carved.

**The rays and attendants.** The head rays, the condor and puma heads, and the **48 winged attendants** (in three rows of eight on each side) form a symmetrical composition. Its meaning is **uncertain**.

**The "calendar".** The engineer **Arthur Posnansky**, in the early twentieth century, read the frieze as a **calendar**, and claimed that Tiwanaku was built more than **15,000** years ago on astronomical grounds. Archaeological dating shows the city is of the first millennium CE, and his calendar reading is **not accepted**.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A monolithic **gateway** at **Tiwanaku**, carved with a frontal **Staff God** and **winged attendants**, from the city's florescence c. **500–1000 CE**.
- The Staff God as a long-lived **Andean** motif, from Chavín to Wari and Tiwanaku.

**What is not supported**

- That Tiwanaku is **tens of thousands of years old**, or that the frieze is a proven **calendar**.
- That the figure is **Viracocha** as the Inca understood him. That identification is later.

**What's genuinely open**

- **Who** the Staff God was to the people of Tiwanaku, and what the frieze meant.
- The gateway's **original location** and exact date.
