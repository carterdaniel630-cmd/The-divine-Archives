# The Skull of "Mary Magdalene" at Saint-Maximin: A Relic in a Golden Reliquary

*Vault entry V88 · Relics & Sacred Objects · Crypt of the Basilica of Saint-Maximin-la-Sainte-Baume, Provence, France*

In the crypt of the great Gothic basilica of **Saint-Maximin-la-Sainte-Baume**, behind protective glass, a **darkened human skull** sits inside a **gilded reliquary**. The metalwork frames the skull with **flowing golden hair** and is carried by **four golden angels**. Roman numerals on the back read **MDCCCLX**, which points to **1860** as the reliquary's date. Tradition holds that the skull is that of **Mary Magdalene**, the follower of Jesus whom the Gospels name as the first witness of the empty tomb. Each year, on or around her feast day of **22 July**, it is carried in procession through the town.

## How a Gospel figure came to rest in Provence

- **The Gospels say nothing of her later life or her burial.** Early traditions disagree. **Gregory of Tours** (6th century) places her at **Ephesus**. **Modestus**, patriarch of Jerusalem in the 7th century, says she returned to Jerusalem.
- **The Provençal story is medieval.** In it, Mary Magdalene, Lazarus, Martha, Maximinus and others arrive by boat at Marseilles and convert Provence, and Mary spends thirty years in penance in a cave at **La Sainte-Baume**. The *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1910), summarizing the historian **Louis Duchesne**, states that **before the middle of the eleventh century there is no trace** of the tradition that these Palestinian saints came to Provence. It says the story first appears in documents connected with **Vézelay**, the Burgundian abbey that then claimed her body.
- **Vézelay first, then Saint-Maximin.** Vézelay was the great Magdalene shrine of the eleventh and twelfth centuries. In **1265** relics were exhumed there, and Louis IX attended their elevation in **1267**. Then, in **1279**, **Charles of Salerno**, later Charles II of Anjou, count of Provence, had the crypt at Saint-Maximin excavated and announced the discovery of **her tomb and body**. He founded a **Dominican** convent there. In **1295** **Pope Boniface VIII** recognized the Saint-Maximin relics. Vézelay's pilgrimage declined. The rival claims have never been reconciled.
- **The Golden Legend.** The thirteenth-century *Golden Legend* of Jacobus de Voragine spread the Provençal story across Europe.

## The relics since

- **The Revolution.** In **1794**, during the French Revolution, the relics were despoiled. According to the account preserved by the shrine and reported by EWTN, the sacristan **Joseph Bastide** saved the **skull**, and other people saved other fragments.
- **The "noli me tangere."** For centuries a patch of tissue on the forehead was venerated as the place the risen Christ touched her when he said "Do not hold on to me" (*noli me tangere*, John 20:17). It stayed on the skull until the eighteenth century. In **1780** it was placed in a **glass reliquary** of its own.
- **The 1860 reliquary** is the one seen today.

## What science has, and has not, said

- In **2017** the forensic anthropologist **Philippe Charlier** and the forensic artist **Philippe Froesch** led a study of the skull and a lock of hair. The shrine did not allow the skull to be taken out of its reliquary. The team made a photogrammetric survey of **several hundred photographs** and examined the hair under magnification, by scanning electron microscope and by X-ray elemental analysis.
- They proposed a **computer-aided facial reconstruction** of a woman of about **fifty**, of Mediterranean appearance, with **dark brown hair**. They published the work in *Clinical Medicine Insights: Ear, Nose and Throat* in **2019**.
- **None of this identifies the person.** The identification with Mary Magdalene **rests on tradition**. The reports on the study themselves state that it is not conclusive historical or forensic evidence.

## Symbology

**The golden head.** Head reliquaries, which enclose a skull in precious metal shaped as a head or bust, were a common medieval form. Here the **gold hair** recalls the **long hair** that became Mary Magdalene's attribute in Western art. That attribute comes from a merger. In a homily of **591**, **Pope Gregory the Great** identified her with the unnamed sinful woman of Luke 7, who dried Jesus's feet with her hair, and with Mary of Bethany. **The Eastern churches never made that merger**, and the reformed Roman calendar of **1969** separated the figures again in the liturgy.

**Touch.** The "noli me tangere" relic turns a Gospel sentence about **not touching** into an object pilgrims could see. A text became a thing.

## In the museum

The museum shows a **modelled rendition** of the reliquary in a niche of its own. It follows published descriptions and a photograph of the reliquary in the crypt, supplied by the site's editor:
- a gilded **plinth** with a rosette frieze and two enamelled shields;
- **four angels** lifting a gilded **bust**, whose hood frames the darkened skull behind a glass window;
- between the angels, a small columned **shrine** holding a glass vial. The published study reports that the reliquary's maker designed a place below the skull for the vial of the *noli me tangere*.

It is **not a replica**. It is not modelled from measurements, and its details are simplified.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A skull has been venerated as Mary Magdalene's at **Saint-Maximin** since the **1279** "discovery" promoted by **Charles of Salerno**. **Boniface VIII** recognized the relics in **1295**. **Vézelay** made the earlier rival claim.
- The present **gilded reliquary** bears the date **MDCCCLX (1860)**.
- The **2017–2019 study** examined the skull only through photographs and the hair by microscopy and elemental analysis. It reconstructed the face of a woman of about fifty.

**What is not supported**

- That the skull is **proven** to be Mary Magdalene's. No test can establish the identity of an individual from the first century.
- That the **Provence voyage** is historical. The *Catholic Encyclopedia* found no trace of it before the eleventh century.

**What's genuinely open**

- **Whose skull it is**, and **how old it is**. No radiocarbon date of the skull has been reported in the sources used here.
- **How the tradition formed** between Vézelay and Saint-Maximin, and what the 1279 excavation actually found.
