# The Crown of Thorns: A Ring of Rushes in Paris

*Vault entry V06 · Relics & Sacred Objects · Notre-Dame de Paris (axial chapel) · Recently added — pending full review.*

On the evening of **15 April 2019**, as the roof of Notre-Dame de Paris burned, the chaplain of the Paris fire brigade, **Father Jean-Marc Fournier**, went into the cathedral with firefighters to bring out its treasures. Among the objects carried to safety was a circlet about the size of a dinner plate, sealed in a ring of crystal and gold. Inside it was **no thorn at all**, only a ring of **bundled rushes bound with gold thread**. It is the object that King **Louis IX** of France bought for a sum close to half his annual revenue, and for which he built one of the most beautiful buildings in Europe. It is venerated as the **Crown of Thorns** placed on Jesus' head before his crucifixion.

This entry follows the crown from the Gospel text to the fire. It separates the **documented chain of ownership**, which runs from the thirteenth century, from the **earlier tradition**, which cannot be traced object by object.

## The text

The crowning with thorns appears in **Mark (15:17)**, **Matthew (27:29)** and **John (19:2)**:

> **Καὶ οἱ στρατιῶται πλέξαντες στέφανον ἐξ ἀκανθῶν ἐπέθηκαν αὐτοῦ τῇ κεφαλῇ, καὶ ἱμάτιον πορφυροῦν περιέβαλον αὐτόν.**
> And the soldiers wove a crown of thorns and put it on his head, and they clothed him in a purple robe.

In the Gospels the crown is **mockery**: a parody of royal insignia placed on a man condemned as "King of the Jews". None of the Gospels records what became of it.

## The documented history

- **Late antiquity and Byzantium.** **Pilgrim accounts** from late antiquity mention a crown of thorns venerated in **Jerusalem**. Later, a crown was kept among the Passion relics of the **Byzantine emperors in Constantinople**. **The continuity between these and the Paris relic cannot be demonstrated object by object.** It is a tradition of succession, not a chain of receipts.
- **1238.** The Latin emperor of Constantinople, **Baldwin II**, was desperate for money. His barons had **pledged the crown to Venetian lenders**. Baldwin turned to **Louis IX**, who paid off the debt. Sources give the sum as **135,000 livres tournois**, close to half the royal budget.
- **1239.** Two **Dominican friars** escorted the relic to France. **Louis received it in person**, by the accounts barefoot and in penitential dress, and brought it to Paris.
- **1242–1248.** Louis built the **Sainte-Chapelle** in his palace on the Île de la Cité as a **walk-in reliquary** for the crown and his other Passion relics. Most of its walls are stained glass.
- **French Revolution.** The Sainte-Chapelle's treasury was dispersed and many reliquaries were melted down. The crown survived and was placed in the **Bibliothèque nationale**. In **1806** it was given to **Notre-Dame**.
- **2019.** It was **rescued from the fire** and kept for safety at the **Louvre**.
- **December 2024.** At the cathedral's reopening it was **returned to Notre-Dame** in a **new reliquary designed by Sylvain Dubuisson**, installed in the **axial chapel** behind the choir. It is shown for veneration on set days.

## The object

What Paris holds is a **circlet of rushes**, a ring of bundled plant stems about **21 cm across**, held together by **gold thread**. It has **no thorns**. Over the centuries individual **thorns were detached** and given as gifts by Byzantine emperors and French kings. That is why **many churches across Europe claim a "Holy Thorn"**. One of the finest reliquaries made for such a thorn, the **Holy Thorn Reliquary** made for the **Duc de Berry** (c. 1390s), is now in the **British Museum**. Some historians have suggested that the rush ring once supported thorny branches woven around or into it. **That reconstruction is a hypothesis.**

The ring has **not been radiocarbon-dated**, and no botanical study establishing its age has been published. Its age and origin before the thirteenth century are **unknown**.

## Symbology and sacred meaning

**A crown that inverts kingship.** In the Gospels the crown is a **parody of royalty**. Christian theology turned the parody around, so that the true king is crowned in suffering. That inversion drives the late-medieval image of Christ as **Man of Sorrows**, crowned with thorns and displaying his wounds.

**The building as reliquary.** The **Sainte-Chapelle** is architecture used as a shrine. Its upper chapel is a cage of stained glass, telling the biblical story from Genesis to the arrival of the relics in Paris. The windows literally write **Louis IX's acquisition into sacred history**. It is one of the clearest documented cases of a building designed as a **theological statement** around a single object.

**Sacred kingship.** For Louis IX, later canonised as **Saint Louis**, owning the crown bound the **French monarchy** to Christ's kingship. The relic and its chapel were political theology in stone, glass and gold.

**The Arma Christi.** In late-medieval devotion the crown joined the **lance, nails, scourge, sponge and dice** as the **"weapons of Christ"** (*Arma Christi*). They were a visual rosary of the Passion, painted in books of hours and carved on church furniture. Each instrument called up one moment of meditation.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- The crowning with thorns is told in **Mark, Matthew and John** as an act of **mockery**.
- **Louis IX** acquired the relic from **Baldwin II's** creditors in **1238** for about **135,000 livres**, received it in **1239**, and built the **Sainte-Chapelle (1242–1248)** to house it. It survived the Revolution, went to **Notre-Dame in 1806**, was **rescued from the 2019 fire**, and was **re-enshrined in December 2024**.
- The object is a **ring of bundled rushes bound with gold thread**, with **no thorns**. Individual thorns were **distributed** as separate relics.

**What is not supported**

- Any **scientific demonstration** that the ring dates to the first century or came from Jerusalem. It has **not been dated**.
- Claims that particular **"Holy Thorns"** across Europe came from the Paris crown, **unless** they have documented gift histories. Many do not.

**What's genuinely open**

- The object's **age and origin** before its Byzantine and Parisian history.
- **How** the rush ring relates to the crown described in the Gospels: whether it was ever a support for thorny branches.
