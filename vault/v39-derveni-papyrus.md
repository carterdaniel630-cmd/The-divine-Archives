# The Derveni Papyrus: Europe's Oldest Book, Saved by Fire

*Vault entry V39 · Manuscripts · Archaeological Museum of Thessaloniki · Recently added — pending full review.*

On **15 January 1962**, road-workers widening the highway from **Thessaloniki** to Kavala uncovered a group of rich **Macedonian cist tombs** at **Derveni**, about 10 km north of the city. On the covering slabs of the tomb labelled **A** lay the remains of a **funeral pyre**, and in the ash a **charred lump of papyrus**. Papyrus does not survive in Greece's damp soil. This roll survived because the fire had turned it to **carbon** without consuming it. It is **the oldest surviving book in Europe**.

## The roll

- Only the **upper part** survives, the rest burned away. Conservators separated the lump into **266 fragments**, which scholars have arranged into **26 columns**.
- The roll itself is dated to **c. 340–320 BCE**, in the time of Philip II and Alexander. The text it carries was composed earlier, around **400 BCE**.
- The **official edition** appeared only in **2006** (Kouremenos, Parássoglou and Tsantsanoglou), 44 years after the find. New readings, many from **multispectral imaging**, have continued since.
- In **2015** it became the first Greek item on UNESCO's **Memory of the World** register.

## What it says

The author is **unknown**. Proposals include Euthyphron, Prodicus and Diagoras, and none is accepted. He writes in the tradition of the natural philosophers, especially **Anaxagoras**, and his book has two parts:

1. **Columns I–VI** discuss **ritual**: the **Erinyes** (Furies), the souls of the dead, **daimones**, and offerings made by *magoi* and initiates, including libations and birds.
2. **Columns VII–XXVI** are a line-by-line **allegorical commentary** on a **theogony attributed to Orpheus**. In the poem, **Night** advises, **Kronos** acts, **Zeus** swallows the phallus (or, in some readings, the body) of **Ouranos**, becomes the beginning, middle and end of all things, and then brings the world forth again. The commentator insists that the poem's shocking images are **riddles** for physics: "Zeus" is **air** or **Mind**, and the swallowing is a cosmic process.

He also quotes **Heraclitus** ("the sun, the breadth of a human foot"), the oldest surviving copy of that philosopher's words.

## Orpheus and the initiates

"**Orphic**" writings claimed the authority of the mythical singer **Orpheus**. They were used by wandering **initiators** who promised purification and a better lot after death. The Derveni author criticises people who undergo initiation **without understanding** it. His book shows that by c. 400 BCE Orphic poetry was already being **read allegorically**, like a scripture needing interpretation. The **gold tablets** found in graves from southern Italy to Thessaly, which instruct the dead soul on the path in the underworld, belong to the same religious world.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**"Shut the doors, you uninitiated."** The poem opened with a formula of **secrecy**, known from later writers and partly preserved here. Sacred words were for those "**for whom it is lawful**". Ironically, the only reason we have them is that someone burned the book at a funeral.

**Allegory as decoding.** The commentator treats the poem as a **code**: every god-name and act stands for something physical. It is one of the earliest surviving examples of **allegorical interpretation** of a sacred text, the method later used on Homer, on the Bible by Philo, and by Christian and Jewish exegetes.

**Burned for the dead.** Why was a book burned on a pyre? Perhaps it belonged to the dead man, perhaps it was a **ritual offering** for his passage. The grave goods do not tell us.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A **carbonised papyrus roll** found in **1962** on a pyre at **Derveni**, dated to c. **340–320 BCE**, containing a commentary on an **Orphic theogony**, in **26 columns**.
- It is the **oldest surviving European book** and a key witness to **Orphic** religion and early **allegorical interpretation**.

**What is not supported**

- Claims that it reveals a complete **secret Orphic doctrine**. It is one author's interpretation of one poem, and it is fragmentary.

**What's genuinely open**

- **Who wrote it**, and the exact **wording** of many damaged lines.
- The details of the **theogony**, including what exactly Zeus swallows.
- **Why** the roll was placed on the pyre.
