# The Aleppo Codex: The Crown of the Hebrew Bible

*Vault entry V38 · Manuscripts · The Israel Museum, Jerusalem · Recently added — pending full review.*

In **December 1947**, days after the United Nations voted to partition Palestine, rioters in **Aleppo** set fire to the city's ancient **Central Synagogue**. In a locked iron chest in a side chamber lay the community's greatest treasure, a Bible its keepers called ***Keter Aram Tsova***, **the Crown of Aleppo**. When the fires died down, the manuscript had survived, but **scattered and damaged**. For ten years it was hidden. In **1958** it was smuggled to **Jerusalem**. By then about **40%** of its leaves were missing, among them **almost all of the Torah**. Only a few stray leaves have come to light since.

## The book

- A **parchment codex** written around **930 CE** in **Tiberias**, then the centre of Hebrew textual scholarship. It is not a scroll: it has **pages**, as a study book, not for reading in synagogue.
- The consonantal text was copied by the scribe **Shlomo ben Buya'a**. The **vowel signs**, **cantillation accents** and **marginal notes** (the *Masorah*) were added by **Aaron ben Asher**, the most renowned of the **Masoretes**, the families of scholars who fixed the pronunciation and reading of the Bible.
- Originally about **480 leaves**; about **295** survive. The surviving text begins in **Deuteronomy 28**.
- Its journey: from **Tiberias** to **Jerusalem**, where the Karaite community held it. It was taken in the Crusader capture of 1099 and ransomed. It went to **Cairo**, and by the late fourteenth century to **Aleppo**, where it stayed for some 600 years.

## Maimonides' model

In the twelfth century **Moses Maimonides**, in Egypt, used a Bible "**known in Egypt**" corrected by **Ben Asher**, which "everyone relied upon", to set the rules for how a Torah scroll's open and closed sections should be laid out. Most scholars identify that book with the Aleppo Codex. That endorsement made it the **model** for the Masoretic text. It is why the loss of its Torah pages is felt so keenly: they would have shown exactly the text that Maimonides saw.

## The missing leaves

What happened to the missing 40% is still argued over. Some leaves were surely **burned** in 1947. But a few leaves and fragments have surfaced in private hands, and journalists and scholars have asked whether the damage was **all** from the fire, or whether pages were **taken** in the years of hiding and transit. The book's own custody, from the Aleppo community to the Ben-Zvi Institute in Jerusalem, led to a **legal dispute over ownership** and, decades later, to investigative reporting. The facts about who held which pages, and when, remain **disputed**.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**Signs added to a fixed text.** The Hebrew Bible was written in **consonants only**. The Masoretes did not change a letter. They added **dots and dashes** above and below the letters for vowels, and **accent marks** that show how each verse is chanted and where it breaks. The interactive text above lets you remove the vowel signs to see the consonantal text they were fitted around.

**The Masorah as guardian.** In the margins, tiny notes count words and letters and flag unusual spellings: "this form occurs three times". They are a **checksum** in manuscript form, meant to stop copyists from introducing errors.

**The "Crown".** Calling the manuscript a **keter**, a crown, marked it as the community's glory. The community believed that its presence **protected** Aleppo's Jews and that curses would fall on anyone who removed it.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A **Tiberian Masoretic codex** of c. **930**, pointed and annotated by **Aaron ben Asher**, kept for centuries in **Aleppo**, **damaged in 1947** and brought to **Jerusalem** in **1958**.
- About **295** of some **480** leaves survive, with **most of the Torah lost**.
- Its identification with the Ben Asher codex praised by **Maimonides** is accepted by most scholars.

**What is not supported**

- That the codex was entirely destroyed in 1947. Most of it survived.
- Claims of precise knowledge of the fate of the missing leaves.

**What's genuinely open**

- **Where the missing leaves went**: burned, lost, or held privately.
- Details of the codex's **custody** between 1947 and 1958.
