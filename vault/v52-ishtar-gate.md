# The Ishtar Gate: Babylon's Blue Door of Dragons

*Vault entry V52 · Relics & Objects · Pergamonmuseum, Berlin (reconstruction) · Recently added — pending full review.*

Each spring, at the **New Year festival** (*Akitu*), the statues of Babylon's gods were carried in procession along a broad road lined with walls of **blue glazed brick**, where **lions** strode in yellow and white relief. The road ran through a double gate faced with the same shining blue, set with row after row of **bulls** and **dragons**. The king who built it, **Nebuchadnezzar II** (r. 605–562 BCE), set a plaque in it:

> I placed wild bulls and ferocious dragons in the gateways and thus adorned them with luxurious splendour so that people might gaze on them in wonder.

This was the Babylon of the Judean exile. Many of the exiles taken there after 597 and 586 BCE will have seen it.

## The gate

- The **eighth gate** of the inner city of Babylon, on its north side, dedicated to the goddess **Ishtar**. It was built around **575 BCE**, in several phases, over earlier gates.
- Faced with **glazed bricks** in deep blue, with moulded relief animals in yellow, white and brown: rows of **aurochs** (wild bulls) and **mušḫuššu** (dragons).
- The **Processional Way** leading to it was lined with about **120 lions**, animals of Ishtar.

## From Babylon to Berlin

A German expedition under **Robert Koldewey** excavated Babylon from **1899 to 1917** and uncovered the gate's foundations and tens of thousands of glazed brick fragments. The fragments were shipped to **Berlin**, sorted and reassembled over years, and the **front gate** and part of the Processional Way were rebuilt in the **Pergamonmuseum** in the **1930s**. The much larger rear gate was not rebuilt. Lions and other panels are also in museums in **Istanbul, Paris, New York, Chicago** and elsewhere. **Iraq** has called for the return of Babylonian antiquities, and parts of the gate's foundations still stand at the site of Babylon, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since **2019**.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**Beasts as gods.** Each animal stands for a god: the **dragon** (mušḫuššu) for **Marduk**, Babylon's supreme god, and his son **Nabu**; the **bull** for the storm god **Adad**; the **lion** for **Ishtar**. A worshipper walking the Processional Way passed between the gods' own emblems.

**Blue as the sacred.** The deep blue glaze imitated **lapis lazuli**, the precious stone of the gods, brought from Afghanistan. The gate announced the city as a dwelling of gods.

**The procession.** At the **Akitu** festival Marduk's statue left his temple, the **Esagila**, and the king "took the hand of Bel [Marduk]", renewing his right to rule. The gate framed that renewal.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A **glazed-brick gate** built by **Nebuchadnezzar II** c. **575 BCE**, decorated with **bulls and dragons**, with a **Processional Way** of **lions**, excavated by **Koldewey** and **rebuilt in Berlin** from original bricks.

**What is not supported**

- That the Berlin gate is the **whole** structure. It is the smaller front gate, reconstructed with many modern bricks alongside the original ones.

**What's genuinely open**

- How the **phases** of construction relate to each other, and the precise original arrangement of the animal rows.
- The future of calls for **restitution**.
