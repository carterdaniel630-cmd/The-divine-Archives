# The Veil of Veronica: The "True Image" and Its Doubles

*Vault entry V25 · Relics & Sacred Objects · A relic kept in St Peter's Basilica, Rome; a rival image at Manoppello (Abruzzo) · Recently added — pending full review.*

During the **first Christian Jubilee** in **1300**, pilgrims crowded into Old St Peter's in Rome to see one thing above all others: a cloth said to bear the **face of Christ**, impressed on it when a woman wiped his face on the way to Calvary. The poet **Dante**, who may have been among them, wrote of the pilgrim who comes from far away "to see our **Veronica**". The name was understood as ***vera icon***, "**true image**". It became one of the great wonders of medieval Rome, and one of the most copied images in Europe.

## The legend and the name

- **Not in the Gospels.** No Gospel mentions a woman wiping Jesus's face. The story grew from **earlier legends**. In the apocryphal **Acts of Pilate** a woman named **Berenike/Veronica** is identified with the woman healed of bleeding (Mark 5). Later tales describe a portrait of Christ that healed the emperor Tiberius. The **wiping on the road to Calvary** became the familiar form in the **later Middle Ages** and entered the **Stations of the Cross**.
- **"Vera icon."** The popular derivation of *Veronica* from *vera icon* is a **medieval folk etymology**. The name is really a Latin form of the Greek *Berenike*. **The etymology is false, but its symbolism is real**: the relic was venerated as **the** authentic image.

## The Roman relic

- A cloth identified as the Veronica was venerated at St Peter's in the **twelfth century**. It was **shown to pilgrims** at the **1300 Jubilee** and in later Holy Years.
- During the **Sack of Rome (1527)**, contemporary letters report that the Veronica was **stolen or destroyed**. Others report that it survived. **The Vatican has never declared it lost**, and a relic is still kept in one of the great piers of the new St Peter's. It is shown briefly, **from a distance**, each year on **Passion Sunday**. Observers report that **no image can be made out** at that distance.
- In the early **seventeenth century** the papacy **restricted reproductions** of the Veronica. Some historians read that restriction as a sign that the image was no longer visible, or no longer the original. **That inference is debated.**

## The Manoppello image

- In a Capuchin church at **Manoppello** in the Abruzzo hangs a **thin, nearly transparent veil** showing a man's face, **eyes open**, visible from both sides depending on the light. Local tradition says it arrived in the **sixteenth century**.
- In **1999** the Jesuit art historian **Heinrich Pfeiffer** argued that it is the **original Roman Veronica**, removed from St Peter's around the time of rebuilding. Claims that the veil is woven of **sea-silk (byssus)** and that its image is **not painted** have been made by supporters. **Neither claim has been published in independent, peer-reviewed analysis.**
- In **2006** **Pope Benedict XVI** visited the shrine and prayed before the image. He did **not** pronounce on its authenticity.

## Symbology

**The true image.** Veronica relics answered the **Byzantine tradition of images "not made by hands"** (*acheiropoieta*), such as the Mandylion of Edessa. They offered the West its own **authentic face of Christ** as a pilgrimage goal. Their authority came from **contact with the living Christ**, which set them apart from ordinary art.

**Copies that carried power.** Pilgrims bought **badges and painted copies** of the Veronica. Prayers said before it earned **indulgences**. The image was **multiplied** so its sacredness could travel. This documented **medieval economy of images** runs through the whole Vault.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A cloth venerated as the **Veronica** was a major relic of **Old St Peter's**, displayed at the **1300 Jubilee** and later Holy Years. Contemporary reports say it was **damaged, stolen or lost** in the **Sack of Rome (1527)**, though the Vatican **still keeps and shows** a Veronica relic.
- The **wiping story** is **not in the Gospels**. It developed from **apocryphal and medieval legend**. *Vera icon* is a **folk etymology**.

**What is not supported**

- That any Veronica image, Roman or Manoppello, is **proven** to bear the **face of Christ**. The **byssus** and **"not painted"** claims for Manoppello are also unproven.

**What's genuinely open**

- **What happened to the Roman Veronica** in 1527 and after.
- **What the Manoppello veil is made of**, and **when and how** its image was made.
