# The Holy Grail: A Cup Made of Stories

*Vault entry V14 · Relics & Sacred Objects · Claimants in Valencia, Genoa, León and elsewhere · Recently added — pending full review.*

In the Cathedral of **Valencia**, in a chapel of its own, stands a small cup of **dark red agate**, about 9 centimetres across. It is mounted on a gold stem with two curved handles and a base carved from an inverted cup of chalcedony. Pope John Paul II celebrated Mass with it in 1982, and Benedict XVI did the same in 2006, calling it "this most famous chalice". It is the most widely known of the objects venerated as the **Holy Chalice**, the cup of the Last Supper. It is **not the only one**. Around the world, perhaps **two hundred places** claim a Grail. What connects them all is not an ancient chain of custody but **a set of stories written in France around the year 1200**.

## The Gospel cup and the literary Grail

- **The Gospels.** Jesus took **"a cup"** at the Last Supper (Mark 14:23; Matthew 26:27; Luke 22:17–20; 1 Corinthians 11:25). **No Gospel says what became of it.**
- **Chrétien de Troyes, *Perceval, or the Story of the Grail* (c. 1190).** A mysterious **graal**, apparently a serving dish, is carried in procession in a castle. **Chrétien does not say it is the cup of the Last Supper**, and the poem breaks off unfinished.
- **Robert de Boron, *Joseph d'Arimathie* (late 12th / early 13th century).** Here the Grail becomes **the vessel of the Last Supper**, used by **Joseph of Arimathea** to catch **Christ's blood** at the Cross and carried west by his family.
- **The Vulgate Cycle and Malory.** The Grail quest becomes the spiritual summit of the **Arthurian legend**: Galahad's quest, the Fisher King, the wasteland.

## The claimants

- **Valencia (Santo Cáliz).** The **agate cup** itself has been dated by some archaeologists to between the **second century BCE and first century CE**, from its style and workmanship. The **mounting is medieval**. Its documented history begins with a **1399** record at the monastery of **San Juan de la Peña** in Aragon. **The link to the Last Supper is tradition.**
- **Genoa (Sacro Catino).** A hexagonal green **dish**, taken from **Caesarea in 1101** during the First Crusade, once believed to be emerald. It was identified with the Grail only later. It proved to be **glass**, and it was **broken** after being taken to Paris under Napoleon. Modern study considers it **Islamic glass of the ninth or tenth century**.
- **León (Chalice of Doña Urraca).** An onyx cup in a jewelled eleventh-century mounting, in the Basilica of San Isidoro. In **2014** two historians argued that it was the Grail, using medieval Egyptian texts. **Most specialists rejected the argument.**

## Symbology

**The Grail as symbol before object.** In the romances the Grail stands for **the Eucharist, divine grace, and a purity only the worthy can approach**. The Fisher King's wound and the wasted land read as **a kingdom's health bound to its king's spiritual state**. This is the same logic as the archive's theme chapter on **sacred kingship** (ch49).

**Modern mythologies.** Twentieth-century books reinterpreted the Grail as a **bloodline** ("San Graal" read as "sang réal", "royal blood") descending from Jesus and Mary Magdalene. That idea was popularised by *The Holy Blood and the Holy Grail* (1982) and *The Da Vinci Code* (2003). The "Priory of Sion" documents behind it were **exposed as twentieth-century forgeries** by their own creator. **This is fiction built on a hoax, not a hidden history.**

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- The Gospels mention **a cup** at the Last Supper and say nothing of its fate. The **Grail** first appears in **Chrétien de Troyes (c. 1190)**, and **Robert de Boron** made it the **Last Supper vessel** and linked it with **Joseph of Arimathea**.
- **Valencia's agate cup** is ancient in style but documented only from **1399**. **Genoa's Sacro Catino** is **medieval Islamic glass**. **León's chalice** is an eleventh-century royal object.

**What is not supported**

- That any surviving object is **proven** to be the Last Supper cup. The **"bloodline"** Grail and the **Priory of Sion** are also unsupported; the Priory documents were **forgeries**.

**What's genuinely open**

- The **source** of Chrétien's graal: Celtic, Byzantine, or liturgical.
- The **origin** of the Valencia agate cup itself.
