# The Sefer Yetzirah: The Book of Creation

*Vault entry V80 · Texts & Tablets · Date of composition disputed (late antiquity to early Islamic period)*

It is one of the shortest books in Jewish literature, a few pages long, and one of the strangest. It begins: "**With thirty-two wondrous paths of wisdom** Yah, the Lord of hosts, engraved and created his world." It then describes those paths: **ten** *sefirot* and the **twenty-two** letters of the Hebrew alphabet. God, in this book, makes the world the way a scribe makes a text, by carving, weighing and combining **letters**. The ***Sefer Yetzirah***, the "Book of Formation" or "Book of Creation", is the oldest surviving work of Jewish speculation about language and cosmos, and the root of much later Kabbalah.

## What it says

- **Thirty-two paths.** Ten ***sefirot belimah***, "numbers (or spheres) of nothingness", and twenty-two letters. The *sefirot* here are not yet the divine attributes of later Kabbalah: they are ten dimensions, the depths of beginning and end, good and evil, above and below, east, west, north and south.
- **Three mothers.** The letters **aleph, mem and shin** (א מ ש) correspond to **air, water and fire**.
- **Seven doubles.** The seven letters that once had two pronunciations, **bet, gimel, dalet, kaf, pe, resh, tav** (ב ג ד כ פ ר ת), correspond to the **seven planets**, the seven days and seven openings of the head.
- **Twelve simples.** The other twelve letters correspond to the **twelve signs of the zodiac**, the twelve months and twelve organs or faculties.
- **The 231 gates.** Every letter is paired with every other, forming **231 gates** (the number of pairs among twenty-two letters), and "all that is formed and all that is spoken" goes out through them.

## When, and by whom?

Tradition attributes the book to the patriarch **Abraham**. Its date is one of the most disputed questions in Jewish studies: **Gershom Scholem** placed it in late antique Palestine (third to sixth centuries CE); others argue for a later origin in the early Islamic world, around the eighth or ninth century. It survives in **three recensions**, short, long and "Saadyan", and the first known commentaries are from the tenth century, by **Saadia Gaon**, **Dunash ibn Tamim** and **Shabbetai Donnolo**.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**Letters as building blocks.** The book's claim is that the Hebrew letters are not signs *about* things but the stuff things are made of, a view that made Hebrew a sacred technology in later Kabbalah, magic and the legends of the **golem**, whose makers were said to combine letters according to this book.

**Number.** 32 is written with the letters *lamed-bet*, **לב**, *lev*, "heart"; later readers noticed that the Torah begins with *bet* and ends with *lamed*. Ten, three, seven and twelve structure the whole text.

**Correspondences.** The book is an early example of a system linking letters, elements, planets, zodiac signs, months and parts of the body: the model of the "correspondences" of Western esotericism, including the Hermetic Tree of Life, whose 22 paths are the 22 letters.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- The text's structure: **32 paths = 10 sefirot + 22 letters**, divided into **3 mothers, 7 doubles and 12 simples**, with the **231 gates**.
- It existed by the **tenth century**, when Saadia Gaon and others commented on it.

**What is not supported**

- That Abraham wrote it, as a historical claim.
- That combining its letters can create life; the golem stories are later legends.

**What's genuinely open**

- The **date and setting** of composition, from late antiquity to the early Islamic period.
- Which recension is closest to the original.
