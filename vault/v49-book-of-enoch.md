# The Book of Enoch: The Scripture Only Ethiopia Kept

*Vault entry V49 · Manuscripts · Ge'ez manuscripts in Ethiopia and Europe; Aramaic fragments from Qumran*

Genesis says only this of **Enoch**, seventh from Adam: he "walked with God; and he was not, for God took him" (Genesis 5:24). From that one strange line grew an entire literature. By the third century BCE, Jewish writers were composing books in Enoch's name: visions of heaven, a history of the fallen angels, the secrets of the calendar, the end of days. The **Letter of Jude** in the New Testament quotes one of them as prophecy. Then, in most of the Christian world, the book disappeared. In **1773** the Scottish traveller **James Bruce** came home from Ethiopia with **three manuscripts** of it, written in **Ge'ez**, the classical language of the Ethiopian church, where it had been read as **Scripture** all along.

## The book and its witnesses

What we call **1 Enoch** is really **five books** gathered into one. The interactive codex above lists them:

1. **The Book of the Watchers** (chapters 1–36).
2. **The Parables** or Similitudes (37–71).
3. **The Astronomical Book** (72–82).
4. **The Dream Visions** (83–90).
5. **The Epistle of Enoch** (91–108).

The evidence comes in three languages:

- **Aramaic**: fragments of **eleven** manuscripts from **Qumran Cave 4** (4Q201–212), the earliest from around **200 BCE**. They cover every part **except the Parables**, perhaps a fifth of the whole text.
- **Greek**: substantial parts, including a sixth-century codex from Akhmim in Egypt.
- **Ge'ez**: the **only complete text**, translated in late antiquity. The surviving manuscripts date from the **fourteenth or fifteenth century** onwards, and there are many of them.

**Richard Laurence** published the first English translation from Bruce's manuscripts in **1821**.

## The Watchers

The book's most influential story expands Genesis 6:1–4, where "the sons of God" take human wives and "the Nephilim" are on the earth. In Enoch the sons of God are angels called **Watchers**, led by **Shemihazah** and **Asael** (Azazel). They descend on Mount Hermon, father **giants**, and teach humanity **metallurgy, weapons, cosmetics, sorcery and astrology**. The earth fills with violence; the **Flood** is God's answer. This became the root of Jewish and Christian ideas of **fallen angels** and **demons** as the spirits of the dead giants.

## Canon: in and out

- **Jude 14–15** quotes 1 Enoch 1:9, and 2 Peter 2:4 alludes to its angels in chains. Several early Christian writers, such as **Tertullian**, valued it.
- By the fourth century most churches had **rejected** it. It is not in the Jewish Bible or in Catholic, Orthodox or Protestant Bibles.
- The **Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church** and the **Eritrean** church include it in their **canon**, and the **Beta Israel** (Ethiopian Jews) also revered it.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**364 days.** The Astronomical Book describes a **364-day solar year**: exactly 52 weeks, so that every festival falls on the same weekday each year. The Qumran community followed this calendar, in conflict with the Temple's lunar calendar.

**Forbidden knowledge.** The Watchers' teaching links **technology and secret arts** with corruption. The theme runs through later ideas of illicit magic and of knowledge as temptation.

**The Son of Man.** The Parables speak of a pre-existent heavenly judge called **the Son of Man** and **the Chosen One**. How this relates to the Gospels' use of the phrase for Jesus is a major, **unresolved** question, bound up with the dating of the Parables.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- 1 Enoch is a composite **Jewish** work written between the **third century BCE** and the **first century CE**, with **Aramaic fragments at Qumran** and a complete text only in **Ge'ez**.
- It is **quoted in Jude** and is **canonical** in the Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox churches.

**What is not supported**

- That it was written by the **biblical Enoch**. It is pseudepigraphic, like most apocalyptic literature.
- Popular claims that it records **real giants** or **ancient visitors**. It is a religious text interpreting Genesis.

**What's genuinely open**

- The **date of the Parables** and their relation to the Gospels' **Son of Man**.
- When exactly the **Ge'ez translation** was made, and from what text.
