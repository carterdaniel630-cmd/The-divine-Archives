# The Phaistos Disc: Printed Signs Nobody Can Read

*Vault entry V56 · Relics & Objects · Heraklion Archaeological Museum, Crete · Recently added — pending full review.*

On **3 July 1908** the Italian archaeologist **Luigi Pernier** was excavating the **Minoan palace of Phaistos** in southern Crete. In a room of the palace complex he found a small disc of fired clay, about **16 centimetres** across. Both faces were covered with a **spiral** of little pictures: a walking man, a head with a plumed crest, a fish, a bird, a ship, a flower. Each sign had been **pressed into the wet clay with a stamp**, so that every occurrence of a sign is identical. It is the earliest known text made with something like **movable type**, and it has never been deciphered.

## The disc

- **241 or 242** stamped signs (the count depends on one uncertain sign) of **45 distinct types**, arranged in **61 groups** separated by incised lines: **31** on side A, **30** on side B.
- The text runs in a spiral, most scholars think from the **rim inwards**.
- Usually dated to the **Middle Minoan** period, c. **1850–1600 BCE**, from its find context, although the context has been questioned.
- It is **unique**. No other text uses the same signs, except for a single possible parallel sign on a sealing found in **1955**.

## Why it cannot be read

Decipherments need **enough text** and ideally a **bilingual** or known names. The Rosetta Stone (V53) had both; Linear B had thousands of tablets. The Phaistos Disc has **fewer than 250 signs**, no parallel texts and no known language. Statistical analysis suggests the **45 sign types** fit a **syllabary** (signs for syllables) better than an alphabet or a word-script, but that does not tell us the language. Most specialists agree that **no proposed decipherment can be verified** unless new texts are found.

## Is it genuine?

In **2008**, for the centenary of the find, the antiquities specialist **Jerome Eisenberg** argued that the disc was a **forgery** made or commissioned by Pernier, pointing to its uniqueness, its crisp firing and unusual find circumstances. Most archaeologists **reject** this. They point to the excavation records, to the 1955 sealing and to features a forger of 1908 would not have known. A **thermoluminescence** test, which could date the firing, has **not been permitted** by the museum. Its authenticity is widely accepted but **not proven by testing**.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**Pictures that are signs.** The signs are pictures, but as in all early scripts they probably stood for **sounds**. Reading the pictures as symbols of their objects is the most common mistake in amateur decipherments.

**Stamps and repetition.** Making **stamps** for 45 signs suggests the maker expected to produce **more than one** text. None has been found.

**The spiral.** A spiral text had to be turned as it was read, perhaps suiting a **ritual** use such as a hymn or prayer. This is a suggestion, not evidence.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- A **stamped clay disc** found at **Phaistos** in **1908**, with **45 sign types** in **61 groups**, **undeciphered**.
- Its **authenticity** is accepted by most archaeologists.

**What is not supported**

- **Any** claimed decipherment. None has been independently verified.
- That it is a proven **forgery**.

**What's genuinely open**

- **What language** it records and **what** it says.
- Its **exact date**, and whether a **thermoluminescence test** will ever be allowed.
