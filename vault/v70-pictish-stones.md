# The Pictish Stones: Symbols Without a Key

*Vault entry V70 · Relics & Objects · About 350 stones and objects, mostly in north-east Scotland*

In the churchyard and by the roadside at **Aberlemno** in Angus, Scotland, great slabs of stone stand in the open. On one, above a hunting scene, are two strange signs: a **crescent** crossed by a rod bent in a V, and a **double disc** crossed by a rod bent in a Z. On another, the back of a Christian cross-slab shows warriors fighting, sometimes read as the battle of **Nechtansmere** (685). The same signs appear on stones across the north and east of Scotland. They were carved by the **Picts**, a people who left almost no writing of their own, and nobody knows what they mean.

## The stones

- Some **350** carved stones and objects bear the symbols, most in north-east Scotland.
- Some **thirty to fifty** different symbols are recognised, depending on how variants are counted, some abstract (crescent and V-rod, double disc and Z-rod, "tuning fork", rectangle), some animals (the **Pictish Beast**, wolf, bull, eagle, salmon, snake), and some objects (**mirror and comb**).
- They usually appear in **pairs**, sometimes with the mirror and comb added below.
- In **1903** J. Romilly Allen and Joseph Anderson sorted the stones into **Class I** (symbols on rough stones), **Class II** (symbols with a relief Christian cross on shaped slabs) and **Class III** (crosses without symbols). The classes broadly follow the Picts' conversion to Christianity from the sixth century.

## What might they be?

Theories include **names** of people or families, marks of **rank** or **lineage**, records of **marriage alliances**, **memorials**, or **boundary** markers. In **2010** Rob Lee, Philip Jonathan and Pauline Ziman argued from a statistical measure, **Shannon entropy**, that the symbol pairs behave like a **written language**. The computational linguist Richard Sproat and others replied that such statistics cannot distinguish writing from other symbol systems. The question is open.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**Pagan or Christian?** The Class I stones are often assumed to be pre-Christian, but no one knows whether the symbols had any religious meaning. On Class II stones they sit comfortably beside the **cross**, which suggests they meant something the Church could accept, such as names or ranks.

**The Pictish Beast.** The most distinctive animal, with a long snout, a crest and scrolled feet, is drawn so consistently that it must have had a fixed meaning. Suggestions include a dolphin, a water-horse (*kelpie*) or a mythical creature.

**Ogham beside the symbols.** Some Pictish stones also carry inscriptions in **ogham**, the Irish stroke alphabet, which are themselves largely unreadable because the Pictish language is poorly known.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- About **350** Pictish carved stones and objects with a shared set of some **thirty to fifty** symbols, mostly from the sixth to ninth centuries, and the **Class I–III** sequence into Christian cross-slabs.

**What is not supported**

- Any **decipherment** of the symbols, or any proven **religious** meaning.

**What's genuinely open**

- Whether the symbols are **writing**, and what they record.
- The dating of individual Class I stones.
