# The Benin Bronzes: The King's Ancestors, Taken and Returned

*Vault entry V64 · Relics & Objects · Dispersed since 1897; returns to Nigeria under way since 2022*

In **February 1897**, a British force of about **1,200** men under Admiral **Harry Rawson** marched on **Benin City**, capital of the kingdom of **Benin** in what is now southern **Nigeria** (not the modern Republic of Benin). The attack followed the killing, in January, of a British party that had tried to reach the city during a sacred royal festival. The city was taken and burned, and the **Oba**, **Ovonramwen**, was sent into exile. From the royal palace and its **altars** the soldiers carried away thousands of objects in **brass**, **ivory** and **wood**. They were auctioned to pay for the expedition and scattered to museums across Europe and North America. The world came to know them as the **Benin Bronzes**.

## What they are

- **Commemorative heads** of past Obas and Queen Mothers, placed on the **ancestral altars** of the palace, often holding a **carved ivory tusk**.
- Hundreds of rectangular **plaques** that once clad the palace pillars, showing the Oba, chiefs, warriors, musicians, rituals and **Portuguese** traders. Most date from the **sixteenth and seventeenth centuries**.
- Figures, bells, altar pieces, and carved ivory, including the famous ivory **mask of Queen Mother Idia**, adopted as the emblem of FESTAC 77, the 1977 Festival of Black and African Arts and Culture in Lagos.
- Most are of **brass** or **bronze**, cast by the **lost-wax** method by the royal guild of casters, the **Igun Eronmwon**, which still works in Benin City today.
- Much of the metal came from brass bracelets, **manillas**, brought by European traders. A **2023** chemical study traced many manillas to the **Rhineland** in Germany.

## Where they are

The **Digital Benin** project (2022) recorded more than **5,000** objects in **more than 130** institutions in **20** countries. The largest holding, about **900** objects, is in the **British Museum**, which is forbidden by law from permanently giving up objects in its collection.

## The return

The interactive timeline above follows the objects' history. From **2021** several institutions agreed to return them:

- **Germany** and Nigeria signed a joint declaration in **July 2022**, and German museums transferred ownership of more than 1,000 objects; the first **22** were handed over in **December 2022**.
- The **Horniman Museum** in London transferred ownership of **72** objects in **2022**; the **Smithsonian** returned **29** the same year.
- In **March 2023** a Nigerian **presidential declaration** recognised the **Oba of Benin** as the owner of returned objects. This led to debate, including in Germany, about where and how they would be displayed, and between the Oba's palace and plans for a new museum in Benin City.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

**The Oba between worlds.** The Oba was a **sacred king**, the link between the living and the royal **ancestors**. Heads on the altars **honoured** past kings and were the focus of ritual; they were not simply portraits. The head (*uhunmwun*) was the seat of a person's destiny and judgement.

**Mudfish and leopards.** The Oba is often shown with legs in the form of **mudfish**, creatures that live in water and on land, linking him with **Olokun**, god of the sea and wealth. **Leopards** were royal animals, symbols of the Oba's power over the wild.

**Coral.** The Oba's regalia of **coral beads**, shown on the heads and figures, carried spiritual power and were renewed in ritual.

## The evidence, honestly

**What's well supported by the evidence**

- Thousands of **brass, ivory and wooden** objects made for the **court and ancestral altars** of the kingdom of Benin, and **looted** by British forces in **1897**.
- Their **dispersal** to museums worldwide and the **returns** that began in **2022**.

**What is not supported**

- The once-common European claim that the bronzes could not have been made by **Africans**. They were made by the kingdom's own guild, which still exists.

**What's genuinely open**

- The **dating** of many individual pieces and the early history of casting in Benin.
- **Where** returned objects will be kept, and how **restitution** will proceed for institutions such as the British Museum.
