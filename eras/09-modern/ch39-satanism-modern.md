# Chapter 39 — Satanism: The Adversary, Imagined and Chosen

Two completely different things wear the same frightening name — the imaginary Devil-cult that persecutors have invented for a thousand years, and the real, mostly atheistic philosophy that modern people have deliberately chosen — and telling them apart, with the same descriptive rigor this book gives every tradition and none of the shock, is the whole task of the chapter.

## The panic that found nothing

Through the 1980s and into the 1990s, a wave of terror swept the United States and beyond: the conviction that a vast, hidden network of **Satanists** was ritually abusing and murdering children in secret. Day-care workers were accused, tried, and imprisoned on the testimony of coached children; talk shows and self-styled experts described underground cults of unimaginable evil; families were destroyed. It was called the **Satanic Panic**. And when the FBI's own investigator, Kenneth Lanning, and others examined the claims exhaustively, they found — after a decade of allegations of thousands of murders — essentially **no evidence** that any of it was real. There was no network, no ritual murder, no secret Satanic conspiracy. There were only frightened people and an old, old fantasy.

That is the necessary starting point, because it is the pattern of one whole side of this subject: the **imagined Satanist** is a scapegoat, not a believer — and has been for a very long time.

## Two Satanisms

Everything written under the word "Satanism" falls into one of two utterly different categories, and confusing them is the source of nearly all the nonsense told about it.

The first is **accusatory, imaginary Satanism** — the Devil-worshipping conspiracy that *other people* invent and project onto their enemies. It is the fabricated **witches' sabbath** of Chapter 32, the medieval **blood-libel**, the fin-de-siècle rumor of the **Black Mass**, and the 1980s **Satanic Panic**. It has no members, because no one actually belongs to it; it is a mask persecutors hang on the faces of the feared.

The second is **self-identified Satanism** — real, living people and organizations who deliberately take the name of Satan for themselves. And here is the fact that dissolves the horror: they are, almost without exception, **atheists**. They do not worship the Devil. They have adopted Satan as a **symbol**.

## The anatomy of the panic

The Satanic Panic did not come from nowhere, and tracing its machinery shows exactly how an imaginary Satanism is manufactured. Its blueprint was a single book: **Michelle Remembers** (1980), written by the Canadian psychiatrist **Lawrence Pazder** and his patient (later his wife) **Michelle Smith**, who under **hypnotic "recovered-memory" therapy** produced lurid tales of being caged, tortured, and made to witness human sacrifice by a Satanic cult in her childhood. Every investigation found its claims **uncorroborated** and traceable to popular fiction — but the book became a template, and its authors toured as "experts." The panic it seeded reached its costliest expression in the **McMartin Preschool** trial (1984–1990), one of the longest and most expensive in American history, in which day-care workers were accused of fantastical ritual abuse on the strength of **suggestively interviewed children**; after years and millions of dollars, it produced **no convictions**. The mechanism — recovered-memory therapy, coercive child interviews, credulous media, and self-appointed cult experts — is the modern cousin of the witch-trial machinery of Chapter 32: a way of generating confessions and accusations for a crime that was never occurring.

## The Devil's party

The *chosen* Satanism has a genealogy too, and it is literary before it is religious. Its taproot is **John Milton's** *Paradise Lost* (1667), whose Satan — proud, eloquent, unbroken, declaring it "better to reign in Hell than serve in Heaven" — is so magnificent a character that readers have argued for centuries over whether the poem accidentally makes him the hero. **William Blake** said Milton was "**of the Devil's party without knowing it**." The **Romantic** poets took the hint and made it a program: for **Shelley**, **Byron**, and their circle, Milton's rebel Satan became a symbol of liberty against tyranny, of the individual defying an unjust cosmic order — a heroic **Adversary** rather than a source of evil. The French carried it further, from **Baudelaire's** "Litanies of Satan" to the anti-clerical "Hymn to Satan" of Carducci. This **Romantic Satanism** — Satan as the emblem of reason, revolt, and freedom — is the direct ancestor of both modern self-identified Satanisms, which took a literary posture and made it a philosophy and a church.

## LaVeyan Satanism: Satan as a symbol

Modern self-identified Satanism begins with **Anton LaVey**, who founded the **Church of Satan** in San Francisco in **1966** and published the **Satanic Bible** in 1969. Its central doctrine is the surprising one: **neither God nor Satan exists** as a real being. Satan is a **symbol** — of the carnal and natural human being, of pride, individualism, indulgence, self-reliance, and defiance of herd-conformity and religious hypocrisy. LaVeyan Satanism is thus a form of atheistic, materialist **philosophy** — a kind of theatrical Nietzschean egoism — expressed through dramatic ritual understood not as literal magic but as **psychodrama**, a cathartic performance. The Satanist, in LaVey's phrase, is his own god. It is, in short, almost the exact opposite of what the word makes people picture.

## The Satanic Temple: Satan as protest

A second, very different organization, **The Satanic Temple** (founded **2013**), is also **non-theistic** but points its symbolism outward, into **politics**. Its members do not believe in a literal Satan either; they invoke him as the **Romantic rebel** — the Miltonic Adversary who says no to tyranny — and organize around **Seven Fundamental Tenets** emphasizing compassion, justice, reason, and bodily autonomy. The Temple is best known for its campaigns for the **separation of church and state** and religious liberty: when a government displays the Ten Commandments, the Temple demands equal room for a statue of **Baphomet**, exposing the establishment of one religion by insisting on parity for another. In 2019 the United States recognized it as a religion. It is, in effect, First Amendment activism in devil's clothing — and a genuine belief system to its adherents.

Smaller and darker currents exist too: a fringe of **theistic Satanists** and **Luciferians** who do venerate Satan or Lucifer as a real power, and, at the extreme, genuinely dangerous groups like the neo-Nazi Order of Nine Angles. They are real and worth naming honestly, but they are a tiny minority, and nothing like the vast conspiracy of the panics.

## Symbology and sacred encoding

Satanism is, fittingly, a religion of **reclaimed and inverted signs** — and their true histories are as revealing as the signs themselves.

- The **inverted pentagram** enclosing a goat's head — the **Sigil of Baphomet**, emblem of the Church of Satan — encodes the reversal of the spiritual by the carnal, spirit pointed downward into matter.
- **Baphomet** itself, the horned "Sabbatic Goat," is not an ancient demon but the **1856 drawing of the French occultist Éliphas Lévi** (of the occult revival two chapters back) — a deliberate composite of opposites (male and female, human and animal, "solve et coagula" on its arms) meant to symbolize the union of all forces. Its supposed antiquity, and its link to the medieval Templars, are **later myths**.
- **666**, the number of the Beast, is reclaimed from Revelation (where, as an earlier chapter showed, it most likely encoded the emperor Nero); and the **Black Mass** — the imagined inversion of the Catholic Mass — is very largely a **literary invention**, popularized by novels like Huysmans's *Là-Bas*, not a widespread real rite.

The thread is deliberate **inversion**: taking the symbols a dominant religion made terrifying and wearing them on purpose, as protest, philosophy, or provocation.

## Connections

The two Satanisms have two entirely separate genealogies. The **imagined** one descends directly from the fabricated Devil-cult of the **witch trials** (Chapter 32) and the medieval libels — the same fantasy, recurring. The **real** one is a modern child of the **occult revival** (Chapter 37, which gave it Baphomet), of Enlightenment individualism, of the **Romantic** re-reading of Milton's Satan as a heroic rebel, and of the modern rejection of religious authority — a **new religious movement** among the others of this age. To treat it, as this book does, with the same calm description as any other tradition is not to endorse it, but to refuse the very confusion that made the panics possible.

## The evidence, honestly

**Well-supported by evidence.** The real modern movements — the **Church of Satan** (1966) and its atheistic philosophy, the **Satanic Bible**, and **The Satanic Temple** (2013) with its non-theistic activism — their founders, texts, tenets, and legal recognition; and the **Satanic Panic** as a documented episode of moral panic and false accusation. The panic's specific machinery — **Michelle Remembers** (1980), recovered-memory therapy, and the **McMartin Preschool** trial (no convictions); and the literary **Romantic Satanism** (Milton's Satan, Blake's "Devil's party," Shelley, Byron, Baudelaire) behind the modern movements — is well documented.

**Not supported (as history).** That a vast, secret network of **murderous Devil-worshippers** has ever existed. The claims of Satanic ritual abuse behind the 1980s–90s panic were investigated at length — most authoritatively in the FBI's 1992 Lanning report — and found to have **essentially no basis**. The imagined conspiracy is, and always was, imaginary.

**Genuinely open.** The **membership** of the real Satanist organizations (both dispute and inflate numbers); the philosophical **boundary** between a provocative philosophy and a religion, on which LaVeyans and the Satanic Temple themselves differ; and the size and seriousness of the small **theistic-Satanist** fringe. None of these uncertainties revives the conspiracy the panics imagined.
