# VR frame budget — the three pilot rooms, measured (G3, 2026-11-17)

**Harness:** `tools/vr-frame-budget.js` (new; beside `tools/museum-baseline.js`). **Ceilings:** `handoff/vr-performance-budget.md` §2
(Quest 2 floor tier; Quest 3 for information). **Raw results:** `2026-11-17-main.json` (live `main` `5570e888`) and
`2026-11-17-stack-178.json` (the waiting living-ground stack, head `dc1f1f75` = #169 → #170 → #174 → #178).

## In plain words
In a simulated Quest 2, the three pilot rooms draw **more than the Quest 2 budget allows** on two of the counts that
matter most: **lights (8 where the floor allows 5)** and, in Egypt and Mesopotamia, **draw calls (about 200–226 where
the floor allows 160)**. The Rotunda is inside the draw-call and triangle budgets. Standing in the Wing II corridor and
looking toward the two rooms is the worst view: 268–270 draw calls and over 700,000 triangles, past the hard-fail line.
The living ground (#169–#178) adds a little (up to ~31,000 triangles in ch03) and does not change the picture.

This does not say the headset will stutter: frame time can only be measured in a real Quest 2. It says the rooms are
not yet *shown* to fit the floor device, and which levers the spec already names would bring them in (below).

## How it was measured
- The museum in the stand-in headset (`tools/xr-standin.js`, the same one `tools/xr-entry-check.js` uses, now shared):
  Quest 2 user agent, so the floor tier (foveation 1.0, framebuffer scale 0.8) is what the museum chose.
- Seven places: the Rotunda (arrival, centre), ch02 Egypt and ch03 Mesopotamia (doorway, mid-room), and the Wing II
  corridor facing the rooms (the "worst portal sightline" of §5). At each, relics are left to finish streaming in, then
  the head turns through 8 headings (every 45°); the worst heading is reported.
- Draw calls and triangles are read from three.js's own XR render (both eyes, one `renderer.render`): the
  stereo-submitted count §2 budgets. Lights are counted in the scene graph, including ones at intensity 0 (three.js
  still evaluates them). Points are per eye. Nothing on the site was changed to measure this: the harness finds the
  scene through three.js's devtools hook.
- The stand-in's per-eye field (1.6 rad vertical, aspect 0.9) approximates a headset's; it is not a Meta figure.
  A wider real field could add a few calls at the edges.

## What this means for the pilot (no decision taken here)
The levers are already in the spec and need no new design:
1. **Lights (§2, settled AM-PER-01):** in a session, take the lightning hemisphere out of the scene while it is 0, merge
   the two hemispheres, and keep 2 pooled points on Quest 2 (3 on Quest 3): 1 ambient + 1 hemisphere + lantern + 2 = 5.
2. **Draw calls (§4b, §8 rule 2):** extend `bake()` to relic meshes that share materials; the ch02/ch03 excess (~45–65
   calls) is the size the Appendix's risk 2 predicts.
3. **The corridor sightline (§5):** portal culling so rooms seen through two walls don't build or draw.
4. **Points:** 5,206 per eye in the Rotunda and ch03 views; find the source before cutting (the ceiling is 4,000).
Each is its own VR-loop piece, measured again with this harness before and after.

## Not measured here (needs the headset)
- p95 frame time ≤ 13.9 ms over a 60 s walkthrough; zero frames > 20 ms after a fade; context-lost recovery; texture
  ledger flat at steady state. These are §7 lines for the in-headset overlay (`?vrfps=1`, not built yet) and Carter's
  walk; SwiftShader frame times (350–820 ms here) are CPU rendering and mean nothing for a headset.
