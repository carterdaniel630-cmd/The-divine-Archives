## Why it is so much: the ground is drawn whichever way the head turns

The ledger records, for every drawn object, whether three.js is allowed to skip it when it is outside the view
(`frustumCulled`). **The ground's plants and creatures are not**: `world.js` line 781 (every plant scatter) and
`fauna.js` lines 165, 288, 330 and 423 (fish, flyers, creatures) set `frustumCulled = false`. At 0° those objects
alone carry **504,236 triangles**, drawn at every heading, including when the visitor faces away from the room they
are in. The per-heading totals show it: every one of the four grounds is drawn at all eight headings, and the worst
view only falls from 736k to 655k when the visitor turns their back on the rooms.

The ground planes themselves are culled normally, but the Spine's is one strip 155 m long: its culling sphere has a
77 m radius, so from anywhere near the Spine it counts as "in view" in almost every direction.

The portal culling (#187) is doing its job: every one of these grounds is in a cell the visitor can see through a door
from here (ch02, ch03, the corridor, the Spine). Portal culling decides which rooms; it cannot skip a room's ground
when the room is seen.

## What would fix it (estimates, not measured — for the next run to build and measure)

All three are invisible changes (nothing on screen changes, flat or headset); each needs its own PR and Carter's
per-item yes before it merges.

1. **Let three.js skip plants and creatures outside the view.** Plants never move (their instance matrices are
   written once), so they can be culled with their real bounding sphere plus a small margin for any sway. Creatures
   move inside their own pit, so they can be culled with the pit's box as their sphere. Rough estimate from the
   recorded spheres and the stand-in's field of view (left, right and near planes only): 0° falls from 736k to about
   560k and 90° to about 400k, but looking down the corridor (270°) stays near 670k, which becomes the worst view.
2. **Cut the Spine's ground into pieces about 10 m long.** Measured with the eyes' actual view volumes on the recorded
   frames (a one-off probe run beside the ledger, not part of this PR): of the Spine ground's 16 ten-metre pieces, 0–2 are in view at seven of the eight headings, and 12 at 0°.
   Most of its 200k triangles drop at most headings (about 300k saved at 90°–225° if every long object were cut), but
   only about 54k at 0°.
3. **A distant tier for the ground in the headset** (fewer plants, or a lower-detail plant beyond about 8 m). The
   rooms' plants are 10–11 m from this spot and seen only through a door. This one changes what the ground looks like
   at a distance, so it is an artistry call: surface it with Carter before building.

On these estimates, 1 and 2 together probably still leave the worst corridor view above the 500k hard line; 3 (or a
change to what the corridor's own ground holds) is likely needed to reach the 350k Quest 2 ceiling. **Measure after
each step; don't trust these estimates.**

## Calls (192 at 0°, 196 at the worst heading, 90°; ceiling 160)

At 0° the calls are spread out: 88 for the four grounds (each object costs two, one per eye), 44 for the room exhibits seen
through the doors, 20 for the Wing II shell and fittings, 36 for small things (Spine shell, signs, plaques). Fix 1 would also remove most of
the 88 at headings facing away from the rooms. Merging the ground's per-kind plant meshes would cut more calls but
is a separate job.
