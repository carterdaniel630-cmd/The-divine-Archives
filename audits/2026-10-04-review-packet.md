### ch45 Pistis Sophia
- The Coptic Gnostic gospel of the Askew Codex (British Library Add MS 5114), known in Europe 160 years before Nag Hammadi.
- The risen Jesus teaches on the Mount of Olives; the fallen aeon Pistis Sophia's repentances and rescue from Chaos.
- Its place among Gnostic texts, its date and its manuscript history.
- **Wikipedia:** 33%. **Sensitivity:** low. **Known issue:** the 2026-09-29 audit found the live page's text behind its markdown; to be rebuilt from the markdown before clearing.

### ch46 Creation & the First Order
- Creation stories side by side: the watery chaos (Tiamat, Nun, tehom), the combat myth (Marduk, Baal, Zeus) and Genesis's refusal of it.
- The world made from a slain body (Ymir, Purusha), the contested Indo-European "cosmic giant", creation by word, and creation from nothing.
- A comparative theme chapter.
- **Wikipedia:** 50% of only 4 links: the source log is thin and should be filled out. **Sensitivity:** low (compares Genesis with other myths; written neutrally). **Known issue:** same as ch45 (live text behind markdown).

### ch75 Siberian & Arctic Shamanism
- The Evenki word "shaman", Witsen's 1692 "Priest of the Devil", and the ethnographic record of trance, drum and the three worlds.
- The Sámi noaidi and their confiscated drums now being returned; the Inuit angakkuq and the Sea Woman.
- Soviet persecution and revival, and a critical look at "shamanism as the first religion".
- **Wikipedia:** 24%. **Sensitivity, check:** Sámi drums are sacred objects whose return is a live issue; confirm no drum image is used beyond what the owning communities and museums publish openly. The 1692 phrase is quoted as history, not endorsed.

### ch76 The Olmec
- Mesoamerica's first great art: offerings at El Manatí, the colossal heads and throne-altars of San Lorenzo and La Venta.
- Buried mosaics and jade figurines; the were-jaguar, the Olmec Dragon and the Maize God; Chalcatzingo, cacao and the ballgame.
- The mother-culture / sister-culture debate and the Cascajal block, both flagged as open.
- **Wikipedia:** 41%. **Sensitivity, check:** newborns' bones among the El Manatí offerings; the chapter says they "have led to suggestions of child sacrifice". Confirm it stays a reported interpretation and not a settled fact.

### ch78 The Scythians & the Steppe

### V88 The skull of "Mary Magdalene" at Saint-Maximin
- A skull in a gilded reliquary, venerated as Mary Magdalene's since Charles of Salerno announced her tomb in 1279.
- The Provence legend has no trace before the eleventh century; Vézelay claimed her first.
- A 2017 study reconstructed the face of a woman of about fifty without identifying her.
- **Wikipedia:** 17%, within the limit. **Sensitivity, check:** human remains still venerated in an active Catholic shrine; the museum shows a modelled reliquary (not a copy of the skull), and the entry keeps devotion and evidence apart.
