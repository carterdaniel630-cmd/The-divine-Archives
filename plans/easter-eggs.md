# An easter egg for every chapter (plan, not built)

**Status (2026-10-06):** proposals for Carter's approval. Nothing is built. Carter asked for "an easter egg added for every topic". This lists one for each of the 83 chapters. The full data, with each card's supporting sentence, is in `plans/easter-eggs.json`.

## The rules every egg follows

- **Built from what the room already has:** the symbol plate, the Pantheon medallions, the cases, the lectern, and the room's sky, water and animals. There are no new models, so phones stay within budget.
- **Effects stay small:** a light warming, a plate's art moving, an animal coming closer, at most one soft sound.
- **The reward is a true fact from that chapter's own text.** Each card's supporting sentence is quoted word for word from the published chapter, and a script checked all 83 quotes against the text. Contested points stay marked as contested.
- **Whimsy goes in the mechanics, never at a tradition's expense.** No egg animates a sacred object, shows a name of God or an image of a holy figure, imitates worship, or plays chant or drumming.

**High or moderate-to-high:** ch06 Zoroaster, ch07 Pre-exilic Israel, ch21 Islam, ch22 Patristic Christianity, ch24 Tantra, ch25 Shinto, ch26 Kabbalah, ch27 Sufism, ch32 The Witch Trials, ch39 Satanism, ch40 African Diaspora Religions, ch51 Sacrifice & the Scapegoat, ch56 Mandaeans, Yazidis & Druze, ch61 Aboriginal Australian Dreaming, ch62 Native North American, ch63 Oceania, ch65 Bahá'í & New Faiths, ch66 The San & Southern African Rock Art, ch70 Pre-Islamic Arabia, ch73 Sabbateans, Frankists & Hasidim, ch75 Siberian & Arctic Shamanism, ch80 Shi'ism, ch83 Global Hinduism & the Guru Movements. ## Every chapter

### ch01 · The Flood
- **Do:** In the Rotunda bay, tap the chapter symbol plate (the vessel on the waters) three times, pausing about two seconds between taps — one bird sent out per tap.
- **See:** Each tap sends a small bird silhouette lifting off the plate and drifting up toward the planetarium dome, where it fades. After the third, the plate's water line glows briefly. Uses the existing plate texture plus a 2D sprite; no new model.
- **Learn:** The man who first read the Babylonian flood story in 1872, George Smith, was a self-taught former banknote engraver, and by one account he was so overcome that he leapt up and began tearing off his clothes. The chapter itself flags that the detail may be embellished (the witness, Budge, was colourful), though the shock behind it was real.
- **Care:** Low. Keep the chapter's caveat on the card ('The detail may be embellished (Budge was a colorful witness), but the shock behind it was real'). Use the plate, not the Noah medallion, as the trigger, since Noah/Nuh is a prophet in Judaism, Christianity and Islam.

### ch02 · Egypt
- **Do:** Tap three Pantheon medallions in the order the chapter's opening scene sets out: Anubis (steadies the scales), then Ma'at (the feather), then Thoth (records the verdict).
- **See:** As each is tapped its medallion rim warms. After Thoth, a thin balance-beam line of light appears under the ankh plate, wobbles, and settles level.
- **Learn:** Egyptians believed hieroglyphs could come alive, so in some burial chambers dangerous creatures were deliberately mutilated: snakes were drawn without heads, or cut in half, so they could not harm the dead.
- **Care:** Low. Ancient religion; there is a small modern Kemetic revival, so keep the tone admiring, not 'spooky'. Do not animate the Pyramid Texts slab or the Papyrus of Ani (funerary texts).

### ch03 · Mesopotamia
- **Do:** Tap three medallions in descending order of their divine numbers, as the chapter lists them: Enki/Ea (40), then Shamash/Utu (20), then Inanna/Ishtar (15).
- **See:** Each medallion briefly shows its number as wedge-shaped cuneiform tally marks drawn in gold on the existing canvas; after Ishtar the eight-pointed star on the plate glints once.
- **Learn:** Mesopotamian gods each had a number in the base-60 system: Anu 60, Enlil 50, Ea 40, Sin 30, Shamash 20, and Ishtar 15, exactly half her father Sin's. Modern 'Anunnaki code' systems built on these numbers are recent inventions, not ancient doctrine.
- **Care:** Low. Keep the chapter's warning that the modern 'Anunnaki' numerology is not ancient. Do not involve the Pazuzu medallion, so the egg does not lean into demon imagery.

### ch04 · Indus Valley
- **Do:** Stand still at the river's edge for 5 seconds. The room has no figures or cases; the river and the Great Bath plate are its features.
- **See:** Ripples on the water settle into a faint square outline echoing the Great Bath plan on the plate, then dissolve. Shader or decal only.
- **Learn:** The one Indus 'code' we can actually read is not religious at all: a standardized set of cubical stone weights, in a regular binary-then-decimal ratio, uniform across the whole civilization. The script itself is still undeciphered.
- **Care:** Low to moderate. The identity and religion of the Indus people is politically contested in modern India; the egg deliberately reveals a non-religious fact and makes no claim about who the Indus people were or what they believed. Avoid the swastika motif in any effect.

### ch05 · Early Vedic
- **Do:** Tap the Indra medallion three times. Each tap plays one abstract tone: high, low, falling (an echo of the three Vedic pitch accents).
- **See:** Three soft synthesized tones from sound.js, with no words or chant, and the medallion's rim pulses with each. The third tone is deliberately 'off', and the rim flickers once.
- **Learn:** In a Vedic cautionary tale, the craftsman-god Tvashtr wanted a son who would be 'the slayer of Indra', misplaced a single pitch accent, and the word came to mean 'one whose slayer is Indra'. His son was killed by Indra: a whole theology of consequence hangs on one syllable's tone.
- **Care:** MODERATE. Vedic recitation is living Hindu practice. Never play recorded or synthesized mantra or Vedic chant (especially not the Gayatri Mantra or Om). Use abstract tones only. Indra and Agni are still honoured, so the medallion effect must stay dignified.

### ch06 · Zoroaster
- **Do:** Stand quietly within about 2 m of one of the room's flame fittings for 6 seconds without moving.
- **See:** Every flame in the room steadies and brightens slightly together for a few seconds, using the existing flame-light and baked glow; nothing flickers out.
- **Learn:** In Yazd, Iran, a temple flame behind glass has, by tradition, been fed by hand and never allowed to die since 470 CE. Its priests wear cloth masks so their breath will not defile it.
- **Care:** HIGH (living community). Zoroastrians are a small living faith and the sacred fire is their central object of veneration. The egg must never let the visitor dim, blow out or 'feed' a flame, and must not simulate entering a fire-temple sanctum (the chapter notes no stranger may enter). Keep 'by tradition' on the 470 CE date. Do not use the Faravahar.

### ch07 · Pre-exilic Israel

- **Learn:** The alphabet, one sign per sound, was invented in the Bronze Age Levant and made literacy far easier than hieroglyphs or cuneiform. It is the ancestor of Hebrew, Greek, Latin, Arabic, and the page you are reading.

### ch08 · Early Greece

### ch09 · Early China

### ch11 · Buddhism

- **See:** The tiered parasols on the stupa plate light one by one, bottom to top, as the circle is completed. An anticlockwise circle does nothing.
- **Learn:** The Dharma wheel's eight spokes stand for the Eightfold Path. Ashoka set it on his pillars, and the same wheel turns today on the flag of India.

### ch12 · Confucianism & Daoism

### ch15 · Classical Greece

### ch16 · Early Christianity

- **See:** One of the lake's fish swims close, and a simple two-arc fish outline appears faintly, as if scratched on the nearest wall, then fades.

### ch77 · The Etruscans
- **Do:** Stand in the north-west corner of the room for 4 seconds.
- **See:** The existing skylight lightning flash fires once, soft (a single pulse, within the museum's lightning limit), and the bolt on the liver plate glints.
- **Learn:** Etruscan priests divided the sky into sixteen regions: the east was lucky, the west ill-omened, and the north-west the most feared.
- **Care:** Low. No living community (only small revivals). Add the chapter's caution that divination does not work as prediction, so the egg does not seem to endorse it. Keep the flash gentle (motion and photosensitivity).

### ch78 · The Scythians & the Steppe
- **Do:** Walk one full circle round the middle of the room.
- **See:** The gold stag on the symbol plate catches the light and glints along its antlers. The eagle overhead banks once.
- **Learn:** Herodotus said the Scythians threw hemp seeds on hot stones in felt tents. Archaeologists later found exactly that kit in the frozen tombs of Pazyryk.
- **Care:** Low to moderate. This is a drug reference (cannabis), so Carter should decide whether it suits all ages. A milder option is the chapter's line that 'One study found archaeological support for eleven of the seventeen elements in Herodotus's description', but that one is about the royal funeral, with strangled servants. Do not use the Ice Maiden (human remains).

### ch79 · The First Christian Kingdoms
- **Do:** Tap the symbol plate twice.
- **See:** The two coins on the plate turn over once, and the light picks out the cross on the later coin.
- **Learn:** King Ezana of Aksum's coins record his conversion: the crescent and disc above his head became a cross, one of the earliest crosses on any coin in the world.
- **Care:** Moderate. The Armenian, Georgian and Ethiopian churches are living. Do not involve the tabot or the Ark (only priests may see them) or any relic. A coin turning over is neutral and not triumphalist; keep the wording factual.

### ch80 · Shi'ism

### ch81 · Tengri & the Mongols

### ch82 · Akbar & the Mughal Synthesis

- **Care:** Moderate. It is on a mosque gate, and Hindu–Muslim history here is a live political issue. Avoid the 'Allahu akbar / Akbar is God' coin pun (a name of God, and contested), Aurangzeb and the temple debates, and any darshan imitation of the emperor.

### ch83 · Global Hinduism & the Guru Movements
- **Do:** Stand still on the glass over the river, facing downstream, for 7 seconds.

## How it would be built

Each egg would be one data entry in a single module, `docs/museum/eggs.js`, which loads only with its room. A small set of reusable triggers covers all of them: tap in order, tap count, stand still, look up or down, walk a circle, face the walls, and time of day. Found eggs are remembered on the visitor's own phone, and the Room guide shows how many of the 83 have been found. A `tools/verify-eggs.js` check, run in CI like the game checks, confirms that every card's quote still appears in its chapter.
