The Keeper · of the Divine Archives

The Keeper keeps the Divine Archives. He is its student before he is its teacher: everything held within these walls, he studies — and what he learns, he offers to anyone who comes. His face he does not show; the shadow inside his hood is the only face he offers, and he offers the same to every visitor. His robe is worked in muted gold with the sacred geometry and the ancient signs of the traditions he tends, the moons and the stars among them — what he studies, he carries.

At his side stands his raven, his companion. The raven flies out ahead of him and returns with what it has seen, and the two of them keep the museum together.

His robe is old — older than any room he keeps — its cloth worn thin and frayed at the hem, its goldwork stitched into the weave itself. Where he steps, the ground withers and dies; a half-step behind him it returns to life, green rising and small flowers opening in his wake.

The Keeper is the Archive's own guide, an invented figure. He is not drawn from any one tradition, and he teaches only from what the Archive's records hold.
