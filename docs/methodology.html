On the keeping of this archive

Methodology

This is a reference meant to be trusted. That trust is earned one honest sentence at a time.

The Divine Archives sets out to gather the spiritual, religious, esoteric, and mythological traditions of every recorded age into a single library. An ambition that wide is only worth anything if it is exact. So the archive holds itself to a standard it will not bend, and states that standard plainly here.

Every claim rests on a real source

Every specific factual or archaeological claim is checked against real, citable evidence — primary texts, current scholarship, and the present archaeological consensus — researched at the time of writing rather than recalled from memory. Each chapter lists the works it consulted. Nothing is asserted because it is well-known or because it sounds right.

Disputes are named, not settled

Where scholars genuinely disagree — over a date, an authorship, whether an event happened at all — the archive lays out the contending positions and leaves them contending. It is not the keeper’s place to crown a winner in a live academic debate. A contested claim is marked as contested, every time.

Gaps are admitted

Where the record falls silent, the archive says so. “We do not know,” “the evidence is thin here,” and “this cannot be settled” are honest, publishable sentences. No gap is ever filled with an invented source or a confident guess dressed as fact. Fabricating a citation is the one unforgivable failure.

Nothing is called proof that is not

The archive never claims proof for anything that is disputed or unfalsifiable. It keeps a clear line between three different things: what a tradition says (its texts), what can be dug up (its archaeology), and what scholars infer from both (interpretation). One is never allowed to masquerade as another.

Every chapter ends with an honest reckoning

Because of all this, each chapter closes the same way — with an explicit account under three headings:

What is supported — the claims the evidence backs.
What is not supported — popular claims the record contradicts or cannot bear.
What is genuinely open — the live disputes and the real unknowns.

Respect, without endorsement

The archive describes what traditions hold without mocking them and without declaring them true. It covers every tradition on equal terms, and carries the symbols of none as its own. The voice throughout is that of a careful writer who refuses to lie to you — neither a debunker nor a believer.

“Recently added — pending review”

Chapters are published as they are written, so the library grows in the open rather than behind a curtain. A chapter that has gone live but has not yet completed its full review pass carries a visible “Recently added — pending review” tag, both in the listings and as a banner at the top of the chapter itself. The tag means exactly what it says: the chapter is sourced to the standard above, but its wording and detail may still be refined, and it has not yet been given a final line-by-line check. The tag is removed only when the chapter has been cleared.

A living document. As new texts are read and new findings emerge, chapters are revised. Where an account here later proves mistaken, the correction is made openly rather than quietly.
