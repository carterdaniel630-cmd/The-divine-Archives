The Sacred Tooth Relic: The Buddha's Tooth at Kandy

Every year in July or August, in the hill city of Kandy, a procession winds through the streets by night. Dozens of caparisoned elephants move among fire-dancers, drummers, whip-crackers and torch-bearers. On the back of the greatest elephant rides a casket, a replica container standing in for the relic itself, which stays in its temple. The festival is the Esala Perahera. It honours what is believed to be a tooth of the Buddha, the most sacred relic of Sri Lankan Buddhism and for centuries the sign of the right to rule the island.

The relic through time

From Kalinga to Kandy, and the procession that honours it each year.

  • 4th c. Arrival from Kalinga: The chronicle Dāṭhāvaṃsa: Princess Hemamala hides the tooth in her hair and, with Prince Danta, brings it to Anuradhapura.
  • Centuries Relic and crown: The tooth moves with the royal capital; possessing it legitimates a king.
  • 1560 Captured at Jaffna?: Portuguese chroniclers say forces of Viceroy Constantino de Bragança seized the tooth.
  • 1561 Destroyed at Goa?: The Portuguese say they ground and burned it, refusing a vast ransom. Sinhalese tradition holds the captured tooth was a replica.
  • 1590s The Kandy temple: The relic comes to rest with the last Sinhalese kingdom at Kandy.
  • 1998 The bombing: An LTTE truck bomb kills sixteen or seventeen people and damages the temple; the relic chamber is not reached.
  • Every year The Esala Perahera: Elephants, drummers and fire-dancers process through Kandy by night; the great elephant carries the relic's casket.

The relic's history

  • Origin in tradition. Buddhist sources say that after the Buddha's cremation his relics were divided among kingdoms. A left canine tooth was kept at Kalinga in eastern India.
  • Arrival in Sri Lanka. According to the Pali chronicle Dāṭhāvaṃsa ("Chronicle of the Tooth") and related sources, the tooth was smuggled out of Kalinga in the fourth century CE, hidden in the hair of Princess Hemamala, who travelled with Prince Danta. It reached the capital Anuradhapura in the reign of King Kirti Sri Meghavarna. The account is traditional. Its details cannot be independently verified.
  • Relic and kingship. For more than a millennium the tooth moved with the royal capital. Possessing it legitimated a king. It came to Kandy with the last Sinhalese kingdom, and the present temple dates from the late sixteenth and seventeenth centuries onward.
  • The Portuguese claim (1560–61). Portuguese chroniclers wrote that forces sent by the viceroy Constantino de Bragança captured the tooth in Jaffna in 1560 and destroyed it in Goa, grinding it and burning it publicly despite a huge ransom offer from the king of Pegu. Sinhalese tradition holds that the captured tooth was a replica, and that the true relic remained safe. Both claims come from interested parties.
  • 1998. Tamil Tiger (LTTE) militants detonated a truck bomb at the temple, killing sixteen or seventeen people (accounts vary) and damaging the building. The relic chamber was not reached.

Symbology

A relic as sovereignty. The tooth was the palladium of the kingdom: kings were expected to house it beside the palace and honour it publicly. That fusion of Buddhist devotion and royal legitimacy is documented across Sinhalese history. It persists in the relic's national importance today (ch49, Sacred Kingship).

The relic that is rarely seen. The tooth itself is kept inside nested caskets shaped like stupas and is shown only rarely. What the public sees, and what the elephant carries in the Perahera, is the container. The sacred object is present but veiled, which heightens its power.

Relics in Buddhism. Buddhist tradition distinguishes bodily relics (śarīra), objects used by the Buddha, and reminders such as images and texts. Venerating relics is a documented practice from the earliest centuries. It is continuous with the stupa cult described in the archive's Buddhism chapters (ch11, ch20, ch53).

✦ The evidence, honestly

What's well supported by the evidence

  • A relic venerated as the Buddha's tooth has been kept in Sri Lanka for many centuries and has been bound to royal legitimacy. It is housed in the Temple of the Tooth in Kandy and honoured each year in the Esala Perahera.
  • Portuguese chroniclers recorded capturing and destroying a tooth relic in 1560–61. The temple was bombed in 1998.

What is not supported

  • Any scientific examination of the relic. None has been made, and none is expected.

What's genuinely open

  • Whether the Kandy relic is the same object that arrived in the fourth century, and what the Portuguese destroyed in Goa.
