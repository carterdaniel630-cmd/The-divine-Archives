The Pyramid Texts of Unas: Spells for a King Who Would Not Die

Recently added · pending review

Held
In place: the Pyramid of Unas, Saqqara, Egypt
Date evidence
Carved c. 2350 BCE (end of the 5th Dynasty)

In 1880 workmen of Egypt's Antiquities Service broke into a ruined pyramid at Saqqara, the pyramid of the Sixth-Dynasty king Pepi I. They found its inner walls covered in columns of hieroglyphs. Auguste Mariette, the head of the service, was dying and doubted the report: no Old Kingdom pyramid had ever been found inscribed. His successor, Gaston Maspero, pressed on. In 1881 he entered the smaller, older pyramid of Unas, last king of the Fifth Dynasty (c. 2350 BCE). From floor to ceiling its chambers were carved with spells, the signs filled with blue pigment. Above them, on the gabled ceiling, were carved stars. It is the oldest large body of religious writing known from anywhere in the world.

The walls of the burial chamber

A schematic of Unas's burial chamber: a gabled ceiling of stars over walls of spells. The cartouche carries the king's name in hieroglyphs.

  • Unas has not died (Utterance 213): “Unas, you have not gone away dead: you have gone away alive.” The spell denies death outright and sends the king to sit on the throne of Osiris.
  • The offering ritual (the offering spells): The words of the priests' offering service, listing bread, beer, cloth and ointments with a spoken formula for each, so the offerings would never end.
  • Against serpents (Utterances 226–243): Short, very old spells to repel snakes and dangerous creatures from the tomb, some in language that was already archaic.
  • The 'Cannibal Hymn' (Utterances 273–274): “The sky is overcast, the stars are darkened…” The king hunts the gods, cooks and eats them, and takes in their power. It is ritual language of royal supremacy, not a record of real cannibalism.
  • Ascent to the sky (several utterances): The king climbs to the sky by ladder, smoke, wings or a leap, to join the sun-god's boat and the undying circumpolar stars.

The texts

  • 283 spells (called utterances in older scholarship) in the pyramid of Unas. Later pyramids of the Sixth Dynasty kings and some queens add others, giving a corpus of about 800.
  • They cover the burial chamber, the antechamber and the corridor: the route the king's spirit (ba) was believed to take from the sarcophagus out towards the sky.
  • They were written for no living reader: sealed inside the pyramid, they worked by being there, in the belief that written words, like spoken ones, acted.
  • They were published by Maspero from 1882 and edited by Kurt Sethe (1908–1922), whose spell numbers are still used. Standard English translations are by R. O. Faulkner (1969) and James P. Allen (2005).

What the spells do

Deny death. "You have not gone away dead: you have gone away alive" (Utterance 213) opens the burial-chamber texts. The king becomes Osiris, the god who died and lives again, and at the same time joins the sun-god Ra in his daily journey.

Feed the dead. An offering ritual lists bread, beer, linen and ointment with a spoken formula for each, so that the king would be provided for for ever, even if the priests stopped coming.

Protect. Among the oldest spells are short charms against snakes, scorpions and other dangers, some in language that was already archaic.

Ascend. The king climbs to the sky by a ladder, rises on smoke, or flies up on wings as a bird, to join the "imperishable stars", the circumpolar stars that never set.

The "Cannibal Hymn." In Utterances 273–274 the sky is darkened and the stars tremble as the king appears as a hunter who catches, cooks and eats the gods, "their magic in his belly", and so takes in their power. It shocked early Egyptologists. It is now read as a ritual image of royal supremacy, drawing on the language of sacrifice and butchery, not as evidence of real cannibalism.

Symbology and sacred encoding

Writing that could hurt. In some Pyramid Texts, hieroglyphs of dangerous creatures, such as snakes, scorpions and lions, are cut in half or drawn without legs, so that the signs themselves could not come alive and harm the king. This is some of the clearest evidence for the belief that hieroglyphs were living images (see Egypt, ch02).

Blue and stars. The signs were filled with blue, the colour of the sky and of the waters of creation, and the ceilings were carved with five-pointed stars, making the burial chamber an image of the night sky the king would enter.

The cartouche. The king's name Unas (wnjs) is written inside a cartouche, the oval ring of rope that symbolised all that the sun encircles: the king as ruler of the world. The cartouche in the interactive object spells his name in hieroglyphs.

✦ The evidence, honestly

What's well supported by the evidence

  • The pyramid of Unas at Saqqara (end of the Fifth Dynasty, c. 2350 BCE) contains 283 spells, the earliest inscribed pyramid texts, found and published by Maspero from 1881.
  • They are royal funerary texts meant to secure the king's survival, provision and ascent, and they draw on older oral ritual.

What is not supported

  • Claims that the Pyramid Texts contain lost advanced science or star maps encoding precise dates of construction of the Giza pyramids. The texts are ritual and mythic, and the Giza pyramids themselves are uninscribed.
  • Reading the "Cannibal Hymn" as a record of actual cannibalism.

What's genuinely open

  • How old the individual spells are. Many are clearly older than Unas, but by how much is debated.
  • The intended order of reading: whether the texts were arranged to be read from the burial chamber outwards, as many scholars now argue, or the reverse.
