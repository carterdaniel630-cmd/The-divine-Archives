✦ Where the pattern breaks — honestly

Not every “flood myth” is the same story

The Mesopotamian–to–Genesis line is a documented literary chain — Ziusudra becomes Atrahasis becomes Utnapishtim becomes Noah, the same plot in three languages. Greek and Hindu deluges share the shape but are not obviously borrowed from it. And two famous “floods” are a different thing entirely: China’s Yu the Great tames the waters by dredging and engineering — a story about good government, not a survivor riding out divine wrath — and Egypt’s Book of the Heavenly Cow has Ra flood the fields with beer dyed red to stop a slaughter. To file them all under one label is to flatten real differences.
