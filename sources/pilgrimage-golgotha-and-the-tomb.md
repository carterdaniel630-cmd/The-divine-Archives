# Sources: the Pilgrimage, Golgotha and the Tomb, c. 30 CE

Model: `docs/pilgrimage/sites/golgotha-and-the-tomb/` (status **preview**: built, noindex, off the menu). Logged
2026-10-11.

**Basis:** the research brief `plans/pilgrimage-jerusalem.md` (prepared 2026-10-06), which gives each claim with its
source and a tag: WS (well supported), TEXT (from an ancient text only), CT (contested) or CJ (our modelling guess).
Only WS, TEXT and CT items were used on the info cards. Items the brief marks **(verify)** were either left out or
worded with that caution on the card. The brief's own research limits apply: most source pages could not be opened
from the build environment, and facts rest on search results that quote or summarise the named publications.

**Owner decisions still open (brief, section 9).** The site uses the brief's recommended defaults, and each is one
place to change: no single date asserted ("Passover, c. 30–33 CE"); the crosses on a neutral rise by the road and
gate, with the traditional rock shown separately; a square blocking stone moved aside; the bench left empty; the
Garden Tomb as a Vestibule panel; no "darkness" view.

## Facts on the cards, with their sources
- The quarry (meleke limestone, Iron Age; outside the city in the early 1st century; partly filled, cultivated,
  used for tombs): S. Gibson and J. E. Taylor, *Beneath the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, Jerusalem*, PEF Monograph
  Series Maior 1 (1994); J. Chadwick, "Revisiting Golgotha and the Garden Tomb", *Religious Educator* 4.1 (BYU, 2003),
  https://rsc.byu.edu/vol-4-no-1-2003/revisiting-golgotha-garden-tomb ; Kenyon's 1960s Muristan sounding as
  summarised there.
- Kokhim tombs west of the Edicule ("Tomb of Joseph of Arimathea"); tombs kept 50 cubits from the town: Gibson and
  Taylor 1994; Chadwick 2003; Mishnah *Bava Batra* 2:9.
- The garden evidence (olive and grapevine pollen and seeds; Sapienza, F. R. Stasolla, from 2022): reports of 2025,
  https://archaeologymag.com/2025/04/ancient-garden-found-at-jesus-burial-site ; dating awaits the final report, and
  the card says so.
- The Second Wall, the Gennath Gate, the Third Wall begun in the 40s CE: Josephus, *Jewish War* 5.146–155; the
  wall's line contested (Gibson and Taylor 1994; brief §2.1).
- Herod's towers (sizes in cubits; the surviving base, which tower debated): Josephus, *Jewish War* 5.161–175
  (https://lexundria.com/j_bj/5.136-5.183/wst); brief §5.
- The Temple's front about 100 cubits, gilded, "like a mountain covered with snow": Josephus, *Jewish War* 5.222–223;
  *Antiquities* 15.391–420.
- The crosses (near the city, seen by passers-by): the Gospels (Mark 15:29; John 19:17–20). Taylor's southern
  execution site: J. E. Taylor, *New Testament Studies* 44 (1998), 180–203. Gibson's view: S. Gibson, *The Final
  Days of Jesus* (HarperOne, 2009). Yehohanan's heel nail, arms probably tied: J. Zias and E. Sekeles, *Israel
  Exploration Journal* 35 (1985), 22–27. The shape of the cross debated: G. Samuelsson, *Crucifixion in Antiquity*
  (2011); J. G. Cook, *Crucifixion in the Mediterranean World* (2014; 2019). The 3 m height is a modelling choice.
  The card says only that crucifixion was public and meant as a warning (the Gospels; Josephus, *Jewish War*
  5.449–451); the Quintilian declamation on crosses by busy roads is marked (verify) in the brief and is not used.
- The rock ("about five metres high"): commonly cited figure, marked on the card as to be checked against Gibson and
  Taylor 1994 or Corbo 1981–82. The 1986 "socket" claim is left out (weakly sourced).
- Tomb form (low square door, standing pit, bench, kokhim 1.8–2 m deep): A. Kloner and B. Zissu, *The Necropolis of
  Jerusalem in the Second Temple Period* (Peeters, 2007). Blocking stones (4 rolling stones in 900+ tombs): A. Kloner,
  "Did a Rolling Stone Close Jesus' Tomb?", *BAR* 25:5 (1999), 22–25.
- What survives under the Edicule (north wall and bench; cut away in the 4th century, destroyed 1009): M. Biddle, *The
  Tomb of Christ* (Sutton, 1999). The 2016–17 NTUA restoration, the marble slab, the limestone bench, mortar dated to
  about 345 CE: National Geographic (Oct 2016; Nov 2017),
  https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/jesus-tomb-archaeology-jerusalem-christianity-rome
- Constantine, Hadrian's temple: Eusebius, *Life of Constantine* 3.25–40.
- The Status Quo and the six communities: *Christianity Today*, "Divvying up the most sacred place" (2008).
- The Garden Tomb (Gordon, 1883; Iron Age date): G. Barkay, "The Garden Tomb: Was Jesus Buried Here?", *BAR* 12:2
  (1986); Chadwick 2003.
- The empty tomb and the resurrection: the Gospels (Mark 16; Matthew 28; Luke 24; John 20), stated as text and as
  Christian belief, not as fact.

## Model choices (CJ), all labelled reconstruction on site
The quarry's shape and 5 m depth, the distances between features, the tomb's size, the 0.5 m cubit, the towers'
placing and the raised palace ground, the city and the hills, the vine rows and olive trees, the 3 m crosses.
