# Sources: the Pilgrimage, Borobudur

Model: `docs/pilgrimage/sites/borobudur/` (status **preview**: built, noindex, off the menu). Sizes are in `dims.js`,
each marked SURVEY (a published figure) or APPROX (our estimate); the drawn surfaces and the stand-in figures are
in `art.js`; the info-point texts are in `site.js`. Logged 2026-10-18.

**How the facts were checked:** every page below marked *opened* was read from this session. No measured plan or
section of the monument could be opened, so the heights of the levels, the galleries' depths, the round terraces'
radii, the stupas' sizes and the stairs are APPROX, fitted to the published base (123 m) and height (35 m). N. J.
Krom's and J. Miksic's books, Moens (1951) and the Long and Voute paper were not read; what is said of them comes
from the sources that cite them, named on the cards. The Heidelberg journal article on Borobudur's restoration
(journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de, *heritage*, article 20031) was blocked by the host's bot check and is not used.

No Wikipedia source is used. Two pages found in searching were set aside because they are adapted from Wikipedia
(photodharma.net on the Karmavibhanga reliefs) or are travel blogs.

## Sources
1. Britannica Editors (revised by A. Tikkanen), "Borobudur", *Encyclopaedia Britannica* (online, last updated
   14 October 2026), https://www.britannica.com/topic/Borobudur (*opened*): central Java, 42 km north-west of
   Yogyakarta; built about 778–850 under the Shailendra dynasty; buried under volcanic ash from about 1000;
   found in 1814 by Raffles; restored by a Dutch team 1907–11, a second restoration completed by 1983; World
   Heritage 1991; about 56,600 m³ of grey volcanic stone enclosing a small hill; a stepped pyramid of a square
   base, five square terraces and three circular terraces; the central stupa 35 m above the base; at once stupa,
   temple mountain after Mount Meru and mandala (square as earth, circle as heaven); the lowest level partly
   hidden; the middle levels with the Buddha's life and the Jatakas; the upper level with little decoration and
   72 bell-shaped stupas, many with a Buddha visible through perforated stonework; the pilgrim's path from the
   east stairway, clockwise, more than 5 km; the annual Waicak procession of thousands of monks at the full moon
   (birth, death and enlightenment of the Buddha).
2. UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Borobudur Temple Compounds", site 592, https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/592
   (*opened*): built in the 8th and 9th centuries under the Syailendra dynasty; five square terraces, three
   circular platforms and a stupa; 72 openwork stupas, each with a Buddha; reliefs about 2,500 m² (2,520 in the
   synthesis); Mendut and Pawon to the east on a straight axis with Borobudur, Mendut with a monolithic Buddha and
   two bodhisattvas; Kamadhatu (the base), Rupadhatu (the square terraces), Arupadhatu (the circular platforms and
   the stupa); abandoned between the 10th and the 15th century; restorations early in the 20th century and
   1973–1983; inscribed 1991.
3. J. Wiener, "Borobudur", *World History Encyclopedia* (21 September 2018), https://www.worldhistory.org/Borobudur/
   (*opened*): Mahayana; Sailendra; about 1.6 million andesite blocks without mortar; reliefs about 2,520 m²;
   72 stupas each with a Buddha; abandoned in the 1400s as the Javanese turned to Islam, the reasons debated; Raffles permitted Hermann Cornelius to organise the 1814 expedition that located it; first restoration 1907–1911; the straight
   line of Borobudur, Pawon and Mendut, its meaning debated.
4. H. Gunarto, *Preserving Borobudur's Narrative Relief Wall of UNESCO Cultural World Heritage*, RCAPS Occasional
   Paper 07-5 (Ritsumeikan Asia Pacific University, October 2007),
   https://en.apu.ac.jp/rcaps/uploads/fckeditor/publications/workingPapers/RCAPS_Occasional_Paper_07-5.pdf
   (*opened*): base 123 × 123 m and height 42 m (in a paragraph citing Krom 1927; the figures
   themselves are not attributed); about 55,000 m³ of stone (Britannica gives 56,600; the card gives both); 1,460 narrative panels (Karmavibhangga 160,
   Lalitavistara 120, Jatakas and Avadanas 720, Gandavyuha 388, Bhadracari 72) and 1,212 decorative; 368 Buddha
   statues in open niches (originally 432) and 72 on the top terraces; the hidden reliefs found by J. W. IJzerman
   in 1885 and photographed by Kassian Cephas in 1890 (Santiko: 1890–91; the card gives Santiko's years); panels 19–22 left exposed for visitors; restorations by
   Van Erp 1907–1911 and by UNESCO and Indonesia 1975–1983. The figures list carries no inline citation; the paper
   cites Krom, Soekmono, Miksic, Dumarçay, Parmono and others. Its "six rectangular levels" against "five square
   platforms" is inconsistent; the model follows Britannica and UNESCO (a base and five square terraces). Its 42 m
   height is set beside Britannica's 35 m on the "How tall?" card.
5. H. Santiko, "Identification of Karmawibhangga Reliefs at Candi Borobudur", *AMERTA* 34.2 (2016; published online 19 January 2024), 129–138,
   https://ejournal.brin.go.id/amerta/article/view/3400 (*abstract opened*; the article itself was not read):
   IJzerman 1885; 160 panels; photographed by Cephas 1890–91 and covered again; the law of cause and effect, set in
   Javanese life of the 9th–10th centuries; the scenes closer to the Chinese translation T 80 than T 81.
6. G. Magli, "Archaeoastronomy of the Sun path at Borobudur", preprint, arXiv:1712.06486 (18 December 2017),
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06486 (*opened*; no journal reference is given): the Pawon–Mendut–Borobudur line at
   azimuth about 263° (83° the other way), about 7° off east–west, measured on satellite imagery; horizon about
   1°48'; the proposal that it points to sunset on the zenith-passage days (about 28 February and 11 October), the
   summit not visible from the start of the axis; "likely intentional", chance not excluded; Moens (1951)'s
   procession reading called controversial; Long and Voute's Altair reading weakly supported.
7. Y. Perdani, report on threats to Borobudur, *The Jakarta Post* (23 August 2014),
   https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2014/08/23/police-alert-over-threat-borobudur.html (*opened*): "In 1985,
   extremists detonated bombs that damaged nine of the stupas at Borobudur." The card does not repeat the names of
   those the police accused.
8. The archive's chapters ch20 (Mahayana Buddhism) and ch53 (Tibetan and Vajrayana Buddhism), linked from the
   cards for the tradition, not as sources on the monument.

## Sensitivity notes (for Carter)
- Borobudur is a place of Buddhist worship and pilgrimage. The 72 statues in the stupas and the niche statues are
  drawn as plain stand-in forms, not likenesses, as at Mogao; no monks or worshippers are shown.
- The reliefs are our own outlines, not copies of any panel, and do not depict particular stories.
- The 1985 bombing is mentioned in one line with its source, without names.
- The alignment card presents Magli's sunset reading as one author's proposal (a preprint), with his own caveat.
