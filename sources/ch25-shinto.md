# Sources — Chapter 25: Shinto

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. Shinto is a subject where the ancient and the modern are unusually easy to confuse, so the chapter keeps three things distinct: attested early kami-worship and shrine practice, the eighth-century imperial mythology and its political purpose, and the modern (post-1868) reinventions that must not be read back into the early period.

## Sources cited

*Texts and mythology*
- **Kojiki (712, Ō no Yasumaro) and Nihon Shoki (720): dating, oral prehistory (Teiki/Kuji), compilation, and role in legitimizing the imperial line** — "Kojiki: Japan's Oldest Book," World History Encyclopedia. https://www.worldhistory.org/Kojiki/
- **Creation myth: Izanagi and Izanami, the birth of the islands, Izanami's death, Izanagi's misogi producing Amaterasu/Tsukuyomi/Susanoo, the cave, Ninigi's descent, Jimmu** — "Japanese creation myth," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_creation_myth
- **Amaterasu as sun-goddess and divine ancestor of the imperial house** — "Amaterasu," EBSCO Research Starters. https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/political-science/amaterasu-mythology

*The fusion with Buddhism (the era-defining development)*
- **Shinbutsu-shūgō; jingū-ji (shrine-temples) from the early 8th c.; kami as protectors of the Dharma** — "Shinbutsu shugo," New World Encyclopedia. https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Shinbutsu_shugo
- **Honji suijaku ("original ground / manifest trace") systematized in the Heian period; kami as local traces of buddhas/bodhisattvas (e.g. Amaterasu–Dainichi); Ryōbu Shintō (Shingon) and Sannō Shintō (Tendai)** — "Honji suijaku," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honji_suijaku

*The honesty spine*

## Notes on handling
- The imperial divine descent is recorded as a **claim of faith**, not history.
- A **"pure," pre-Buddhist Shinto** cleanly separable in the medieval period is treated as unsupported; the evidence points to fusion (shinbutsu-shūgō).
- **State Shinto (1868–1945)** and the 1868 shinbutsu bunri (forced separation) are flagged as **modern constructions** and kept out of the early-medieval account except to be excluded.
- The **three regalia** are handled on the relic-evidence standard: never shown, so existence/authenticity are unverifiable; only the tradition and enthronement role are attested.
- Left open: how much Kojiki myth is old vs. 8th-c. shaping, whether "Shinto" was a distinct religion pre-modern (Kuroda debate), and Izumo's real early independence.
