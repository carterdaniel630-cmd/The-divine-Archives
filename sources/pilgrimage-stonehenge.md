# Sources: the Pilgrimage, Stonehenge

Model: `docs/pilgrimage/sites/stonehenge/` (status **preview**: built, noindex, off the menu). Sizes are in
`dims.js`, each marked SURVEY (a published figure) or APPROX (our estimate); every stone's place and state is in
`layout.js`; the info-point texts are in `site.js`; the shared stone builder is `docs/pilgrimage/megalith.js`.
Logged 2026-10-11.

**How the facts were checked:** this session could not open publisher or English Heritage pages directly (the
sandbox blocks those hosts). Each fact below was confirmed through search results that quote or summarise the
source, and is to be re-read against the source itself at review. Where sources disagreed, the card uses the
safer figure and says "about".

## Figures used in the model
- Henge about 110 m across, bank inside the ditch: Cleal, Walker and Montague, *Stonehenge in its Landscape:
  Twentieth-Century Excavations*, English Heritage Archaeological Report 10 (1995), as quoted in summaries. Ditch
  about 6 m (20 ft) wide: PBS, "Murder at Stonehenge: background". Ditch depth, bank size: APPROX.
- 56 Aubrey Holes, about 1 m across, on a circle about 87 m (284 ft) across; named after John Aubrey; later
  cremation burials in many of them (Britain's oldest known cremation cemetery): several summaries agree; "Aubrey
  holes" summaries of the excavation record.
- Sarsen circle: 30 uprights; lintel ring about 30 m across held about 4 m up; many fallen, most lintels missing:
  English Heritage, "Discover more about the Stones",
  https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/stonehenge/things-to-do/stone-circle/stones-of-stonehenge/ .
  Uprights about 4 m high, 2.2 m wide, 1.2 m deep; lintels about 0.8 m high: English Heritage teachers' kit (KS3),
  "Small-scale Stonehenge" (PDF on english-heritage.org.uk). Weight "in the order of 25 tonnes": St Andrews
  MacTutor gazetteer, "Stonehenge, Wiltshire". Mortise-and-tenon and tongue-and-groove joints: English Heritage.
- Five trilithons in a horseshoe; three complete, two partly fallen; one fell in 1797 and was re-erected in 1958:
  English Heritage (stones page; "Description of Stonehenge"; "From restoration to conservation"). Stone 56, the
  surviving upright of the Great Trilithon, is the tallest sarsen: English Heritage ("Discover more about the
  Stones"); about 8.71 m long with 2.13 m buried (English Heritage blog, found by the 2026-10-18 review through search
  results). The Great Trilithon about 7.3 m high: secondary summaries; not found on English Heritage's own pages, and
  its teachers' kit gives the uprights about 6.5 m, so the card says "about 7.3 m with its lintel" (corrected
  2026-10-18; it had been marked SURVEY from English Heritage). Heights of the other four trilithons: APPROX.
- Bluestones: from the Preseli Hills, west Wales, more than 200 km (sources give 150–185 miles); about 80 in all;
  the inner horseshoe 19, six upright: summaries of English Heritage and the Stonehenge Riverside Project. The
  number in the model's outer circle and every bluestone's size: APPROX.
- Altar Stone about 5 × 1 m, about 6 tonnes, from the Orcadian Basin, at least 750 km: A. J. I. Clarke et al.,
  "A Scottish provenance for the Altar Stone of Stonehenge", *Nature* 632 (2024), 570–575,
  doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07652-1. Thickness and position: APPROX.
- Heel Stone leaning, outside the entrance; midsummer sunrise just left of it seen from the centre; a hole beside
  it that may have held a partner: English Heritage, "Celestial Stonehenge". Height about 4.5–4.7 m above ground:
  Wikipedia, "Heel Stone" (a pointer only: about 4.7 m, with 1.2 m buried) and Stetson University's Neolithic
  studies pages (4.5 m), both through search results. The card's earlier "about 4.9 m" was found in no source and was
  corrected on 2026-10-18. Distance from the centre: APPROX.
- Slaughter Stone: fallen; named for the reddish colour of rainwater in its hollows (iron in the stone): summaries
  (the name's date was not confirmed, so the card says only "a modern name").
- Two of probably four Station Stones survive: summaries of English Heritage. Positions: APPROX.
- Avenue banks about 22 m apart; natural periglacial fissures aligned on the solstice axis; "Bluestonehenge" at the
  river end: M. J. Allen et al., "Stonehenge's Avenue and 'Bluestonehenge'", *Antiquity* 90 (2016), 991–1008,
  doi:10.15184/aqy.2016.98 (accepted manuscript: https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/25584). The Avenue's length to
  the River Avon: sources give 1.5 miles or 3 km, so the card says "some two and a half to three kilometres".

## Facts on the cards
- Phases (bank and ditch about 3000 BCE; sarsens about 2500 BCE; bluestones rearranged later): English Heritage
  and summaries; the exact dates are debated and the cards say "about".
- Sarsens from West Woods: D. J. Nash et al., "Origins of the sarsen megaliths at Stonehenge", *Science Advances*
  6 (2020), as reported by English Heritage, "Research reveals origin of Stonehenge stones"
  (https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/about/search-news/research-reveals-origin-of-stonehenge-stones/): 50 of 52
  sarsens; the core from Stone 58, taken in 1950s repairs, returned 2019.
- 2013 parchmarks: S. Banton et al., "Parchmarks at Stonehenge, July 2013", *Antiquity* 88 (2014); the authors'
  own caution is reported on the card.
- Waun Mawn: M. Parker Pearson et al., "The original Stonehenge?", *Antiquity* 95 (2021); T. Darvill, "Mythical
  rings?", *Antiquity* 96 (2022), and the reply in the same issue. Darvill's reading of the Aubrey Holes as post
  holes is from the same exchange.
- Meaning of the monument (ancestors; healing): M. Parker Pearson, *Stonehenge: Exploring the Greatest Stone Age
  Mystery* (Simon & Schuster, 2012); Religion Media Centre, "Stonehenge: a Neolithic cathedral, a healing place, or
  a memorial to ancestors?"; Durrington Walls pig bones and midwinter feasts: English Heritage, "Food and feasting
  at Stonehenge".

## Not used (could not be confirmed)
- A 33 m sarsen circle and 4.1 m lintel height (sources give 30 m and 4 m).
- That the Altar Stone's name dates to the seventeenth century; that the ditch was dug with antler picks (true of
  Neolithic ditches generally, but not confirmed for this one in the sources seen); lunar alignments of the
  Station Stones (left as "speculative").
- Exactly which sarsens stand today: the model's pattern is a simplification, and the card says so.
