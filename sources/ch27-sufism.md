# Sources — Chapter 27: Sufism

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. Sufism is the mystical dimension of Islam; the chapter keeps the tradition's teaching, the historical record, and the modern (often de-Islamized) Western reception clearly distinct, and presents the intra-Islamic dispute over Sufi practice without resolving it.

## Sources cited

- **Tasawwuf as Islamic mysticism; origins in Umayyad-era asceticism (zuhd); Hasan al-Basri (d. 728) and the science of purifying the heart; Rabia al-Adawiyya (d. 801) and selfless divine love** — "Tasawwuf," Encyclopedia.com. https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/tasawwuf

## Notes on handling
- Saint miracles (karamat) and wahdat al-wujud are recorded as **faith/theology**, not historical findings.
- The **origins debate** (Neoplatonic/Christian-monastic/Indian influence vs. purely Qur'anic roots; the critiqued "zuhd-to-tasawwuf" model) is left **open**.
- The **Salafi/Sufi dispute** over tombs and dhikr is presented as a real, live theological disagreement the chapter does not adjudicate.
- The popular modern **universal, de-Islamized Rumi** is flagged as a documented distortion of the historical, devoutly Muslim poet.
- Wine/tavern/Beloved imagery is presented as a deliberate **symbolic code** (parallel to Tantra's twilight language and Kabbalah's Sod), not literal.
