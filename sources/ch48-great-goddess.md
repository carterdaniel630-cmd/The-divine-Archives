# Sources — Chapter 48: The Great Goddess

Per the project sourcing standard. A comparative theme chapter on a genuinely contested topic: the emphasis is on keeping documented goddess-worship distinct from the largely rejected "single prehistoric Great Goddess / matriarchy" hypothesis, and from the modern (self-aware) Goddess movement.

## Notes
- Well-supported: documented goddess-worship (names, temples, texts, cults) across cultures; the persistence of the divine feminine as Wisdom/Sophia and Marian devotion; the existence of a self-aware modern Goddess movement.
- Not supported (as fact): a single universal prehistoric Great Goddess and a peaceful Neolithic matriarchy (Gimbutas's central claims, largely rejected); the reduction of many goddesses to "really one."
- Genuinely open: what the prehistoric figurines meant; how much Marian devotion continues older goddess-worship; whether the "one goddess of many names" intuition tracks something real in the mind.
- Deliberately kept distinct: the Goddess as (rejected) historical hypothesis vs. the Goddess as living modern symbol — many in the movement embrace the latter knowingly.
