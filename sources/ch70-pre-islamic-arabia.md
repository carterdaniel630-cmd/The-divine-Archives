# Sources — Chapter 70: Pre-Islamic Arabia

Per the project sourcing standard. The chapter sets the late Islamic-era literary sources (Qur'an, sira, Ibn al-Kalbi) beside the contemporary inscriptions (Safaitic, Nabataean, Sabaic, Himyaritic), which tell a story of gradual change; Islamic belief about the Kaaba and Abraham is reported as faith.

## Notes
- Well-supported: the northern deities and Nabataean betyls; Safaitic prayer, sacrifice and mourning; South Arabian kingdoms and Almaqah's Awwam temple; Himyar's shift to Rahmanan (~380) and Jewish elite; Najran (~523); Abraha's Christian inscriptions; Christian and Jewish Arabs before Islam.
- Not supported: Allah as a moon god or as Hubal; Book of Idols details (360 idols) as accurate history; Queen of Sheba in South Arabian inscriptions; Abraham's founding of the Kaaba (faith claim).
- Open: Meccan religion (no contemporary evidence); the mushrikun as polytheists or monotheists (Hawting, Crone vs critics); nomad 'lh and Allah; depth of Himyarite Judaism; the Satanic verses; Abraha and the Year of the Elephant.
- Placed in era 05 (Late Antiquity), where the richest evidence and the Himyarite turn to monotheism fall; the chapter is the prehistory of ch21.

## Flags for Carter
- **Date of the Taymāʾ stele (no change made).** The chapter says that "a stele of the sixth century BCE names the god Ṣalm of Maḥram with Shingalā and Ashīrā," in the same paragraph as Nabonidus's stay at Taymāʾ. The stele in question is the Louvre stele (AO 1505, found 1880). Stein (2013, cited above) follows J. Naveh's dating of its script and places it in the late fifth or early fourth century BCE, about 380 BCE. On that dating it is roughly 150–200 years later than Nabonidus, not of the sixth century. A correction would read "a later stele (of about the fifth to fourth century BCE)". (Confirmed via search results; to re-read Stein at review.)
- **Resolved 2026-10-05 (Carter):** the text now names the dispute, giving both datings and their sources, without choosing.
