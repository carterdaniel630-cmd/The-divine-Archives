# Sources — Chapter 47: Journeys to the Underworld

Per the project sourcing standard. A comparative theme chapter: the emphasis is on the documented descent-motif and the contrast between judged and un-judged afterlives, keeping attested texts distinct from the contested question of cross-cultural influence.

- **Inanna's Descent (Sumerian, c. 1900–1600 BCE manuscripts) and the Akkadian Descent of Ishtar: the seven gates and stripping, death on the hook, the rescue by Enki, the substitute Dumuzi, and the "land of no return"** — "Descent of Inanna into the Underworld," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descent_of_Inanna_into_the_Underworld ; "Ereshkigal," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ereshkigal
- **Greek/Roman katabasis and nekyia: Odysseus' summoning of the dead, Heracles and Cerberus, Orpheus and Eurydice, Aeneas and the Sibyl, Persephone and the seasons, Charon the ferryman** — "Descent into the Underworld," Encyclopedia.com. https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/descent-underworld

- **The Japanese Izanagi/Izanami descent to Yomi (the Orpheus parallel) and the Norse death and Hel-journey of Baldr; the Christian Harrowing of Hell** — general reference to the Kojiki (Izanami in Yomi), the Prose Edda (Baldr), and the descensus ad inferos; corroborated via the comparative overviews above.

## Notes
- Well-supported: the texts and their dates; the descent-motif as a genuine cross-cultural pattern; the real distinction between traditions that judge the dead and those that do not.
- Contested (flagged, not resolved): single-origin vs. independent invention for the parallels (esp. Orpheus/Izanagi); and how much the judged afterlife of Judaism/Christianity/Islam owes to Zoroastrian Persia.
- Genuinely open: why the descent-and-return story is so universal. No afterlife map is treated as a verified account of death.
- Connects to ch01 (flood/renewal), ch02 (Egypt/Duat), ch06 (Zoroaster), ch07/ch10/ch16/ch21 (Sheol → judged afterlife), ch50 (apocalypse/last judgment), ch53 (Tibetan bardo).
