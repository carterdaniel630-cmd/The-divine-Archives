# Sources — Chapter 28: Scholasticism

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. Scholasticism is abundantly documented; the chapter keeps the historical record (the movement, its figures and texts) distinct from the philosophical status of its arguments (which it records as made, not as proven), and corrects the "Dark Ages" myth without overclaiming in the opposite direction.

## Sources cited

*Definition, method, universities*
- **Scholasticism as a method (c. 1100–1500); the schoolmen; lectio/quaestio/disputatio; reconciling faith and reason; Aristotelian logic + Christian theology** — "Scholasticism," New World Encyclopedia (https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Scholasticism); Britannica (https://www.britannica.com/topic/Scholasticism)
- **Recovery of Aristotle via Toledo translations from Arabic; Avicenna and Averroes as commentators; the rise of Paris/Oxford/Bologna** — Britannica, "Scholasticism."

*Figures and texts*
- **Anselm: fides quaerens intellectum and the ontological argument; Abelard's Sic et Non; Peter Lombard's Four Books of Sentences as the standard textbook** — Britannica / New World Encyclopedia, "Scholasticism."
- **Aquinas's Five Ways (motion, efficient causation, contingency, degrees of perfection, design/teleology)** — "Five Ways (Aquinas)," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Ways_(Aquinas)
- **Duns Scotus (haecceity, univocity of being) and William of Ockham (nominalism, Ockham's razor); the problem of universals (realism vs. nominalism)** — "Ockham, William of," Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy. https://iep.utm.edu/ockham/
- **Maimonides, Guide for the Perplexed (c. 1190), reconciling Aristotle with the Torah in dialogue with Avicenna and Averroes** — "The Influence of Islamic Thought on Maimonides," Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/maimonides-islamic/
- **The Condemnations of 1277 (Bishop Étienne Tempier, 219 theses); the Duhem thesis linking them to the birth of modern science (debated)** — "Condemnation of 1277," Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2003/entries/condemnation/

*Symbology*
- **The four senses of Scripture (literal, allegorical, moral, anagogical) and the medieval mnemonic couplet** — "Medieval Exegesis," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Exegesis
- **The Gothic cathedral as a "summa in stone"; manifestatio and concordantia** — Erwin Panofsky, *Gothic Architecture and Scholasticism*. https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/724250.Gothic_Architecture_and_Scholasticism
- **The "Dark Ages"/anti-science myth corrected (medieval rigor; Roger Bacon; the Oxford calculators)** — "Beyond the Myth: Medieval Science and Technology (1200–1500)," FactSpark. https://factspark.blog/posts/beyond-the-myth-medieval-science-and-technology-1200-1500

## Notes on handling
- The **Five Ways** and the **ontological argument** are recorded as **philosophical arguments, still contested** — the chapter says they were made and how, not that they succeed.
- The **truth of the theological conclusions** is treated as a matter of philosophy/faith, not a historical finding.
- The **"Dark Ages" anti-science caricature** is corrected as false; the **opposite overclaim** (that scholasticism or 1277 "invented" science) is explicitly limited, and the Duhem thesis flagged as debated.
- The four senses are noted as the Christian parallel to Kabbalah's PaRDeS; the cathedral-as-summa is Panofsky's interpretive thesis, presented as such.
- Jewish (Maimonides) and Islamic (Avicenna, Averroes) scholasticism are treated as parallel members of one shared Aristotelian project; al-Ghazali's critique and Averroes's response are noted in the Islam/Sufism chapters.
