# Sources — Chapter 12: Confucianism & Daoism: The Way and the Rites

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. It is derived from the Sources section published with the chapter; contested points are flagged as contested in the chapter text, and faith claims are kept distinct from historically attested facts.

*Note on sourcing:* Researched from standard reference scholarship (Stanford Encyclopedia
of Philosophy, Britannica) and recent peer-reviewed work on the manuscript evidence
(the Anhui/Wangjiazui *Analects* slips; the Guodian and Mawangdui *Laozi*), per the
project standard, rather than memory alone. The chapter is careful to separate the
**historical** core (a real teacher Confucius; the Warring States texts; the archaeologically
dated *Laozi* manuscripts; the Celestial Masters as a datable institution) from the
**legendary** material (the Laozi biography; Confucian authorship of the Classics and the
Yijing's Ten Wings; Fuxi's trigrams), draws the crucial **daojia / daojiao** distinction
explicitly, and flags the genuinely unresolved questions (the existence of Laozi, the
recoverability of Confucius's own words). Written to be educational, with attention to the
contrast between the two traditions and to their shared roots in the world of Chapter 9.
