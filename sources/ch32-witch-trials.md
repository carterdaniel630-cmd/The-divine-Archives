# Sources — Chapter 32: The Witch Trials

Per the project sourcing standard, and CLAUDE.md's mandate to treat the witch-trial era as documented history/persecution, not validation of trial-era accusations. The chapter holds steady that the persecution was real and the satanic conspiracy imaginary; the Murray witch-cult thesis and the "nine million" toll are named as discredited/mythical. Expanded 2026-09 into the five-section template (with the believer's lens = the persecutors' worldview).

## Re-sourcing log — 2026-11-17 (Wikipedia strip flag 86)
The 2026-11-15 strip (part 7) removed the only pointer for the Murray "witch-cult" thesis and the "nine million" toll (a Wikipedia overview), leaving flag 86 without a source. No sentence of the chapter was changed. Sources read this day:
- **Murray thesis and early toll guesses** — J. Gibbons, "Recent Developments in the Study of The Great European Witch Hunt," *The Pomegranate* no. 5 (Lammas 1998), DOI 10.1558/pome.v13i5.2 (Equinox's page gives the issue as "5 (Summer)"; the DOI string says v13i5 — the two do not agree; the DOI resolves to the article). Full text read in the reprint on a UMass Boston course page (faculty.umb.edu/gary_zabel/…/witch_hunt.html, "reprinted with permission"). Read: Murray "proposed that witches were members of a Pagan sect that worshipped the Horned God"; "Murray's research was exceptionally poor, and occasionally skated into out-right textual manipulation"; she assumed the trial evidence "was basically accurate, and that the Devil was 'really' a Pagan god. None of these assumptions have held up under scrutiny." On numbers: early estimates "ranged from several hundred thousand up to a high of nine million" and "were simply people trying to guess"; "less than 15,000 definite executions have been discovered in all of Europe and America combined"; "death tolls higher than 100,000 are not believable".
- **Where "nine million" came from** — "The Myth of Nine Million Victims," a University of Oregon course page (pages.uoregon.edu/dluebke/Witches442/; no author named on the page; its image caption credits W. Behringer, "Neun Millionen Hexen: Enstehung, Tradition, und Kritik eines populären Mythos," *Geschichte in Wissenschaft und Unterricht* 49 (1998), 664–685). Read: "It originated in the eighteenth century, with a colossal error of misguided statistical extrapolation"; Voigt counted Quedlinburg's trials (1569–1598), worked out a rate per century, and extrapolated to Europe's population "over 650 years"; published 1783 in the *Berlinische Monatsschrift*; the figure "was never accepted by scholars who dealt with the documentary evidence". Behringer's article itself was not read.
- **The modern toll** — R. Hutton, "Witch-Hunting in European and World History," Gresham College lecture, 5 June 2024, transcript PDF. Read: "Between forty and fifty thousand people died in total." The lecture does not discuss Murray or the "nine million".
