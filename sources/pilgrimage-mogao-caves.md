# Sources: the Pilgrimage, the Mogao Caves

Model: `docs/pilgrimage/sites/mogao-caves/` (status **preview**: built, noindex, off the menu). Sizes are in
`dims.js`, each marked SURVEY (a published figure) or APPROX; the info-point texts are in `site.js`; the clay
figures and painted walls are drawn in `paint.js`. The moment shown is the cliff as a visitor finds it today, with
one switchable view of Cave 17 as found in 1900. Logged 2026-10-11.

**How the facts were checked:** each fact below was confirmed by opening the page named, or through search results
that quote the source, and is to be re-read against the source at review. No measured plan or section of any cave
could be opened, so **the size of every cave and room, their places along the cliff, the height of the cliff and
the shape of the nine-storey building are estimates**. The statues and paintings are our own simple renderings of
the subjects, not copies; the stele's marks stand for its text and are not a transcription.

## Sources
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Mogao Caves" (site 440, inscribed 1987), https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/440 :
  492 cells and shrines, about 45,000 m² of murals, more than 2,000 painted sculptures, the founding in 366, the
  length of the cliff (about 1,600 m).
- Dunhuang Academy, e-Dunhuang, "Cave 17", https://www.e-dunhuang.com/cave/10.0001/0001.0001.0017 : made 851–862 as
  a memorial chapel for Hongbian; his seated portrait on a platform against the north wall; the twin-tree mural
  with the bag and the water flask, the lay woman with staff and towel, the nun with a round fan; the stele of 851
  on the west wall, about 1.5 × 0.7 m; the truncated-pyramid ceiling; the room off the north wall of Cave 16's
  corridor, found in 1900, with more than 50,000 items of the 4th–11th centuries.
- Dunhuang Academy, e-Dunhuang, "Cave 254", https://e-dunhuang.com/cave/10.0001/0001.0001.0254 : Northern Wei; the
  earliest central-pillar cave at Mogao; gabled front, flat rear ceiling; the Maitreya niche on the pillar's east
  face; the Mahasattva (tigress) jataka on the south wall, Sibi on the north, the white-robed Buddha on the west;
  1,235 small Buddhas.
- M. M. Chan, "Mogao Cave 17", Smarthistory, https://smarthistory.org/?p=57609 : the statue of Hongbian had been
  moved to another cave higher up and was returned after the room was emptied.
- International Dunhuang Programme (British Library), "Discovering Cave 17",
  https://idp.bl.uk/learning/dunhuang/dunhuang-articles/cave-17-the-library-cave/discovering-cave-17/ and
  https://idp.bl.uk/?p=23132 : June 1900, Wang Yuanlu; Stein in 1907; Pelliot in 1908; the Japanese and Russian
  expeditions; the removal to Beijing in 1910.
- S. van Schaik, "Secrets of the Cave II: The 'Library Cave'", *early Tibet* (2011),
  https://earlytibet.com/2011/03/11/secrets-of-the-cave-ii/ : Rong Xinjiang's theory of a monastic library hidden
  in the face of a threat, and the counter-arguments (about two hundred manuscripts tied to that monastery). Flagged
  **contested**.
- Royal Society for Asian Affairs blog, "Looting the Silk Road: the re-discovery of Dunhuang",
  https://rsaa.org.uk/blog/looting-the-silk-road-the-re-discovery-of-dunhuang/ : a contributor's opinion piece,
  cited only for the "plunder" view of the dispersal.
- Ancient History Sites, "Mogao Caves: A Buddhist Cave Complex on the Silk Road",
  https://www.ancient-history-sites.com/?p=133357 : about 735 caves; Lezun in 366 after the *Fokan ji* (another
  record gives 353); the storeys of the building in front of Cave 96 (four under the Tang, one added 874–885, two in
  1898, nine after the twentieth-century restorations); 35.5 m; 695.
- *China Daily*, 29 April 2025, https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202504/29/WS680ff708a310a04af22bcae0.html : the
  great Buddha of Cave 96, 35.5 m, begun 695.
- Tokyo National Research Institute for Cultural Properties, symposium abstract "Life of the Tallest Buddha at
  Dunhuang" (2002), https://www.tobunken.go.jp/info/event/2002/sympo02/abstract/abstract31_e.html : gives 34 m.
  The card gives both figures.
- The archive's own chapter on Mahayana Buddhism (ch20), for Maitreya and the jataka tales as belief.

## Notes for review
- **Sensitivity (for Carter):** the "Where the manuscripts went" card covers the dispersal of the Cave 17 items.
  Whether it was rescue or plunder is still argued, and the card says so without taking a side. Accounts of what
  Stein paid differ, so the card gives no sum.
- The founding date of 366 comes from a later record; the card says so.
- The statue in Cave 96 is rendered in the posture with both legs down and at the published height; its face,
  hands and robes are simplified. The Maitreya in Cave 254's great niche is a generic seated stand-in; its actual
  posture was not confirmed from a source and the card does not describe it.
- Not shown: the second great Buddha, the other caves, the visitor centre and the river.
- **Review fixes (2026-10-12):** the dispersal card now puts the Japanese and Russian purchases after the 1910 removal, from what Wang kept back (IDP page above); "clearing sand" and Pelliot's "three weeks" were dropped as not on the cited page.
