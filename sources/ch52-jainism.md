# Sources — Chapter 52: Jainism

Per the project sourcing standard. A tradition chapter: the emphasis is on the documented doctrines, practices, and material record, keeping the historicity of Mahavira (well-founded) distinct from the legendary lineage of the earlier Tirthankaras.

## Sources cited
- **Jainism overview: Mahavira and the 24 Tirthankaras; the shramana context beside the Buddha; core doctrines (ahimsa, anekantavada, aparigraha); jiva/ajiva, karma as subtle matter, moksha; no creator god; the eternal cyclic universe** — "Mahavira," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavira ; "Jainism," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism
- **Doctrine and philosophy: anekantavada (many-sidedness) and syadvada (conditional, seven-fold predication); the five vows (maha/anu-vratas); the Digambara/Svetambara sects; the Tattvartha Sutra** — "Jainism" (doctrines overview), PMF IAS. https://www.pmfias.com/jainism/ ; "Jainism, Founder, Symbol, Teachings, Beliefs," Vajiram & Ravi. https://vajiramandravi.com/upsc-exam/jainism/
- **Ritual practice: samayika (temporary ascetic status), puja and the veneration of the Tirthankaras as liberated exemplars (not petitioned deities), pratikramana, the Namokara/Navkar mantra** — "Jainism: Ritual practices and religious institutions," Encyclopædia Britannica. https://www.britannica.com/topic/Jainism/Ritual-practices-and-religious-institutions ; "Sāmāyika," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C4%81m%C4%81yika
- **Sallekhana/santhara: the voluntary end-of-life fast, its distinction from suicide, and the 2015 legal controversy** — "Sallekhana," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sallekhana
- **The Jain emblem and symbolism (the loka outline, the raised hand of ahimsa, the swastika of the four rebirth-states, the three jewels, the siddha crescent) and Tirthankara iconography (kayotsarga posture, distinguishing emblems, srivatsa)** — "Jain Symbol," Jainworld. https://jainworld.com/education/jain-education-material/jain-symbol/ ; "Introduction to the Iconography of Jain Tirthankaras," Encyclopedia of Jainism. https://encyclopediaofjainism.com/introduction-to-the-iconography-of-jain-hrthankaras/
- **Supporting detail corroborated via the above and adjacent chapters:** the Ardhamagadhi Prakrit Agamas and the canon-loss debate; the Gommateshwara/Bahubali colossus (c. 981 CE, Shravanabelagola) and the Dilwara temples; Jainism's influence on Gandhi's ahimsa (ch05/ch11/ch51 context).

## Notes
- Well-supported: Jainism as an ancient continuous shramana tradition; Mahavira's historicity (6th–5th c BCE); the doctrines and the two sects; ahimsa, samayika, sallekhana; the artistic/archaeological record; the influence on Gandhi.
- Not supported (as fact): the traditional dates/biographies of the Tirthankaras (Parshvanatha, the 23rd, is plausibly historical; the earlier 22 are legendary); the tradition that Chandragupta Maurya died by sallekhana; the precise date of the sect split and canon loss.
- Genuinely open: earliest Jain–Buddhist mutual influence; how much of the Agamas preserves Mahavira's actual words; the tradition's deep prehistory before Parshvanatha.
- Placed in era 04 (Axial Age) as a shramana tradition contemporary with Buddhism.
- Connects to ch11 (Buddhism), ch05 (Vedic sacrifice rejected), ch51 (ahimsa/sacrifice), ch30 (Bhakti/pluralism).
