# Sources — Chapter 63: Oceania (Polynesian & Melanesian Religion)

Per the project sourcing standard. A tradition chapter on the living Indigenous religions of the Pacific: the emphasis is on documented concepts, gods, and monuments, flagging the contested Rapa Nui "collapse" narrative and rejecting the condescending "cargo cult" caricature, while noting the missionary/colonial distortion of many sources.

## Sources cited
- **Polynesian religion: mana and tapu (source of "taboo"), the ariki and tohunga, the pantheon (Tangaroa, Tāne, Tū, Rongo, Māui, Pele), Rangi-and-Papa creation, marae worship and human sacrifice** — "Polynesian culture: Religion," Encyclopædia Britannica. https://www.britannica.com/place/Polynesia/Religion ; "Polynesian Religions: An Overview," Encyclopedia.com. https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/polynesian-religions-overview
- **Tangaroa/Kanaloa, the sea-god and creation** — "Tangaroa," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangaroa
- **Rapa Nui: the moai as ancestor images channeling mana (facing the villages), and the birdman (tangata manu) cult of Makemake (the sooty-tern egg race)** — "Rapa Nui (Easter Island) Moai," Smarthistory. https://smarthistory.org/easter-island-moai/ ; "Makemake (deity)," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makemake_(deity)
- **Melanesian cargo cults as millenarian movements (John Frum, Tanna) logical within Melanesian cosmology (ancestors provide cargo)** — "Cargo Cult," Encyclopedia.com. https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/anthropology-and-archaeology/anthropology-terms-and-concepts/cargo-cult ; "What Cargo Cult Rituals Reveal About Human Nature," SAPIENS. https://www.sapiens.org/culture/cargo-cult-rituals/
- **Polynesian tattoo (tatau): sacred encoding of genealogy/rank, and the origin of the English word "tattoo" (via Cook)** — "In Polynesia, tattoos are more than skin deep," National Geographic. https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/in-polynesia-tattoos-are-more-than-skin-deep
- **Supporting detail corroborated via the above and adjacent chapters:** the Austronesian settlement and sacred wayfinding navigation; the Melanesian "big man" and secret societies; the contested Rapa Nui "collapse" narrative; mana/taboo in the anthropology of religion (ch62 context).

## Notes
- Well-supported: the Pacific settlement and sacred navigation; mana and tapu/taboo; the Polynesian pantheon and Rangi-and-Papa creation; marae/heiau worship (with human sacrifice in some societies); the moai and the birdman cult; Melanesian ancestor cults and secret societies; the historical cargo cults; the Polynesian origin of "tattoo."
- Contested / rejected (flagged): the popular Rapa Nui "ecocide/collapse" narrative (seriously disputed by recent archaeology); the condescending "cargo cult" caricature (rejected for the movements' internal logic); "cargo cult" as an outsider label.
- Caveat: much pre-contact Oceanic religion is recorded through missionary/colonial sources with their distortions; some pre-contact forms are reconstructed.
- Placement: era 08 (Early Modern), keyed to the Polynesian settlement climax (moai era) into the age of European contact; the cargo cults noted as a colonial-era development.
- Connects to ch46 (world-parents/creation), ch42 (megalithic ancestor-cult), ch62 (mana/orenda; Indigenous traditions), ch50/ch35 (cargo cults/millenarianism), ch61 (living oral traditions).
