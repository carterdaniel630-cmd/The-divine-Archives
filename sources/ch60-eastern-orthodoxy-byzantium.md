# Sources — Chapter 60: Eastern Orthodoxy & Byzantium

Per the project sourcing standard. A tradition chapter on well-documented history and distinctive doctrine: the emphasis is on the councils, controversies, and schism as documented events, marking the Vladimir's-envoys story as legend and the theological claims (theosis, uncreated Light) as faith.

## Notes
- Well-supported: Byzantine Christianity's development; the seven councils and their doctrines; iconoclasm and its resolution; the 1054 excommunications and the long estrangement (with 1204); the Hesychast controversy and Palamas's dogma; Cyril and Methodius; 1453 and the autocephalous churches; the Oriental Orthodox and Chalcedon 451.
- Not supported (as fact): the Vladimir's-envoys "heaven or earth" story (later edifying legend); the theological claims (theosis, uncreated Light, the icon's and Jesus Prayer's efficacy) are faith, reported as Orthodox belief.
- Genuinely open / nuanced: "1054" is symbolic — the schism was a gradual process, the mutual excommunications narrow and personal; historians debate when the churches truly separated.
- Placed in era 06 (Early Medieval): the iconoclastic age and the mature Byzantine synthesis (with the later Schism and Palamas noted within the Byzantine arc).
