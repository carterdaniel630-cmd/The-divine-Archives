*Note on sourcing:* Researched from standard reference scholarship (Britannica, specialist
Celtic-studies resources, and archaeological summaries) rather than memory alone, per the
project standard. This is the archive's most source-critical chapter by necessity: it foregrounds
the **unreliability of the three witnesses** (hostile classical writers, much-later Christian-era
vernacular texts, and mute archaeology), keeps the well-attested (groves, votive deposits, bog
bodies, named gods, the Coligny calendar) separate from the **reconstructed** (systematic myth),
explicitly reserves the Norse Eddic material for the early-medieval chapter, honestly dates the
**runes and ogham to later centuries**, and flags the genuinely open questions — including whether
a single "Celtic religion," or even the label "Celtic," is meaningful. Written to be educational,
with attention to how much we cannot know about religions that, by design, kept no book.
