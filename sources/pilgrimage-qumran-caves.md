# Sources: the Pilgrimage, the Qumran caves

Model: `docs/pilgrimage/sites/qumran-caves/` (status **preview**: built, noindex, off the menu). Sizes are in
`dims.js`, all marked APPROX; the info-point texts are in `site.js`; the shared cave builder is
`docs/pilgrimage/cave.js`. Logged 2026-10-10.

**How the facts were checked:** this session could not open the excavation reports or most publisher pages (the
sandbox blocks those hosts). Each fact below was confirmed through search results that quote or summarise the
source, and is to be re-read against the source itself at review. No cave plan could be opened, so **every size
in the model is an estimate** of published proportions, not a measurement.

## Excavation reports and syntheses
- R. de Vaux and J. T. Milik, *Qumrân grotte 4. II*, Discoveries in the Judaean Desert VI (Oxford, 1977),
  "Archéologie" (Cave 4, its two chambers and the holes in the walls). Record:
  https://orion-bibliography.huji.ac.il/orion_editor/node/104819
- D. Barthélemy and J. T. Milik, *Qumran Cave 1*, Discoveries in the Judaean Desert I (Oxford, 1955).
- M. Baillet, J. T. Milik and R. de Vaux, *Les 'Petites Grottes' de Qumrân*, DJD III (Oxford, 1962) (Cave 3).
- J. Magness, *The Archaeology of Qumran and the Dead Sea Scrolls* (Eerdmans, 2002; 2nd ed. 2021): the
  settlement, the caves, the jars, and the debates over who lived there and what the caves were for.

## Facts used, by info point
- **Cave 4** (found by Bedouin August 1952; excavated 22–29 September 1952 by Harding, de Vaux and Milik; more
  than 15,000 fragments from some 500+ manuscripts; most contents removed by Bedouin first and bought back through
  the dealer Kando): Biblical Archaeology Society, "Caves and Contents (Cave 4)",
  https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/biblical-artifacts/dead-sea-scrolls/caves-and-contents-cave-4/ ;
  DJD VI. Counts differ by source (15,000 or 16,000 fragments; 500 or 575 manuscripts); the card says "more than
  15,000" and "some five hundred".
- **About 900 manuscripts; eleven caves**: Israel Antiquities Authority, Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital
  Library, https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/ (900 manuscripts in its collection), and Magness (eleven caves).
- **Enoch in Aramaic** (4Q201–4Q212; Book of Giants; earliest witnesses): J. T. Milik with M. Black, *The Books
  of Enoch: Aramaic Fragments of Qumrân Cave 4* (Oxford: Clarendon, 1976), scan at
  https://archive.org/details/MILIKEnochInAramaicQumranCave4
- **Cave 1** (winter 1946–47; Muhammed edh-Dhib; jars; seven scrolls; excavated 1949): Magness; DJD I. The card
  notes that accounts of the discovery differ.
- **The Great Isaiah Scroll** (17 sheets, 54 columns, about 7.3 m; second half of the 2nd century BCE): Center
  for Online Judaic Studies, "The Isaiah Scroll", https://cojs.org/the_isaiah_scroll/
- **Cave 3 and the Copper Scroll** (found March 1952; two rolls; cut into 23 segments at Manchester in 1955–56;
  Jordan Museum, Amman; list of hiding places, reality of the treasure debated): BAS Library, "The Mysterious
  Copper Scroll", https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/article/the-mysterious-copper-scroll ; É. Puech et
  al., *Le Rouleau de cuivre de la grotte 3 de Qumrân (3Q15)* (Brill, 2006).
- **The looted cave of 2017 ("Q12")** (Gutfeld and Ovadia, Hebrew University, with Price, Liberty University;
  1950s pickaxe head; blank parchment; designated Q12; the claim called speculation): BAS, "New Dead Sea Scroll
  Cave", https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/news/new-dead-sea-scroll-cave/
- **Where the scrolls are, and the ownership dispute**: IAA; the Jordan Museum (Copper Scroll); Magness. Stated
  without taking a side, as plans/pilgrimage-sites.md requires.
