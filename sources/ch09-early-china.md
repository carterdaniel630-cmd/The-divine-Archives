# Sources — Chapter 9: Early China: Ancestors, Oracle Bones, and the Mandate of Heaven

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. It is derived from the Sources section published with the chapter; contested points are flagged as contested in the chapter text, and faith claims are kept distinct from historically attested facts.

## Sources cited

*Oracle bones and Shang religion*
- [Smarthistory: Oracle Bone, Shang Dynasty](https://smarthistory.org/oracle-bone/);
  [Wikipedia: Shang dynasty religious practitioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty_religious_practitioners);
  David N. Keightley, *Sources of Shang History* and *The Ancestral Landscape* (the

*The Zhou and the Mandate of Heaven*
- [World History Encyclopedia: Mandate of Heaven](https://www.worldhistory.org/Mandate_of_Heaven/);

*Bronzes, sacrifice, and Sanxingdui*
- [Wikipedia: Taotie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taotie);
  [Wikipedia: Human sacrifice in ancient Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sacrifice_in_ancient_Chinese_culture);

*Divination and symbol*
- The *Yijing* (*Book of Changes*): Richard Rutt, *The Book of Changes (Zhouyi): A
  Bronze Age Document*; on ritual jade (*bi* and *cong*), museum and archaeological
  overviews.

*Note on sourcing:* Researched from current scholarship (Keightley on the Shang) and
standard reference works rather than memory alone, per the project standard. The live
debates — the nature of Di, the meaning of the taotie, the origins of the ritual
jades, and the dating of the classics — are flagged rather than resolved, and later
developments (yin-yang cosmology, philosophical Daoism, the Pangu/Nüwa myths) are
carefully *not* read back into the Bronze Age. Written with an educational emphasis
on how the evidence works (oracle bones as primary documents; the idealizing bias of
the transmitted classics). Confucius, Laozi, and the philosophical flowering are
reserved for the Axial Age chapter in the next era.
