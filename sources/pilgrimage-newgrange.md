# Sources: the Pilgrimage, Newgrange

Model: `docs/pilgrimage/sites/newgrange/` (status **preview**: built, noindex, off the menu). Sizes are in
`dims.js`, each marked SURVEY (a published figure) or APPROX (our estimate); the place of each kerbstone and
Great Circle stone is in `layout.js`; the carved panels are drawn in `art.js`; the info-point texts are in
`site.js`. Logged 2026-10-18.

**How the facts were checked:** the pages below marked *opened* were read in full from this session. Pages marked
*search only* were confirmed only through search results that quote or summarise them, and are to be re-read at
review. M. J. O'Kelly's excavation report, *Newgrange: Archaeology, Art and Legend* (1982), and its plans could
not be opened, so the chamber's plan, the passage's width and the place of every stone are APPROX.

Wikipedia ("Newgrange") is used as a labelled pointer only, for six facts the opened sources do not give
(listed under "From Wikipedia only" below). It is 1 source in 14 (about 7%), under the 20% limit.

## Sources
1. World Heritage Ireland, "Newgrange", https://www.worldheritageireland.ie/bru-na-boinne/built-heritage/newgrange/
   (*opened*): about 80 m across; kerb of 97 stones; passage "just less than 19 m"; cross-shaped chamber with three
   recesses; corbelled roof with its capstone about 6 m above the floor, still waterproof; built about 3200 BC;
   white quartz from Wicklow; Kerbstones 1 and 52 among the finest Neolithic art; remains of at least five people,
   mostly cremated, with the basins; the Great Circle.
2. UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Brú na Bóinne – Archaeological Ensemble of the Bend of the Boyne", site 659,
   https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/659 (*opened*): inscribed 1993; Newgrange, Knowth and Dowth; about 40 satellite
   passage graves; the largest concentration of megalithic art in Europe.
3. Heritage Ireland (Office of Public Works), "Brú na Bóinne Visitor Centre (Newgrange and Knowth)",
   https://heritageireland.ie/places-to-visit/bru-na-boinne-visitor-centre-newgrange-and-knowth/ (*opened*):
   entry by guided tour from the visitor centre only; the passage "low and narrow".
4. S. Weadick, "The Winter solstice at Newgrange", National Museum of Ireland,
   https://museum.ie/en-IE/Collections-Research/Irish-Antiquities-Division-Collections/Irish-Antiquities-Articles/The-Winter-solstice-at-Newgrange
   (*opened*): excavation 1962–75 by M. J. O'Kelly for the Office of Public Works; first modern sighting of the

5. WorldHistory.org, "Newgrange", https://www.worldhistory.org/Newgrange/ (*opened*): 76 m across, 12 m high,

6. E. Burke-Kennedy, "Shedding light on winter solstice at Newgrange", *The Irish Times*, 19 December 2013,

## From Wikipedia only (to be checked against O'Kelly 1982 or another primary source at review)
- Kerbstone 1 about 3 m long, 1.2 m high, about 5 tonnes (the card gives length and height only).
- The passage lined with 43 uprights, 22 west and 21 east (the model uses the count; the card says only "upright
  slabs").
- The Great Circle: 12 surviving stones of perhaps 38; usually dated to the Early Bronze Age, the date debated.
- 1699: Charles Campbell's workmen found the entrance; Edward Lhwyd described it. Roman coins of about 320–337 CE
  and gold jewellery, now in the National Museum of Ireland and the British Museum.
- The 2016 claim (Gibbons) that the roof-box was fabricated, and the reply (O'Brien and Lynch) citing photographs
  from the early 1900s. The card names neither writer.
- The solstice lottery (the card does not give the number chosen).

## Where sources differ
- **How far the beam reaches today.** Ray (1989, and quoted in *The Irish Times*, 2013) says the beam is now 1–2 m
  shorter than when built and barely reaches the back of the chamber; the National Museum of Ireland's article and
  the archive's chapter ch42 ("floods the burial chamber") describe it reaching the triple spiral or the chamber
  today. The card "Where the light falls" reports both and follows Ray in the model. **Possible correction note for
  ch42, for Carter.**
- **Size of the mound:** 76 m (WorldHistory.org), about 80 m (World Heritage Ireland), 85 m (Wikipedia). The card
  gives about 80 m and the range.

## Figures used in the model (dims.js)
- SURVEY: radius 40 m (about 80 m across); height 12 m; 97 kerbstones; Kerbstone 1's length and height; passage
  18.8 m ("just less than 19 m"); floor rise 2 m; capstone 6 m above the chamber floor.
- APPROX: passage width (1.0 m) and height (2.05 m, kept higher than the real one so the visitor can walk upright,
  and said so on the card); the roof-box's size and height; the chamber's plan and the recesses' sizes; the run of
  the quartz wall (±42° either side of the entrance); the Great Circle's radius and every stone's place; the
  ground and the hill on the south-eastern skyline; the width of the solstice beam.

## Not used (could not be confirmed)
- A figure for the beam's width at the chamber; the exact sun bearing (about 136°) and the date of the earliest
  observation before 1967 (local tradition): left out.
- Which stone in the back recess carries the triple spiral's face, and where on it: the panel sits on the back
  wall, and the card says "on a stone of the back recess".
- George Eogan's own alternative for how the quartz lay: the card says only "in some other way".
- Hensey and Twohig (2017), cited on Wikipedia as supporting O'Kelly's facade: the paper was not found, so it is
  not cited.
