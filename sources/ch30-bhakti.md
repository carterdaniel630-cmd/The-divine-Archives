# Sources — Chapter 30: Bhakti

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. The Bhakti movement is well attested through its poetry, but the lives of its saints are largely devotional hagiography; the chapter keeps the movement and its verse (documented) distinct from the saints' miracle-biographies (legend) and from firm dating (often uncertain).

## Sources cited

*The movement, its philosophy, and its two streams*
- **Bhakti as a devotional movement (c. 6th–17th c.) democratizing religion across caste and gender; saguna (with form) vs. nirguna (formless) devotion; spread from the Tamil south northward** — "Bhakti Movement," PW/UPSC overview (https://www.pw.live/upsc/exams/bhakti-movement); Fiveable, "Bhakti Movement: Saints and Devotion."
- **Ramanuja and Vishishtadvaita (qualified non-dualism); salvation open to shudras and outcastes by surrender; Madhva's dualism** — "Bhakti Movement" overviews (theIAShub; Anantam IAS). https://theiashub.com/upsc/bhakti-movement/

*The poet-saints*
- **Alvars (twelve devotees of Vishnu, incl. the woman saint Andal; Nalayira Divya Prabandham) and Nayanars (sixty-three devotees of Shiva; Tevaram); Tamil vernacular; challenge to caste and Brahmin privilege** — "Alvars," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvars
- **Basava and the Lingayat/Virashaiva vachana tradition (Karnataka); Akka Mahadevi; Jnaneshwar (Jnaneshwari) and Namdev (abhangs, Vithoba of Pandharpur, Varkari); Kabir, Ravidas, and the northern sants** — "A Complete List of Saints and Teachers of the Bhakti Movement," IAS Gyan. https://www.iasgyan.in/blogs/a-complete-list-of-saints-and-teachers-of-bhakti-movement

*Practice, language, and the Sufi meeting*
- **Bhajan-kirtan, nama-japa, the rejection of priest/temple/ritual intermediary; Bhakti as the single most powerful force in shaping the modern Indian languages (Tulsidas→Awadhi, Surdas→Braj, Tukaram→Marathi, etc.)** — "The Bhakti Movement: Devotion, Vernacular Culture, and Social Critique," SocialStudiesHelp. https://socialstudieshelp.com/world-history/the-bhakti-movement-devotion-vernacular-culture-and-social-critique/

## Notes on handling
- The **movement and its poetry** are treated as well-documented; the **saints' biographies** (Kabir's body turning to flowers, Mirabai's poison-to-nectar, etc.) are flagged as **hagiography, not history**, and firm dates as often uncertain.
- The **anti-caste/anti-clerical message** is affirmed as plainly present in the verse; **how far it actually reformed society** (vs. being spiritually radical but socially absorbed) is left **open** as a live scholarly debate.
- The layered, partly-later authorship of corpora like "**Kabir's**" is noted; whether Kabir is Hindu, Muslim, or beyond both is left open, as he himself insisted.
- **nama-japa** is presented as the Indian devotional cousin of Sufi dhikr, tantric mantra, the Kabbalistic divine names, and Shinto kotodama — cross-tradition rhymes within the same age.
- Later blooms (Mirabai, Surdas, Tulsidas, Chaitanya; and the modern global export of Krishna-bhakti) are noted as belonging to later eras.
