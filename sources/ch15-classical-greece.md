*Note on sourcing:* Researched from standard reference scholarship (Stanford Encyclopedia
of Philosophy, Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Britannica) rather than memory alone,
per the project standard. The chapter keeps the **well-attested** philosophy (the figures,
texts, and their reconceptions of the divine) separate from the **legendary** (especially
the biography of Pythagoras), corrects the caricature of the philosophers as simple
atheists, flags the genuinely contested questions (the Socratic problem; the literal or
mythic reading of the *Timaeus*; the reach of Orphic-Pythagorean salvation), and cross-
references the early Greek religion and mysteries of Chapter 8. Written to be educational,
tracing how the Greek turn from *mythos* to *logos* reshaped the very idea of God for the
traditions that followed.
