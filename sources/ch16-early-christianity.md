*Note on sourcing:* Researched from standard reference and scholarly sources (Britannica, Wikipedia's
sourced overviews, university biblical-studies resources, and the mainstream historical-critical
scholarship they summarize) rather than memory alone, per the project standard. Following the archive's
evidence-honesty rule and the faith-and-history note above, the chapter keeps the **historically
attested** (Jesus's existence and crucifixion; the early proclamation of the resurrection; Paul; the
canon's formation; persecution and Constantine) rigorously separate from the **claims of faith** (the
resurrection, incarnation, and miracles as sacred belief, neither asserted as proven nor dismissed),
foregrounds the **diversity** of early Christianities, reserves the **Gnostic** movements and the **Nag
Hammadi** texts for the next chapter, and flags the genuinely open questions (the historical Jesus, the
dating of texts, the parting of the ways). Written to be educational, and to respect both the tradition
and the historical record without confusing the two.
