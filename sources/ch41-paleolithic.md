# Sources — Chapter 41: The Paleolithic

Per the project sourcing standard. This is the era of maximum uncertainty; the chapter foregrounds that we possess the artifacts but not the meaning, and treats every interpretation (shamanism, "goddess," hunting magic) as hypothesis, not fact.

## Sources cited
- **Cave art (Chauvet, Lascaux), therianthropes, and the shamanism reading (Lewis-Williams; Breuil's earlier "sorcerer" idea)** — MDPI, "Art and Shamanism"; ArtsLookup, "Shamans in Paleolithic Art." https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/10/1/54
- **The Lion Man of Hohlenstein-Stadel (c. 40,000 years old)** — "Lion-man," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion-man
- **Venus figurines and the contested fertility/goddess interpretation** — "Venus figurines," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_figurines
- **Deliberate burial and grave-goods (Sungir); Neanderthal burial debate (Shanidar)** — Brewminate, "Shamans and Ecstasy." https://brewminate.com/shamans-and-ecstasy-an-exploration-of-religious-beliefs-and-practices-in-the-prehistoric-world/
- **Red ochre and engraved Blombos ochre (c. 73,000 years) as earliest abstract mark-making** — "Blombos Cave," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blombos_Cave
- **The interpretive limits of prehistoric-art readings** — Albert.io, "Theories and Interpretations of Prehistoric Art." https://www.albert.io/blog/theories-and-interpretations-of-prehistoric-art-ap-art-history-review/

## Notes
- Not supported as fact: the meaning of the art/figurines; the "Mother Goddess" of the Venuses; specific shamanic readings (plausible, not proven); whether Neanderthals had religion.
- The therianthrope, hand-print, red ochre, and resonant cave are treated as the earliest encoded/symbolic behavior.
- Written last (era I), it roots the whole archive (ancestor-cult, shamanism, animal-headed gods).
