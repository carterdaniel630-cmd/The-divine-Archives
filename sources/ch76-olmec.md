## Notes
- Well-supported: sites and dates; colossal heads and thrones; La Venta offerings; El Manatí deposits (oldest rubber balls); Chalcatzingo; composite imagery; cacao at San Lorenzo; spread of Olmec style.
- Not supported: African/Chinese/Atlantean/extraterrestrial origins; unprovenanced objects as secure evidence; knowledge of Olmec god names or myths.
- Open: mother vs sister culture; Joralemon's gods; shaman reading of transformation figures; infant sacrifice at El Manatí; Cascajal block; the c. 400 BCE decline.
- Placed in era 02 (Bronze Age) by date.

## Wikipedia-replacement pass (2026-10-04)
All twelve Wikipedia citations were replaced with the excavation reports, journal articles and museum or university sources behind the same claims; no sentence of the chapter was changed. Most publisher and journal sites could not be opened from the build sandbox, so each replacement was confirmed via search results (author, title, venue, year and URL) and is to be re-read at review.
- Olmec overview → Diehl 2004 and Pool 2007 (Britannica and the *Cambridge World Prehistory* chapter were already cited).
- Olmec religion → Taube, *Olmec Art at Dumbarton Oaks* (2004).
- La Venta, Complex A, the Massive Offerings and Offering 4 → Drucker, Heizer and Squier 1959, the excavation report (it also replaces the "Olmec figurine" article for Offering 4).
- El Manatí and the rubber balls → Trafficking Culture case study and Hosler, Burkett and Tarkanian, *Science* 1999. The excavators' own report (Ortiz and Rodríguez) could not be confirmed by exact title, so it was not added.
- Ballgame and Paso de la Amada → Hill, Blake and Clark, *Nature* 1998, which reports the court at about 1400 BC in uncalibrated radiocarbon years; the chapter's "about 1650 BCE" is the calibrated figure also given in the 2020 *Science Advances* paper already cited. These figures agree, so this is not a flag.
- Las Limas → Metropolitan Museum, "Olmec Babies".
- Chalcatzingo → Grove (ed.) 1987 and Grove's FAMSI report.
- Cascajal block → Rodríguez Martínez et al., *Science* 2006, and the critical letter of Bruhns and Kelker, *Science* 2007.
- Alternative-origin claims → Ortiz de Montellano, Haslip-Viera and Barbour, *Ethnohistory* 1997, and Van Sertima's own book (1976).

No stronger source found disagreed with the chapter's text.
