# Sources — Chapter 42: The Neolithic

Per the project sourcing standard. A religion readable only in architecture; the chapter keeps the well-dated sites distinct from contested interpretation (the Mother Goddess, "temple before the city").

## Sources cited
- **Göbekli Tepe (c. 9600 BCE): T-pillars, animal reliefs, pre-agricultural ceremonial center; Schmidt's "temple first" hypothesis** — Biblical Archaeology Society, "The Göbekli Tepe Ruins and the Origins of Neolithic Religion." https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/ancient-cultures/ancient-near-eastern-world/the-gobekli-tepe-ruins-and-the-origins-of-neolithic-religion/
- **Çatalhöyük (c. 7400–6000 BCE): under-floor burial, bucrania, plastered skulls, the goddess debate** — bioRxiv, "Female lineages and changing kinship patterns in Neolithic Çatalhöyük." https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.23.600259
- **The Mother Goddess hypothesis (Gimbutas) and its scholarly critique** — "Mother goddess," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_goddess
- **Megaliths and astronomical alignments (Stonehenge, Newgrange solstice; Ġgantija)** — "Megalith," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith
- **The Neolithic revolution and the origins of monumental religion** — Tepe Telegrams (German Archaeological Institute), "Religion at Göbekli Tepe." https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/tag/religion/

## Notes
- Not supported as fact: the universal "Mother Goddess"/matriarchy (largely rejected); "temple before the city" (influential but debated); the specific meanings of pillars/figurines/spirals.
- Solstice alignments are real and measurable; the theology behind the monuments is a silence filled honestly with question marks.
- Connects the Paleolithic to the Bronze Age (ziggurats/pyramids) and to the modern Goddess movement (the contested hypothesis).
