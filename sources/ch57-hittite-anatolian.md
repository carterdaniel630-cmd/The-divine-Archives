# Sources — Chapter 57: Hittite & Anatolian Religion

Per the project sourcing standard. A tradition chapter drawing on an unusually rich Bronze Age textual archive: the emphasis is on the well-documented pantheon, myths, and rites, marking the Greek-myth transmission and the treaty/covenant parallel as real but debated, and the biblical "Hittites" name-overlap as uncertain.

## Notes
- Well-supported: the empire and Hattusa; the recovered archive and decipherment; the layered pantheon and chief deities; the Kumarbi Cycle and Telipinu myth as texts; the king's priestly role; Yazılıkaya; the Hittite–Egyptian treaty and Mursili's plague prayers.
- Real but debated (flagged): the direction/channel of the Kumarbi -> Hesiod transmission; whether the Hittite-treaty / biblical-covenant resemblance reflects direct influence, shared convention, or later development.
- Uncertain (flagged): the "Hittites" of the Hebrew Bible are often a Canaanite group whose relation to the Anatolian empire is unclear — the shared name is not simple identity.
- Genuinely open: the full Hattian substrate; the precise mechanics of transmission to Greece; the inner meaning of many rituals.
