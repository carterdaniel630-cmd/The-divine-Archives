# Sources — Vault V68: The Tjurunga of Central Australia

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the entry's specific claims. It is derived from the Sources section published with the entry. Contested points are flagged as contested in the entry text.

**Drafting note (2026-09-27, batch 8).** Facts were checked by web search during drafting. The drafting environment could not open most holding institutions' sites directly because its network policy blocked them; institutional URLs should be re-checked in review. No institutional photographs are reproduced, and for secret-sacred or devotional objects (the tjurunga, the Báb) no image of any kind is shown. The interactive objects present documented facts and primary text, in public-domain originals or this archive's own working renderings; schematic objects are labelled as such.

## 2026-10-24 — entry withdrawn (Carter's ruling 2026-10-20)

The Vault exhibit was taken down and `vault/tjurunga.html` replaced by an absence page, per
`handoff/tjurunga-takedown-order-2026-10-20.md` (wording verbatim from §3). The absence page keeps the
online sources above and Spencer & Gillen (1899) and the 2018 *Museum Management and Curatorship* article;
Durkheim (1912) is no longer cited there because the trimmed text no longer discusses his use of the
material. No new claims were added and no new sources were needed.

## 2026-10-31 — Wikipedia link replaced with an official source (Carter's ruling 2026-10-26)

Per `handoff/decisions-2026-10-26-absence-page-sources.md`, the absence page cites official (museum, government and
academic) sources only, none leading toward sale or auction descriptions. The Wikipedia "Tjurunga" link is removed.
In its place: AIATSIS, "Arrernte returns" (opened 2026-10-31), the national institute's record of restricted
Arrernte ceremonial objects returned from the Illinois State Museum and Manchester Museum (2019) and the Kluge-Ruhe
collection (celebrated 2021). It supports the page's sentence that museums overseas are returning secret-sacred
objects; the page's other claims rest on the Central Land Council, Northern Territory Government, Spencer & Gillen
project and 2018 *Museum Management and Curatorship* sources already cited. The AIATSIS page has no sale or auction
links. Page text unchanged; no image of any kind added.
