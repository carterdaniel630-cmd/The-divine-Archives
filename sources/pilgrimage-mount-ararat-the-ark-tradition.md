# Sources: the Pilgrimage, Mount Ararat & the Ark tradition

Model: `docs/pilgrimage/sites/mount-ararat-the-ark-tradition/` (status **preview**). Label (C): a claimed or
traditional site. Logged 2026-10-11.

**How the facts were checked:** publisher pages could not be opened from the build environment; each fact was
confirmed through search results quoting or summarising the source, to be re-read at review.

**The model:** the mountain is a scale model (every distance and height divided by 50) set so that it fills the right
angle of view from each place. Its heights are published; its shape (cones, gullies, the Ahora Gorge, the snowline)
is our own height field, not a copy of a survey or elevation model. The places walked (the plain, the formation, the
summit dome) are true size; the formation's width and height and every map position are estimates (`dims.js`).

## Facts on the cards
- Greater Ararat 5,137 m; ice cover 7.28 km² (2014 field campaign and satellite imagery): R. S. Azzoni et al.,
  "Geomorphology of Mount Ararat/Ağrı Dağı (Ağrı Dağı Milli Parkı, Eastern Anatolia, Turkey)", *Journal of Maps* 13.2
  (2017), 182–190, doi:10.1080/17445647.2017.1279084. Lesser Ararat 3,896 m: summaries agreeing with it. Massif
  35–40 km across: *Britannica* gives about 40 km, other summaries 35 km, so the card says "35 to 40 km" (2026-10-18).
- The ice cap and four outlet glaciers 7.37 km², over 60% of Turkey's glacier area; a loss of 2.99 km² (29%)
  1990–2016: R. S. Azzoni, A. Sarıkaya and D. Fugazza, "Turkish glacier inventory and classification from
  high-resolution satellite data" (2020).
- The 1840 eruption and earthquake, the Ahora Gorge flows, Akori destroyed: MTA (General Directorate of Mineral
  Research and Exploration), Turkish volcanoes, "Ağrı", https://www.mta.gov.tr/turkvolc/en/agri ; R. S. Azzoni,
  D. Fugazza, M. Garzonio, K. Nicoll, G. Diolaiuti, M. Pelfini and A. Zerboni, "Geomorphological effects of the
  1840 Ahora Gorge catastrophe on Mount Ararat (Eastern Turkey)", *Geomorphology* 332 (2019), 10–21 (University of
  Milan repository, https://air.unimi.it/handle/2434/625867), as summarised in search results: it calls the old
  accounts "obscure and ambiguous" and argues for an earthquake-triggered eruption and a lahar; others read the
  deposit as a landslide. Whether there was an eruption is therefore shown as disputed (review of 2026-10-18).
- "The mountains of Ararat" (Genesis 8:4) and Urartu; the identification with this peak comparatively recent;
  early Armenian tradition (to the 10th century) and Islamic tradition placing the landing on Judi: *Encyclopaedia
  Iranica*, "Ararat"; Britannica (student edition), "Mount Ararat"; 1911 *Encyclopædia Britannica*, "Ararat";
  Livius.org, "Ağrı Dağı (the so-called Ararat)".
- Qur'an 11:44 (al-Jūdī); Gilgamesh Tablet XI (Mount Nimush): as in the archive's Flood chapter (ch01).
- Turkey since the early 1920s (Treaties of Moscow and Kars, 1921): Wikipedia pointer, "Mount Ararat"; no stronger
  source opened.
- First recorded ascent, October 1829, Friedrich Parrot and Khachatur Abovian with two soldiers and two Armenian
  villagers: *The Armenian Mirror-Spectator*, "Ararat Redux: Abovian, Prof. Parrot and First Ascent".
- The Durupınar formation (about 157–164 m long; exposed 1948 as usually told; aerial photographs 1959; dug in 1960,
  soil and rock only): summaries of the site; the card says "usually said". Its geology (a doubly plunging syncline;
  limonite and magnetite layers; the "bark" altered peridotite; Fasold's change of view): L. G. Collins and D. F.
  Fasold, "Bogus Noah's Ark from Turkey Exposed as a Common Geologic Structure", *Journal of Geoscience Education* 44.4
  (1996), 439–444, https://www.csun.edu/~vcgeo005/bogus.html . Recent "scan" claims not peer reviewed: press reports
  of 2025–26.
- Navarra's 1955 wood dated to about 720–790 CE (several laboratories): TalkOrigins Archive, claim CH504.2;
  *Bible and Spade*, "Ararat 'Ark' Wood Dated at A.D. 700".
- Noah's Ark Ministries International, 2010, and the hoax allegation by a former team member: UPI, "Noah's ark
  claim doubted" (28 April 2010); *Christian Science Monitor*, "Doubt cast on Noah's ark found in Turkey" (2010).
- Genesis 6:15 (300 × 50 × 30 cubits); the cubit taken as about 0.5 m (estimates 0.45–0.52 m).

## Added 2026-10-25: the card "The ark in Armenian tradition" (the believer's side, at Carter's go-ahead)

- Saint Jacob of Nisibis (Hakob of Mtsbin), 4th-century bishop; the ark relic at Etchmiadzin; the angel and the
  dream tellings: Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin, liturgical calendar (armenianchurch.org/en/Liturgical-Calendar);
  Armenian Church of Sydney calendar entries (armenianchurchsydney.org.au); *The Christian Century*
  (christiancentury.org, node 45162), which also notes the reliquary and that scholars debate the history.
- Movses Khorenatsi's descent of the Armenians from Noah through Japheth and Haik; the mountain on Armenia's coat
  of arms: WorldHistory.org, "Mount Ararat" (worldhistory.org/Mount_Ararat/). The ark resting on its summit in the
  arms: *The Christian Century* (above). Jacob fell asleep at the foot of the mountain, as the church's calendar
  tells it (wording corrected at review, 2026-10-25). The card gives no date
  for Movses, whose dating is itself debated.
