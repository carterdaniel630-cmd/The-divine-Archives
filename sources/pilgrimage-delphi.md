# Sources: the Pilgrimage, Delphi

Model: `docs/pilgrimage/sites/delphi/` (status **preview**: built, noindex, off the menu). Sizes are in `dims.js`,
each marked SURVEY (a published figure) or APPROX; the info-point texts are in `site.js`; the columns, roofs,
hillside and painted textures are drawn in `greek.js`. The moment shown is the sanctuary as Pausanias described it,
about 170 CE. Logged 2026-10-11.

**How the facts were checked:** each fact below was confirmed by opening the page named, or through search results
that quote the source, and is to be re-read against the source at review. No measured plan of the sanctuary could
be opened, so **where each building stands, the line of the Sacred Way (straightened into four runs) and the height
of every terrace are estimates**. The statues are plain stand-ins, and the lettering on the Polygonal Wall, the
Athenian Treasury and the porch imitates the look of inscriptions, with three exceptions that are real wording: the
two maxims; the hymn panel, the opening words of the first Delphic Hymn as edited (below); and the line repeated on the
Polygonal Wall, which follows the manumission formula of one edited record, CID V 534 (153/2–144/3 BCE; Attic
Inscriptions Online, https://atticinscriptions.com/inscription/CIDV/534?text_type=greek: "ἀπέδοτο … τῶι Ἀπόλλωνι τῶι
Πυθίωι σῶμα γυναικεῖον … ἐφ' ὧιτε ἐλευθέρα εἶμεν"), with the names left out, so it is not a transcription of the whole
record. Its earlier ending "ΕΠ ΕΛΕΥΘΕΡΙΑΙ" was not found in any edited record and was replaced (late fix run 2026-10-25).

## Ancient texts
- Pausanias, *Description of Greece* book 10, read at https://www.theoi.com/Text/Pausanias10A.html and
  https://dmdhist.sitehost.iu.edu/Pausaniasdelphi.htm : 10.5.9–13 (the legendary temples), 10.7.1 (Nero took five
  hundred bronze statues), 10.7.2–8 (the Pythian Games), 10.9–10.10 (offerings along the lower way), 10.11.2
  (Siphnian Treasury), 10.11.5 (Athenian Treasury from the spoils of Marathon), 10.11.6 (Stoa of the Athenians),
  10.12.1 (the Rock of the Sibyl, Herophile), 10.13.9 (the Phocians took the Plataean tripod's gold, the bronze
  remained), 10.16.3 (the omphalos "the centre of all the earth"), 10.24.1 (maxims in the fore-temple), 10.24.4–5
  (the altar of Poseidon, the Fates, the hearth, Pindar's chair), 10.32.1 (stadium).
- Herodotus 2.180 and 5.62 (rebuilding of the temple after 548 BCE; the Alcmaeonids), 3.57 (Siphnian wealth), 9.81
  (the Plataean tripod). Thucydides 1.132 (Pausanias the Spartan's inscription erased).
- Plutarch, *On the E at Delphi* (the E; 389c, Dionysus in the winter months); *On the Obsolescence of Oracles*
  (the vapour; the trembling goat); *On the Pythia's Oracles*; *Greek Questions* 9 (monthly consultation; that it fell on the seventh is a modern inference, per the review of 2026-10-12).
- Plato, *Protagoras* 343a–b (the Seven Sages and the maxims); *Charmides* 165a (the third maxim).

## Notes for review
- **ch08 error:** the chapter on early Greece says the omphalos was "inscribed with the maxims *know thyself* and
  *nothing in excess*". Pausanias (10.24.1) puts the maxims in the porch of the temple. The "Know thyself" card
  says so; ch08 needs a correction.
- The plans of the hall and the inner room are not known; the model's are illustrative, and the cards say so.
- Not yet confirmed from a source: the height of the temple's columns (APPROX after comparable Doric temples) and the
  ramp at its east front (shown on published plans, not measured here).
- **Review fixes (2026-10-12):** the hall card no longer says the hearth fire was fed with pine wood (Pausanias names the hearth, not its fuel); the vapours card names Etiope and Papatheodorou together rather than saying who led the 2006 team; the Athenian Treasury's disputed date (about 510–480 BCE) now cites the Museum of Classical Archaeology, Cambridge, https://museum.classics.cam.ac.uk/node/3539 (opened).
