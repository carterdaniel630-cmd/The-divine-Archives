## Re-sourcing log — 2026-11-17 (Wikipedia strip flags 101–105)
The 2026-11-15 strip (part 9) removed the Wikipedia pointers for Gardner, Dianic Wicca / *The Spiral Dance*, Ásatrúarfélagið, the VA emblems and Modern Paganism, leaving flags 101–105 without a source. No sentence of the chapter was changed. Sources read this day:
- **Flag 101 (priority), the New Forest coven claim** — R. Hutton, "Modern Pagan Witchcraft," Gresham College lecture, 14 May 2025, transcript PDF. Read: Gardner claimed in 1951 that the pagan witch religion had survived and that "in 1939 he had been initiated into one of the last covens of it, in the New Forest"; "No confirmation has been found to date of the existence of the coven into which Gardner claimed to be initiated, and so thus far he is still the sole known source of his religion"; "It seems most likely that he and his friends developed it." The lecture does not discuss *Witchcraft Today*, Murray or Valiente.
- **Flag 101, *Witchcraft Today* (1954) and Murray; also flags 102 and 105 in part** — H. A. Berger, "Wicca," World Religions and Spirituality Project (wrldrels.org; the site's own WRSP page is wrldrels.org/?p=2636), 5 April 2013. Read: Gardner "contended that he was initiated into the New Forest Coven, by Dorothy Clutterbuck in 1939"; *Witchcraft Today* was "the first non-fiction book on Wicca"; Murray "wrote the foreword to his book" and her witch-trial work is no longer accepted by historians; Valiente "wrote much of the poetry used in the rituals"; the eight sabbats; 1971 "The first feminist coven was formed in California by Zsuzsanna Budapest"; *The Spiral Dance* (1979); Starhawk "the founder and one of the leaders" of the Reclaiming Witches; 2007, the pentagram permitted on military graves.
- **Flag 101, corroboration** — M. Introvigne, review of J. Callow, *Gerald Gardner and the Creation of Wicca* (Cambridge University Press, Elements in Magic, 2025), *Bitter Winter*, 16 February 2026. Read: *Witchcraft Today* "with an introduction by Margaret Murray that added academic credibility"; of Gardner's claimed initiation, "Scholars remain skeptical". The Callow book itself was not read (its Cambridge abstract page's permission request was not answered; not fetched another way).
- **Flag 102** — "Budapest, Zsuzsanna E. (1940–)," *Encyclopedia of Occultism and Parapsychology*, via Encyclopedia.com (no editor or edition shown on the page). Read: "Founder of the main branch of Dianic (feminist) Wicca"; priestess of "the Susan B. Anthony Coven, in Venice, California".
- **Flag 103** — "Ásatrú" timeline, World Religions and Spirituality Project, 5 August 2020 (no author shown on the page read). Read: "Late 1960s: A group led by poet Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson began forming Ásatrúarfélagið"; "1972-1973: The Ministry of Religious Affairs granted Ásatrúarfélagið recognition as a state-supported religious organization"; Beinteinsson "elected to lead the Society as its first Allsherjargoði".
- **Flag 104** — ACLU press release, "Veterans Win Right to Post Religious Symbol on Headstones," 23 April 2007. Read: the VA "will add the Pentacle to its list of approved emblems of belief"; settlement of the Americans United suit, which also resolved the ACLU's parallel claims (*Egbert v. Nicholson*).
- **Flag 105, "Mabon" coined in the 1970s** — Religion News Service report on Mabon (no byline; publication year not shown; the text dates the festival "Thursday (Sept. 22)"). Read: "in 1974, American witch Aidan Kelly claims to have given the holiday its name." A news report of Kelly's own claim, not scholarship; labelled as such in the entry.
