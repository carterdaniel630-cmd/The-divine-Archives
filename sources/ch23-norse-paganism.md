# Sources — Chapter 23: Norse Paganism

Research was done by web search during drafting (not memory alone), per the project's sourcing standard. The chapter is unusually careful about source-criticism because Norse religion is reconstructed largely from Christian-era texts and from mute objects; contested points and the ancient-versus-modern distinction are flagged in the text.

## The textual record (Eddas, skaldic verse, sagas)
- **Poetic Edda; Codex Regius (c. 1270); poems older than the manuscript, copied from lost exemplars** — "Poetic Edda," New World Encyclopedia. https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Poetic_Edda
- **Prose Edda; Snorri Sturluson (c. 1220–1230); written as a Christian-age handbook to teach skaldic imagery** — "Prose Edda," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prose_Edda
- **Skaldic verse as the older check; kennings presuppose shared myth; imagery reaching to the ninth century** — "Edda," Encyclopædia Britannica. https://www.britannica.com/topic/Edda

## Cosmology and myth
- **Æsir and Vanir; the Nine Worlds; Yggdrasil at the center; no single canonical list of nine** — World History Encyclopedia, "Nine Realms of Norse Cosmology." https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1305/nine-realms-of-norse-cosmology/
- **Yggdrasil; Ragnarök; Líf and Lífþrasir sheltering in the tree; Völuspá stanza on the nine worlds** — "Yggdrasil," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yggdrasil

## Worship, sacrifice, and the Uppsala problem
- **Adam of Bremen (1070s), Gesta Hammaburgensis; the gilded Uppsala temple and nine-yearly sacrifice; the reliability debate (polemic vs. some archaeological support)** — "Temple at Uppsala," Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_at_Uppsala) and World History Encyclopedia, "Temple at Uppsala" (https://www.worldhistory.org/Temple_at_Uppsala/)
- **Human sacrifice as real but exceptional in the Norse/Germanic record** — National Museum of Denmark, "Human sacrifices?" https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/religion-magic-death-and-rituals/human-sacrifices/

## Magic: seiðr and the völva
- **Seiðr as trance-magic tied to Freyja (and Odin); practised chiefly by women; the staffs in rich female graves read as völur's wands; Sámi shamanic parallels** — National Museum of Denmark, "The magic staffs of the Viking seeresses." https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/religion-magic-death-and-rituals/the-magic-staffs-of-the-seeresses/

## Archaeology
- **Oseberg (dendro-dated 834) and Gokstad (c. 890); blue-clay preservation; grave goods; two women at Oseberg, one elderly with staff and pouch** — Britannica, "Oseberg ship" (https://www.britannica.com/topic/Oseberg-ship); "Dendrochronological dating of the Viking Age ship burials at Oseberg, Gokstad and Tune, Norway" (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291416516)
- **Gotland picture stones (400+); message in image not runes; pre-Christian motifs; Tjängvide and Ardre image stones (Sleipnir)** — "Picture stone," Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picture_stone); "Tjängvide image stone" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tj%C3%A4ngvide_image_stone)

## Symbology and sacred encoding
- **Elder Futhark (24 runes, from c. 2nd c. CE) and Younger Futhark (16 runes, from c. 8th c.); genuine magical/invocatory inscriptions (e.g. alu, curses) on weapons, amulets, bracteates; modern tile-drawing divination as a 20th-century revival** — "Runic magic," Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runic_magic); VikingRune, "Magic Runes" (https://www.vikingrune.com/2008/12/magic-runes/)
- **Bind runes (bandrún) attested on Viking-Age artifacts; many modern meanings are modern** — Nordlys Viking, "About Bind runes." https://nordlysviking.com/en-us/blogs/blog/about-bind-runes
- **Mjölnir amulets (hundreds; rising in the conversion era, worn against the cross); valknut on picture stones near death imagery, name and meaning modern/uncertain** — general runestone/amulet overviews; History Hit, "The Viking Rune Stones of Gotland." https://www.historyhit.com/the-viking-rune-stones-of-gotland/

## The end of the old religion
- **Christianization of Iceland (c. 1000); Þorgeir Þorkelsson, law-speaker and pagan goði; "one law and one religion"; private sacrifice tolerated; Olaf Tryggvason's pressure** — "Christianization of Iceland," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Iceland

## Notes on handling
- The chapter's core honesty move: our systematic mythology is largely a **thirteenth-century Christian reconstruction** (Snorri), checked where possible against **contemporary** skaldic verse, runestones, and archaeology.
- Ancient-versus-modern distinctions are drawn explicitly for runes (real ancient magical inscriptions vs. modern rune-tile divination) and bind runes/valknut (attested forms vs. modern meanings), per the project's symbology standard.
- Live debates left open: Christian influence on Völuspá's Ragnarök, the völva reading of the Oseberg staff, the real extent of human sacrifice, and the reliability of Adam of Bremen on Uppsala.
- Sámi religion, modern Ásatrú/Heathenry, and Icelandic early-modern folk-magic grimoires are noted as connections but reserved for fuller treatment elsewhere.
