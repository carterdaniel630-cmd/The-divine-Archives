# Sources — Chapter 10: Second Temple Judaism: The Many Judaisms

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. It is derived from the Sources section published with the chapter; contested points are flagged as contested in the chapter text, and faith claims are kept distinct from historically attested facts.

## Sources cited

*The period, the exile, and diversity*
- [Britannica: Jewish Diaspora](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Jewish-Diaspora);
  [Wikipedia: Second Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Temple);
  Shaye J. D. Cohen, *From the Maccabees to the Mishnah* (the standard survey).

*The sects and the Scrolls*
- [Jewish Virtual Library: Pharisees, Sadducees & Essenes](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/pharisees-sadducees-and-essenes);
  [Britannica: Dead Sea Scrolls](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dead-Sea-Scrolls);
  Josephus, *Jewish War* and *Antiquities*.

*Theology and the Zoroastrian question*
- On apocalyptic, angelology, and the (debated) Persian influence: the balanced
  discussions at [Bible Interp: Afterlife and Resurrection in the Second Temple Period](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/articles/afterlife-and-resurrection-beliefs-second-temple-period)
  and works such as Vicente Dobroruka on Iranian influence — with the caution that
  the case is one of resonance, not proven borrowing.

*The literature*
- [My Jewish Learning: Apocrypha and Pseudepigrapha](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/apocrypha-and-pseudepigrapha/);
  [Wikipedia: Jewish apocrypha and pseudepigrapha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_apocrypha_and_pseudepigrapha);
  on the canon, and Philo of Alexandria for the allegorical/cosmic readings.

*Note on sourcing:* Researched from standard scholarship (Cohen; the Dead Sea Scrolls
literature; Josephus and Philo as primary witnesses) rather than memory alone, per the
project standard. The most sensitive matter — the **Zoroastrian and Hellenistic
influence** on Second Temple theology — is deliberately reported as a real but
unresolved debate (resonance, not proven dependence), and the diversity of "Judaisms"
is kept front and center. Written to be educational (how the exile reshaped the
religion; why there was no single Bible yet). The birth of Christianity and the rise
of Rabbinic Judaism out of this matrix are reserved for the Late Antiquity chapters of
the next era.
