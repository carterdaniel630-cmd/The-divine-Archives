# Sources — Chapter 6: Zoroaster and the Iranian Reformation

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. It is derived from the Sources section published with the chapter; contested points are flagged as contested in the chapter text, and faith claims are kept distinct from historically attested facts.

## Sources cited

*Primary texts and translations*
- The **Gathas** and the **Avesta** — see [Encyclopaedia Iranica: "Gathas"](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/gathas-i-texts/)
  and the texts at [Avesta.org](https://www.avesta.org/); the Pahlavi **Bundahishn**
  and **Denkard** for later systematic cosmology.

*The prophet, dating, and doctrine*
- [Britannica: Zarathustra](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Zarathustra);
  [Encyclopaedia Iranica: "Zoroaster ii. General Survey"](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/zoroaster-ii-general-survey/)
  and ["Dualism"](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/dualism/).

*Eschatology and its influence*
- [Encyclopaedia Iranica: "Eschatology i. In Zoroastrianism and Zoroastrian
  Influence"](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/eschatology-i/);
  [World History Encyclopedia: Chinvat Bridge](https://www.worldhistory.org/Chinvat_Bridge/).
  On the debated influence on Judaism/Christianity/Islam, see the balanced
  discussions in the Iranica eschatology article and A. Akbar, "The Zoroastrian
  Provenance of Some Islamic Eschatological Doctrines" (2020).

*Practice and symbol*
- Fire temples and the sacred fire; the **dakhma**; the **Faravahar**
  ([Wikipedia: Faravahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faravahar), noting the
  fravashi vs. khvarenah debate) and **khvarenah**
  ([Britannica: hvarenah](https://www.britannica.com/topic/hvarenah)); the
  **sudreh** and **kusti** (72 threads = 72 Yasna chapters) and the **Navjote**
  initiation.

*Note on sourcing:* Researched from Encyclopaedia Iranica, standard reference works,
and primary-text translations rather than memory alone, per the project standard.
The genuinely contested matters — Zarathustra's date, monotheism vs. dualism, the
meaning of the Faravahar, and above all the direction and extent of influence on the
Abrahamic traditions — are flagged as live debates rather than resolved. Reviewer
note: the eschatological-influence question is deliberately left open here and
cross-referenced forward to the Second Temple Judaism and Christianity chapters,
where the receiving side of the exchange can be examined directly.
