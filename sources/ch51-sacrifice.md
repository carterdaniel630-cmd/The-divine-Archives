# Sources — Chapter 51: Sacrifice & the Scapegoat

Per the project sourcing standard. ## Notes
- Well-supported: near-universal sacrificial offering; the documented systems (Israelite, Greek, Vedic, etc.); the Leviticus 16 scapegoat and the Greek pharmakos; animal-for-human substitution as a textual pattern; Aztec human sacrifice (archaeologically attested); the post-70-CE and Christian transformations.
- Contested/exaggerated (flagged): Girard's and Burkert's universal-origin theories; the scale of Aztec sacrifice in colonial sources; whether the Carthaginian tophet evidences systematic child sacrifice.
- Genuinely open: why sacrifice is so universal (gift/communion/feeding/violence-discharge, or a braid); interpretation of ambiguous physical evidence (bog bodies, some infant burials).
