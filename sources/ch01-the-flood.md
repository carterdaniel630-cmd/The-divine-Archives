# Sources — Chapter 1: The Flood

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. It is derived from the Sources section published with the chapter; contested points are flagged as contested in the chapter text, and faith claims are kept distinct from historically attested facts.

## Sources cited

*Primary texts*
- *The Epic of Gilgamesh*, Tablet XI — Andrew George, trans., *The Epic of
  Gilgamesh* (Penguin Classics).
- *Atrahasis* — Old Babylonian flood epic (18th c. BCE). Overview:
  [Center for Online Judaic Studies](https://cojs.org/atrahasis_epic-_the_flood_story-_18th_century_bce/).
- *Eridu Genesis* / Ziusudra (Sumerian) —
  [Livius: The Great Flood, Sumerian version](https://www.livius.org/articles/misc/great-flood/flood2/);
  [Wikipedia: Eridu Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eridu_Genesis).
- Genesis 6–9 (Hebrew Bible).
- Ovid, *Metamorphoses* Bk. I (Deucalion); Plato, *Timaeus*. See
  [Wikipedia: Ancient Greek flood myths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_flood_myths).
- *Shatapatha Brahmana* (Manu and the fish);
  [Britannica: Manu](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Manu).

*Discovery and history of scholarship*
- George Smith, *The Chaldean Account of the Deluge* (1872) and *The Chaldean
  Account of Genesis* —
  [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/60559/60559-h/60559-h.htm);
  [Britannica: George Smith](https://www.britannica.com/biography/George-Smith-British-Assyriologist);
  [Linda Hall Library: Scientist of the Day](https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/george-smith/).

*Comparison and interpretation*
- [BioLogos: Gilgamesh, Atrahasis and the Flood](https://biologos.org/articles/gilgamesh-atrahasis-and-the-flood).
- [TheTorah.com: The Mesopotamian Origin of the Biblical Flood Story](https://www.thetorah.com/article/the-mesopotamian-origin-of-the-biblical-flood-story).
- [Big Think: Why are flood myths so common?](https://bigthink.com/high-culture/flood-myth-origin/).

*Archaeology and geology*
- Leonard Woolley's Ur flood layer and the cross-site correlation problem —
  [NCSE: The Flood — Mesopotamian Archaeological Evidence](https://ncse.ngo/flood-mesopotamian-archaeological-evidence);
  [Penn Museum, Expedition: Reflections on the Mesopotamian Flood](https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/reflections-on-the-mesopotamian-flood/).
- Black Sea deluge hypothesis (Ryan & Pitman 1997) and its disputed status —
  [Wikipedia: Black Sea deluge hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_deluge_hypothesis);
  [Science: Black Sea Flood Theory Put to Test](https://www.science.org/content/article/black-sea-flood-theory-put-test).

*Note on sourcing:* This draft relies on web research into primary translations
and current scholarship rather than memory alone, per the project standard.
Where the record is genuinely contested — the dating of the biblical borrowing,
the reality of the Black Sea event, the reach of deep diffusion — the draft
flags the dispute rather than resolving it. Reviewer: the George Smith
"undressing" anecdote is traditional (via Budge) and possibly embellished; it is
