# Sources — Chapter 50: The End of Days

Per the project sourcing standard. A comparative theme chapter: the emphasis is on documenting the apocalyptic worldview and the linear-vs-cyclical divide, while flagging the origin of Jewish/Christian/Islamic apocalyptic (Zoroastrian influence) as genuinely contested and endorsing no prophecy.

## Notes
- Well-supported: the texts and dates; the emergence of the apocalyptic worldview in the Second Temple period; the linear-vs-cyclical contrast; the recurrence of millenarian movements and the reinterpretation of failed prophecy.
- Contested (flagged, not resolved): whether Jewish (and thus Christian/Islamic) apocalyptic derives from Zoroastrianism — parallels are real, but direction/degree of influence and text-dating are debated; inner-Jewish and broader Near Eastern origins are also argued.
- No prophecy is treated as a prediction that will or did come true; the chapter documents expectation, not forecast.
- Genuinely open: the historical origin of the apocalyptic worldview; why the imagination divides between the line and the wheel.
