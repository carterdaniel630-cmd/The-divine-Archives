# Sources — Chapter 49: Sacred Kingship

Per the project sourcing standard. A comparative theme chapter: the emphasis is on documenting sacred kingship case by case while flagging Frazer's grand unifying (myth-and-ritual) thesis as rejected by modern scholarship even where individual cases are sound.

## Notes
- Well-supported: sacred kingship as a real, widespread phenomenon in two forms (king-as-god; king-as-Heaven's-conditional-appointee); the specific cases above; the documented substitute-king rite as hard evidence for a dying-king-type ritual.
- Not supported (as fact): Frazer's grand thesis that all religion began in a fertility cult around a periodically-killed sacred king; the myth-and-ritual school is disputed; sweeping claims that every sacred king was regularly ritually killed.
- Genuinely open: how literally/often dying-king rites were carried out (Shilluk debated); independent origin vs. mutual influence among the systems.
