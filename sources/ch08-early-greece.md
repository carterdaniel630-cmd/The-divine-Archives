# Sources — Chapter 8: Early Greece: The Gods Before the Philosophers

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. It is derived from the Sources section published with the chapter; contested points are flagged as contested in the chapter text, and faith claims are kept distinct from historically attested facts.

## Sources cited

*Mycenaean origins and the poets*
- On Linear B and the Mycenaean gods: [World History Encyclopedia: Linear B Script](https://www.worldhistory.org/Linear_B_Script/);
  [TheCollector: The Gods of the Myceneans](https://www.thecollector.com/mycenaean-gods/).
- Homer and Hesiod; the Homeric Question and oral tradition (Milman Parry and
  Albert Lord); Herodotus, *Histories* 2.53.

*Myth and Near Eastern influence*
- Hesiod, *Theogony* and *Works and Days*; on the succession myth and the Kumarbi
  cycle: [TheCollector: The Kumarbi Cycle](https://www.thecollector.com/kumarbi-cycle-hurrian-myth/);
  M. L. West, *The East Face of Helicon* (the standard study of Near Eastern
  elements in Greek poetry).

*Practice, oracles, and the afterlife*
- [World History Encyclopedia: Ancient Greek Religion](https://www.worldhistory.org/Greek_Religion/);
  Walter Burkert, *Greek Religion* (the standard scholarly handbook).
- [Wikipedia: Eleusinian Mysteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleusinian_Mysteries);
  on Orphism and the gold tablets, F. Graf &amp; S. I. Johnston, *Ritual Texts for
  the Afterlife*.

*The alphabet*
- [Wikipedia: History of the Greek alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Greek_alphabet)
  (the adoption of Phoenician letters and the innovation of vowels).

*Note on sourcing:* Researched from standard reference works and current scholarship
(Burkert, West) rather than memory alone, per the project standard. The live debates —
the Homeric Question, Mycenaean-to-Archaic continuity, the origins of individual gods,
and the secret of the Mysteries — are flagged rather than resolved. Written with an
educational emphasis on how Greek religion actually worked (orthopraxy, not creed) and
on how we know it (Linear B, comparative myth, oral-formulaic analysis). Classical
Greek philosophy, tragedy, and the full flowering of the mysteries are deliberately
reserved for the Axial Age chapter in the next era.
