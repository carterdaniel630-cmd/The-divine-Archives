# Sources — Chapter 61: Aboriginal Australian Religion (The Dreaming)

Per the project sourcing standard. A tradition chapter on a living, diverse, and partly-restricted set of traditions: the emphasis is on public, documented knowledge; the archive respects restricted sacred knowledge as a boundary (not a gap to fill), rejects appropriations, and flags "the Aboriginal religion" and "Dreamtime" as oversimplifications.

## Sources cited
- **The Dreaming/Dreamtime as the "everywhen," the Ancestral Beings and the shaping of the land, the Law (Tjukurpa/Altjira), and the 65,000-year continuity** — "Dreamtime," EBSCO Research Starters. https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/religion-and-philosophy/dreamtime ; "Australian Aboriginal mythology," EBSCO Research Starters. https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/religion-and-philosophy/australian-aboriginal-mythology/
- **Songlines: dreaming tracks as song, navigational map, and title-deed, crossing language boundaries** — "Songline," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songline
- **Totemism, connection to Country, sacred sites, and the diversity of distinct traditions** — "Australian Aboriginal Religions: History, Beliefs & Practices," Study.com. https://study.com/academy/lesson/australian-aboriginal-religions-history-beliefs-practices.html ; "Aboriginal Religion," AboriginalCulture.com.au. https://www.aboriginalculture.com.au/aboriginal-religion/
- **The Wandjina (Kimberley creator/rain beings; mouthless, storm-eyed), and the reclaiming of sacred images from appropriation (rejecting the "aliens" claim)** — "Wandjina," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandjina ; "What are Wandjinas?", Creative Spirits. https://www.creativespirits.info/aboriginalculture/arts/what-are-wandjinas
- **Rock art: the Gwion Gwion (Bradshaw) figures, Wandjina, and X-ray art as the oldest continuous painting tradition, including ritual repainting** — "Aboriginal Rock Art in Australia," Artark. https://artark.com.au/blogs/news/aboriginal-rock-art
- **Supporting detail corroborated via the above and adjacent chapters:** the Rainbow Serpent as a widespread creator-being; the tjurunga/churinga and the graded, restricted men's/women's knowledge; Durkheim's use of Aboriginal totemism in the sociology of religion; the 2020 Juukan Gorge destruction; desert concentric-circle iconography and dot-painting.

## Notes
- Well-supported: the >60,000-year antiquity/continuity (archaeologically established); the core concepts (Dreaming, songlines, totemism, Country, Ancestral Beings, Rainbow Serpent, Wandjina); ceremonial/oral transmission and restricted knowledge; the rock-art record and living repainting; the colonial catastrophe and Juukan Gorge (2020).
- Not supported / rejected: a single "Aboriginal religion" (there are many); a literal reading of "Dreamtime/Dreaming"; sensational appropriations (Wandjina-as-aliens; New Age repackaging).
- Deliberately closed: much deep content is restricted sacred knowledge, not to be published — the archive reports only public material and respects that boundary; the oldest rock art's dating/authorship is debated.
- Placement: era 01 (Prehistory), positioned as a living window on the deep past while insisting it is a present, living faith.
- Connects to ch41–ch42 (deep prehistory), ch46 (Rainbow Serpent/creation), ch50 (everywhen vs line/wheel), ch05/ch59 (oral transmission), ch62/ch63 (other living oral traditions).
