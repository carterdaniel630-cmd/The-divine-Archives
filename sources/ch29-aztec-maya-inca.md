# Sources — Chapter 29: Aztec, Maya & Inca

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. Because so much of what survives about these religions was recorded by the Spanish who destroyed them, the chapter is careful to keep archaeological reality distinct from conquest propaganda and from modern myth, and to flag what reaches us only through hostile, post-conquest transcription.

## Sources cited

*Maya*
- **Popol Vuh (K'iche', transcribed c. 1554), the Hero Twins and Xibalba, humans made of maize; gods (Itzamna, Chaac, Kukulkan); the hieroglyphic writing system and its decipherment; the Long Count** — Britannica, "Pre-Columbian civilizations: The Maya calendar and writing system." https://www.britannica.com/topic/pre-Columbian-civilizations/The-Maya-calendar-and-writing-system
- **Bloodletting rites, the Vision Serpent, and the "body debt"** — Classic Maya bloodletting studies; Maya-religion overviews. (See NCBI PMC4177853, "Classic Maya Bloodletting.")
- **The Long Count's 13th bak'tun (21 Dec 2012) as a cycle-turn, not a doomsday; the 2012 apocalypse as a modern, non-Maya invention** — National Geographic, "Maya Doomsday Calendar Explained"; Smithsonian NMAI, "Living Maya Time: The Meaning of 2012." https://maya.nmai.si.edu/2012-resetting-count/meaning-of-2012

*Aztec (Mexica)*
- **Huitzilopochtli and Tlaloc; the Templo Mayor; the Five Suns cyclical cosmology; sacrifice as repayment of a cosmic debt (the gods' self-sacrifice to move the sun)** — Britannica, "Aztec religion." https://www.britannica.com/topic/Aztec-religion
- **The Huei Tzompantli (great skull rack) excavated at the Templo Mayor; the scale debate — the Spanish claim of 80,400 in four days (1487) is logistically impossible and served conquest propaganda; archaeological reality is systematic sacrifice in the low thousands per year** — Science/AAAS, "Feeding the gods"; The Archaeologist, "Aztec Human Sacrifice: Separating Propaganda from Archaeological Fact." https://www.thearchaeologist.org/blog/aztec-human-sacrifice-separating-propaganda-from-archaeological-fact

*Inca*
- **Inti (sun, ancestor of the Sapa Inca), Viracocha (creator), Pachamama (earth mother); the Coricancha in Cusco; huacas (sacred places/objects); royal ancestor mummies; the quipu as a knotted-cord record system** — Britannica, "Inca religion." https://www.britannica.com/topic/Inca-religion
- **The capacocha ceremony — child sacrifice at high-altitude sites at key imperial events; the Llullaillaco mummies** — "Capacocha," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacocha

*The colonial source filter*
- **Diego de Landa's 1562 burning of the Maya codices (only four survive: Dresden, Madrid, Paris, Grolier), and the paradox that his own account became the key to decipherment** — "Diego de Landa," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_de_Landa

## Notes on handling
- Human sacrifice is presented as **archaeologically real** (Templo Mayor skull rack; Inca ice mummies) and described in its own cosmological logic (cosmic debt/maintenance), **neither sensationalized nor denied**.
- The **inflated Spanish sacrifice figures** and the **modern 2012 doomsday** are both flagged as **not supported** — the first conquest propaganda, the second a Western fabrication with no Maya basis.
- The chapter foregrounds the **source problem**: most of the record reaches us through the Spanish who destroyed these religions; much Maya myth is reconstructed from partial decipherment and iconography, and the quipu is largely unread.
- The three cultures are treated as **independent** (they never knew one another), with their striking parallels — divine kingship, cyclical time, temple-mountains, blood offering — noted as independent answers to shared religious questions, echoing the Bronze Age chapters.
