# Sources — Chapter 68: Nubia & Kush

Per the project sourcing standard. Kerma is known only from archaeology, Napata from Egyptian-language royal texts, Meroë from images and a script whose language is still largely unread; the chapter keeps those kinds of evidence apart and flags the contested claims (Qustul, race, Diodorus, the end of Meroë).

## Notes
- Well-supported: the kingdoms' sequence; Kerma deffufa and retainer burials; Jebel Barkal cult; the 25th Dynasty and Piye's stela; pyramids and horse burials; the Meroitic gods and temples; the Kandake queens; Philae's Nubian worship, the 394 CE graffito and Justinian's closure; Christian Nubia, the Baqt (652) and Faras.
- Not supported: Nubian origin of pharaonic kingship (Qustul); modern racial categories read into antiquity; decipherment of the Meroitic language; Diodorus's priests-order-the-king-to-die as routine fact; Aksum's destruction of Meroë as certain.
- Open: Kerma belief; Meroitic texts; origins of Apedemak; double uraeus meaning; the end of Meroë; the direction of religious influence between Egypt and Kush.
- Placed in era 03 (Early Iron Age), the Napatan heyday; the chapter also covers Kerma (Bronze Age) and Meroë and Christian Nubia for continuity.
- Connects to ch02, ch07, ch16, ch18, ch21, ch49, ch51, ch60 and Vault V66.

## Flags for Carter
- **Qustul date (minor, no change made).** The chapter says that "In 1986 the archaeologist Bruce Williams suggested that pharaonic kingship might have begun here." 1986 is the date of Williams's full excavation volume (OINE III, above), but the proposal was already public earlier: W. Y. Adams published his critique of it, "Doubts about the 'Lost Pharaohs'," in *JNES* 44 (1985). If you want a correction, "in the early 1980s" (or "in 1980", the date usually given for Williams's first article in *Archaeology* magazine, which I could not confirm directly here) would be more exact.
- **Faras paintings count (note only, no disagreement).** The Warsaw museum says the excavators found 169 wall paintings in the cathedral and took down about 120 (66 of them now in Warsaw). The chapter's "more than 120 wall paintings" is consistent with this.
- **Resolved 2026-10-05 (Carter):** the text now says Williams made the proposal in the early 1980s, with his full report in 1986.
- **Strabo link replaced (2026-10-05).** The earlier citation pointed to a copy of Strabo 17.1.54 on asor.org that could not be confirmed. It now points to the same passage (Candace, blind in one eye; Petronius's war and the sack of Napata) in two known editions: LacusCurtius's text of H. L. Jones's Loeb translation (Book XVII, ch. 1, §§ 25–54) and the Perseus Digital Library (Hamilton and Falconer translation, 17.1.54). Same claim; no chapter wording changed.
