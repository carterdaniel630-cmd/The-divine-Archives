## Museum rendition (visual pass, 2026-10-06)

The 3D rendition in the Virtual Museum (`docs/museum/relics.js`, `CAT.v53`) is modelled to:
- **Dimensions:** 112.3 cm (maximum height) × 75.7 cm × 28.4 cm, the British Museum's figures for EA 24, as reported in search results (the museum's own collection page, https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA24, could not be opened from the build environment; to be re-read at review).
- **Material:** grey and pink granodiorite.
- **Layout of the writing:** 14 lines of hieroglyphs (the top of the stone is lost), 32 lines of Demotic and 54 lines of Greek, as commonly reported (e.g. https://www.worldhistory.org/Rosetta_Stone/). The marks are illustrative, never the text.
- **Real text not used yet.** Carter's rule (2026-10-06): show the real text where a reliable published public-domain transcription exists, and cite it; never write or reconstruct it. The Greek text has such an edition, W. Dittenberger, *Orientis Graeci Inscriptiones Selectae* no. 90 (1903, public domain), but no copy could be reached from the build environment (Wikisource, archive.org, Perseus and PHI were blocked). The marks stay until a session can copy it exactly. The hieroglyphic and Demotic texts have no reliable public-domain digital transcription in Unicode, so they stay as marks.
- **Painted edges not shown.** White inscriptions were painted on the stone's sides after 1801. Published wordings differ ("Captured in Egypt by the British Army in 1801" / "…Army 1801"; "Presented by King George III"), and the British Museum's own record could not be opened, so they are left off rather than guessed.
- **Not matched to photographs here:** the holder's images could not be opened from the build environment. Shape and damage follow the published descriptions (broken top, rough back) and should be checked by eye at review.
