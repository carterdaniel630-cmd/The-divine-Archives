# Sources — Chapter 33: African Traditional Religion

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's claims. Because these traditions are largely oral and known through colonial/missionary/anthropological records, the chapter foregrounds diversity and the source problem, and rejects the old contemptuous labels.

## Notes on handling
- "African traditional religion" is treated as **hundreds** of distinct traditions with family resemblances, not one static system.
- The colonial labels "fetishism," "animism," "ancestor worship" are flagged as **distortions**; ancestors are honored/consulted, not worshipped as gods.
- Pre-colonial detail is often **uncertain** because the record is oral and mediated by outside, often hostile observers.
- The living **diaspora religions** (Santería, Candomblé, Vodou; later Rastafari) are noted here at their colonial-era formation and reserved for fuller treatment among the era-09 diaspora syntheses.
