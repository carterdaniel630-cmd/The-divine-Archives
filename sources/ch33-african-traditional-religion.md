## Re-sourcing log — 2026-11-17 (Wikipedia strip flag 89)
The 2026-11-15 strip (part 7) removed the Wikipedia pointers for the Dogon and Nommo, leaving flag 89 (the Dogon–Sirius controversy: Griaule/Dieterlen, Temple, van Beek) without a source. No sentence of the chapter was changed. Sources read this day:
- **B. R. Ortiz de Montellano, "The Dogon People Revisited," *Skeptical Inquirer*, November/December 1996, pp. 39–42** (PDF from the Center for Inquiry; volume number not shown on what was read, so not given). Read: Temple's book holds that Dogon knowledge came from "visitations five thousand or more years ago from inhabitants of the Sirius star system"; Griaule and Dieterlen "studied the Dogon from 1931 to 1952" and "make no claims about the antiquity of this information"; their research and Temple's book are "the sole source" of the claim; van Beek, eleven years among the Dogon, found "no Dogon outside of the circle of Griaule's informants had ever heard of sigui tolo or po tolo", and "no one … had ever heard or understood that Sirius was a double star"; Griaule "probably reinterpreted statements from his informant in the light of his own knowledge", and the Dogon "accepted his analysis as if it were theirs". Also reports Sagan's and Brecher's suggestion of earlier European contact (not added to the page).
- **W. E. A. van Beek, "Dogon Restudied: A Field Evaluation of the Work of Marcel Griaule," *Current Anthropology* 32(2), 1991, 139–167.** Citation from the Leiden University repository record (handle 1887/9039; no abstract there); abstract read on the eHRAF World Cultures record (ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu, culture FA16, document 031). Read: Griaule's *Dieu d'eau* and *Le renard pâle* could not be corroborated in the field; the Dogon interviewed do not attribute "nearly as much significance" to Nommo, the fox, or Sirius. The full article was not read.
