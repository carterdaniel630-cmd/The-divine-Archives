# Sources — Vault V88: The Skull of "Mary Magdalene" at Saint-Maximin

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the entry's specific claims. It is derived from the Sources section published with the entry. Contested points are flagged as contested in the entry text.

**Drafting note (2026-09-28).** Added at Carter's request, together with a modelled rendition of the reliquary in the museum.

- **How the facts were checked:** by web search while drafting. The drafting environment's network policy blocked direct access to Wikipedia, SAGE, PubMed Central and History Hit, so claims were confirmed from search excerpts of those sources and of the others listed. Page-level quotations should be re-checked in review.
- **Images:** no photographs of the relic are reproduced. The museum's model follows published descriptions (gilded hair, four angels, the date MDCCCLX) and is labelled as a rendition, not a replica.

## Sources cited

*The relic and its reliquary*

- P. Charlier, P. Froesch, N. Benmoussa, S. Morin, A. Augias, Y. Ubelmann, R. Weil, S. Morin, F. Straub, S. Deo, "Computer-Aided Facial Reconstruction of 'Mary-Magdalene' Relics Following Hair and Skull Analyses", *Clinical Medicine Insights: Ear, Nose and Throat* (2019), https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1179550618821933 · open copy https://hal.science/hal-02263987v1. The source for:
  - the photogrammetric and hair analyses;
  - the reliquary's four angels, golden hair and the MDCCCLX inscription.
- "Is This the Face of Mary Magdalene?", National Geographic, https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/mary-magdalene-face-skull-france-video-spd. The source for:
  - the photographs only (the skull was not removed from the reliquary);
  - the woman of about fifty;
  - identification resting on tradition.
- "The Relics of Saint Marie-Magdalene at La Sainte Baume", EWTN, https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/relics-of-saint-mariemagdalene-at-la-sainte-baume-1728. The source for:
  - the despoiling of the relics in 1794;
  - the sacristan Joseph Bastide saving the skull.
- "The Skull and Bones of Mary Magdalene", Atlas Obscura, https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/marys-house-in-provence
- "The Basilica of St. Maximin, Provence and Mary Magdalen's Relics", https://christinesunderland.com/2010/06/26/the-basilica-of-st-maximin-provence-and-mary-magdalens-relics/. The source for the *noli me tangere* tissue being placed in a glass reliquary in 1780.
- "Relics of Mary Magdalene", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relics_of_Mary_Magdalene. The source for:
  - the 1279 discovery and the 1295 recognition by Boniface VIII;
  - the 22 July procession.

*The legend and the rival shrine*

- "St. Mary Magdalen", *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1910), https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09761a.htm. The source for:
  - Gregory of Tours on Ephesus;
  - the Provençal tradition.
- "Archdiocese of Aix", *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1913), https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia_(1913)/Archdiocese_of_Aix. The source for Duchesne: no trace of the Provence tradition before the mid-eleventh century, and its origin in documents connected with Vézelay.
- "Vézelay Abbey", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A9zelay_Abbey. The source for:
  - the 1265 exhumation and the 1267 elevation attended by Louis IX;
  - Vézelay's decline after 1279.
- K. L. Jansen, *The Making of the Magdalen: Preaching and Popular Devotion in the Later Middle Ages* (Princeton, 2000)

*The composite Magdalene*

- "Who framed Mary Magdalene?", U.S. Catholic, https://uscatholic.org/articles/201603/who-framed-mary-magdalene/. The source for:
  - Gregory the Great's homily of 591;
  - the 1969 calendar reform.
- "Mary Magdalene", OrthodoxWiki, https://orthodoxwiki.org/Mary_Magdalene. The source for the Eastern churches keeping the figures distinct.
