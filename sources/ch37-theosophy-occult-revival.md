## Re-sourcing after the Wikipedia strip (2026-11-16, flags 98 and 100)
The Wikipedia strip (2026-11-15) removed two Sources bullets whose only citation was Wikipedia: *The Secret Doctrine* / root races (flag 98) and the 1885 Hodgson report with the 1986 reassessment (flag 100). Three entries now cover them. The chapter text is unchanged.
- **The Secret Doctrine (1888): root races, Lemuria and Atlantis** — Blavatsky's own text, Theosophical University Press online edition (stated as corrected against the 1888 facsimile), https://theosociety.org/pasadena/sd/sd-hp.htm; the vol. II close (pp. 778–798) read on sacred-texts.com, https://sacred-texts.com/the/sd/sd2-3-09.htm, which says "every Root-Race runs through a gamut of seven sub-races" and distinguishes Lemuria from Atlantis. Cited as the claim in its author's own words, not as evidence that the races or continents existed.
- **Root races central to Theosophy; the dispute over race** — J. Strube, "Theosophy, Race, and the Study of Esotericism," *JAAR* 89.4 (2021), 1180–1189, open access (CC BY 4.0), https://doi.org/10.1093/jaarel/lfab109. Read: root races are "central to Theosophical doctrines"; the fifth root race "is called the 'Aryan' race"; Theosophical race theories "had concrete effects". It reports the opposing view of J. A. Santucci, *Nova Religio* 11.3 (2008), 37–63 (https://doi.org/10.1525/nr.2008.11.3.37; record and summary read on relbib.de, not the full text) and of I. Lubelsky (racial discourse vs. racial policy). **Contested, and the entry says so:** whether the root-race teaching is itself racist is a live scholarly dispute (Strube vs. Santucci/Lubelsky); the chapter's own wording ("later seized and twisted by others") is not resolved by either side here. Strube also refers to "often-sensationalist claims that Theosophists were among the precursors of National Socialism" and stresses the "crucial differences" between Theosophy and Nazism.
- **The 1885 Hodgson report and Harrison's 1986 critique** — L. Price, "The Hodgson Report on Theosophy," *Psi Encyclopedia*, Society for Psychical Research (created 2016, updated 2026), https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/hodgson-report-theosophy/. Read: Hodgson concluded the phenomena "fraudulent, and the Mahatmas fictitious" (*Proc. SPR* 3, 1885, 201–400); Harrison, "a senior SPR member", published "J'Accuse" (*JSPR* 53, 1986, 286–310), found "three distinct hands" and "seriously criticised the procedure used by Hodgson".
