# Sources — Chapter 24: Tantra

Research was done by web search during drafting (not memory alone), per the project's sourcing standard. Tantra is a subject unusually overlaid by modern reinvention, so the chapter is careful to separate (a) what the medieval tradition teaches, (b) what history can date, and (c) what later Western reworking added; those distinctions are flagged in the text.

## Definition, scope, and dating
- **Tantra as esoteric traditions in Hinduism and Buddhism; śakti, siddhi, mokṣa/nirvāṇa; mantra/yantra/dīkṣā/guru; "Tantrism" as a 19th-c. Western scholarly construct (etic, not emic)** — "Tantra," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantra
- **Arose no later than the 5th c. CE; manuscripts 600–900; Āgamas/Nigamas and their four parts (jñāna, yoga, kriyā, caryā); twilight language (sandhābhāṣā)** — "Tantra," Encyclopedia.com. https://www.encyclopedia.com/philosophy-and-religion/eastern-religions/hinduism/tantra

## Schools and figures
- **Kashmir Shaivism / Trika, arising after c. 850 CE; Abhinavagupta (c. 950–1020) and the Tantrāloka (5,800 verses, 37 chapters) as the nondual synthesis** — "Abhinavagupta," Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhinavagupta) and Kashmir Shaivism overviews.
- **Kāpālika and Kaula: Bhairava Tantras; cremation-ash asceticism, skull-bowl, propitiation with blood/meat/wine/sexual fluids, deliberate breach of caste and purity; descendants incl. Aghori** — "Kapalika," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapalika
- **Śākta / Śrī Vidyā (right-handed Goddess worship around the Śrī Yantra); Nāth yogis feeding later haṭha yoga** — general reference overviews.

## The left-hand path
- **Vāmācāra (left-hand path) vs. dakṣiṇācāra (right-hand); the pañcamakāra (madya, māṃsa, matsya, mudrā, maithuna); ritual containment; the genuine and unresolved literal-vs-symbolic debate** — "Vamachara," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vamachara

## The subtle body and symbology
- **Three bodies; 72,000 nāḍīs with suṣumṇā/iḍā/piṅgalā; chakras as intersection points with bīja mantras; kundalini** — Yogajala, "Bija Mantras 101" (https://yogajala.com/bija-mantra-101/) and subtle-body overviews.
- **Śrī Yantra: nine interlocking triangles (four up/"male," five down/"female"), bindu, lotus petals, bhūpura with four gates; inward yantra-dhyāna; OM as bīja** — Yoga Basics, "Sri Yantra Meaning." https://www.yogabasics.com/connect/yoga-blog/sri-yantra/

## Buddhist Vajrayāna
- **Origins with the mahāsiddhas; first tantric texts from ~3rd c. but Vajrayāna proper 6th–7th c., term first attested 8th c.; Mahāyoga/Yoginī tantras 8th–10th c. (Guhyasamāja, Cakrasaṃvara, Hevajra); coded language of deity yoga, charnel grounds, wrathful deities, sexual yoga** — "Vajrayana," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vajrayana
- **Hevajra Tantra composed late 8th–10th c. in eastern India; Padmasambhava (747) first transmission (Nyingma); second diffusion of later cycles into Tibet late 10th–11th c.** — "Hevajra Tantra," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hevajra_Tantra

## Material record
- **Chausath Yogini Temple, Khajuraho (c. 885 CE), 64 yoginī cells + the Goddess (65th), 35 surviving; open-air roofless circular plan; Kāpālika/Kaula association; ~13 yoginī temples across India, 8 in Madhya Pradesh** — "Chausath Yogini Temple, Khajuraho," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chausath_Yogini_Temple,_Khajuraho

## Notes on handling
- The chapter draws explicit ancient-vs-modern lines per the project's symbology standard: the **classical** subtle-body/mantra/yantra system is medieval and attested; the **seven-chakra rainbow** color system and the popular equation of **"Tantra" with sacred sex** are flagged as largely 20th-century Western reworkings.
- The subtle body (chakras, nāḍīs, kundalini) is presented as a **contemplative/experiential model, not anatomy** — never asserted as physiology.
- Live debates left open: text dating (esoteric/oral transmission), how literally the pañcamakāra was performed, and the relation of Khajuraho's erotic sculpture to ritual.
- Tibetan Buddhism proper, Nāth/haṭha yoga, Bhakti, and modern global yoga are noted as connections and reserved for their own treatment.
