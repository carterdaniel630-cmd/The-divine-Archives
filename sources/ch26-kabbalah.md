# Sources — Chapter 26: Kabbalah

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. Kabbalah is a tradition that presents itself as ancient and received while its major texts are, to history, medieval pseudepigrapha; the chapter keeps the tradition's own claims and the documentary record clearly distinct, and follows the project's symbology rule in separating attested medieval technique (gematria/notarikon/temurah) from later occult and Christian adaptations.

## Sources cited

*History, texts, and dating*
- **Medieval flowering in Provence and Spain; the three foundational texts (Sefer Yetzirah 2nd–6th c.; Bahir 12th-c. Provence; Zohar 13th-c. Spain, Moses de León); Ein Sof and the sefirot** — "Kabbalah and Hasidism," My Jewish Learning. https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/kabbalah-and-hasidism/
- **Sefer Yetzirah: alphanumeric cosmology; 10 sefirot + 22 letters = 32 paths of wisdom** — "Sefer Yetzirah," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefer_Yetzirah
- **Sefer ha-Bahir (redacted c. 1170–1200, Provence); first mythic treatment of the sefirot; pseudepigraphic to Nehunya ben ha-Kanah** — "Kabbala," New World Encyclopedia. https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Kabbala
- **Zohar authorship: traditional attribution to Shimon bar Yochai (2nd c.) universally rejected by modern scholars; consensus (following Scholem) that Moses de León and/or a circle wrote it c. 1270–1330; the widow-of-Ávila report** — "Zohar," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zohar

*Cosmology and structure*
- **Ein Sof; the ten sefirot; the Tree of Life (Etz Chaim); Keter…Malkhut/Shekhinah** — "Tree of life (Kabbalah)," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_of_life_(Kabbalah)
- **The four worlds — Atzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah, Assiah (read from Isaiah 43:7)** — "Four Worlds," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Worlds
- **Merkavah/Hekhalot mysticism (seven palaces, throne-chariot ascent) as the late-antique background** — general reference (Scholem; Hekhalot literature overviews).

*Schools and figures*

## Notes on handling
- Traditional attributions (Zohar→bar Yochai; Bahir→Nehunya; Sefer Yetzirah→Abraham/Akiva) are recorded as **pious pseudepigraphy**, distinct from the texts' medieval/late-antique dating.
- Gematria and its sister techniques are presented as **real, attested devotional/interpretive arts**, explicitly **not** a cipher that decodes verifiable hidden facts or predicts events — per the project's symbology standard.
- **Christian Kabbalah** (Pico, Reuchlin), **Hermetic Qabalah** (Golden Dawn), and the modern "Kabbalah Centre" are named as **later adaptations** and reserved for their own chapters, kept distinct from the medieval Jewish tradition.
- Left open: the precise authorship of the Zohar (de León alone vs. a circle) and the Bahir; how much older esoteric material underlies them; and the exact channels (Merkavah, Neoplatonism) feeding the sefirot.
- Lurianic Kabbalah (16th c., tzimtzum/tikkun) and Hasidism (18th c.) are noted as downstream developments belonging to later eras.
