# Sources — Chapter 35: New Religious Movements

Per the project sourcing standard. The chapter treats NRMs as religions (not "cults"), keeps their revelatory claims as faith (recent claims neither more nor less faith than ancient ones), flags the contested boundary of the category, and separates the empirically-rejected "brainwashing" model from the documented harms of specific groups. Expanded 2026-09 into the five-section template (narrative / believer's lens / skeptical lens / symbology / evidence).

## Notes
- Revelatory claims (golden plates, founders' scriptures, "thetans") = faith, not adjudicated; recency neither adds nor subtracts faith-status.
- The "brainwashing"/coercive-mind-control model is flagged as empirically rejected (APA 1987; Barker's conversion data), while Jonestown, Waco, Aum, and Heaven's Gate remain documented harms — belief and behavior kept in separate columns.
- Stark–Bainbridge church–sect–cult typology and Wallis's world-rejecting/affirming/accommodating scheme used as the sociological frame.
