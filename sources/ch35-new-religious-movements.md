# Sources — Chapter 35: New Religious Movements

Per the project sourcing standard. The chapter treats NRMs as religions (not "cults"), keeps their revelatory claims as faith (recent claims neither more nor less faith than ancient ones), and flags the contested boundary of the category.

## Sources cited
- **Definition of NRMs; the move away from "cult"; innovation/counterculture/charisma; examples (LDS, JW, Adventists, Bahá'í, Falun Gong, Paganism)** — "New religious movement," Britannica. https://www.britannica.com/topic/new-religious-movement
- **19th-c. American millenarianism producing the Adventists and Jehovah's Witnesses** — "New religious movements in the United States," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_religious_movements_in_the_United_States
- **The pejorative "cult" label and judging movements by conduct** — Religion Media Centre, "Factsheet: New Religious Movements." https://religionmediacentre.org.uk/factsheets/factsheet-new-religious-movements/
- **Category overview; charismatic leadership; syncretism** — Study.com, "New Religious Movements." https://study.com/academy/lesson/contemporary-religious-movements.html

## Notes
- Revelatory claims (golden plates, founders' scriptures, "thetans") = faith, not adjudicated.
- Jonestown (1978) and Heaven's Gate (1997) named as documented tragedies; the "cult" alarm reserved for actual harm, not unfamiliar belief.
- Hare Krishna noted as a transplanted Bhakti order; forward/back links to Spiritualism and Theosophy.
