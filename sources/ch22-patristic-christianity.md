# Sources — Chapter 22: Patristic Christianity

Research was done by web search during drafting (not memory alone), per the project's sourcing standard. Specific factual claims are tied to the sources below; contested points are flagged as contested in the chapter, and faith claims are kept distinct from historically attested facts.

## The councils, the Fathers, and the canon
- **Nicaea (325), homoousios, Arius, Athanasius** — Britannica, "First Council of Nicaea." https://www.britannica.com/event/First-Council-of-Nicaea-325
- **Constantinople (381), Cappadocians, divinity of the Holy Spirit, three hypostases in one ousia** — Britannica, "First Council of Constantinople." https://www.britannica.com/event/First-Council-of-Constantinople-381
- **Chalcedon (451), "two natures," miaphysite response, Oriental Orthodox / Church of the East schism** — "Chalcedonian schism," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcedonian_schism
- **Apostolic Fathers — definition, ~70–150 dating, grouping around Nicaea** — "Apostolic Fathers," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Fathers
- **Ignatius of Antioch — seven-letter middle recension; Lightfoot (1889) consensus and German dissent; forgery minority** — "Ignatius of Antioch," Evidence Unseen. https://evidenceunseen.com/theology/historical-theology/ignatius-of-antioch
- **Didache — c. 100 with a contested range (50 to 4th c.); composite, undated text** — Chris Thomas, "The Didache: Its Origin and Significance," EarlyChurch.org.uk. https://www.earlychurch.org.uk/article_didache.html
- **Marcion's canon; Muratorian Fragment (c. 170–200); Irenaeus and the four Gospels; Carthage 397** — "Muratorian fragment," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muratorian_fragment
- **Athanasius, 39th Festal Letter (367) — first surviving complete 27-book NT list; caution that it ratified an existing consensus rather than inventing the canon** — Michael J. Kruger, "10 Misconceptions about the NT Canon, #10," Canon Fodder. https://michaeljkruger.com/10-misconceptions-about-the-nt-canon-10-athanasius-festal-letter-367-a-d-is-the-first-complete-list-of-new-testament-books/

## Symbology and sacred encoding
- **Ichthys acrostic (Iēsous Christos Theou Yios Sōtēr); earliest witness Clement of Alexandria (c. 200); Tertullian's "little fishes"** — "Symbolism of the Fish," Catholic Encyclopedia (New Advent). https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06083a.htm
- **Chi-Rho and the labarum (Eusebius) vs. the staurogram (Lactantius); Constantine's vision at the Milvian Bridge; Chi-Rho on coins only from c. 319–320; nomina sacra** — "Labarum," Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labarum

## Notes on handling
- Faith claims (the Trinity, Christ's two natures, the Spirit's divinity, divine guidance of councils, Constantine's vision as divine) are recorded as belief and neither proven nor disproven.
- Live scholarly debates are presented as open: the Ignatian recensions, the Didache's date, whether 553 (ecumenical) or 543 (local synod) condemned Origen, and exactly what sign Constantine used.
- Gnosticism, later Eastern/Western monastic developments, and the medieval church are reserved for their own chapters.
