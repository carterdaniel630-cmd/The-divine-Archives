# Sources — Chapter 7: Pre-Exilic Israel: How a People Became Monotheist

Per the project sourcing standard, this citation log records the real, checkable sources behind the chapter's specific claims. It is derived from the Sources section published with the chapter; contested points are flagged as contested in the chapter text, and faith claims are kept distinct from historically attested facts.

## Sources cited

*Origins and emergence*
- [Wikipedia: Merneptah Stele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merneptah_Stele);
  [Biblical Archaeology Society: the first mention of Israel](https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/ancient-cultures/ancient-israel/does-the-merneptah-stele-contain-the-first-mention-of-israel/).
- On Yahweh's southern origins: [Wikipedia: Kenite hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenite_hypothesis)
  and the "Yhw in the land of the Shasu" inscriptions.

*Yahweh, El, Asherah, and the rise of monotheism*
- Mark S. Smith, *The Early History of God: Yahweh and the Other Deities in
  Ancient Israel*; *The Origins of Biblical Monotheism*.
- [World History Encyclopedia: Asherah](https://www.worldhistory.org/Asherah/);
  [Biblical Archaeology Society: Kuntillet Ajrud](https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/biblical-artifacts/puzzling-finds-from-kuntillet-ajrud-a-drawing-of-god-labeled-yahweh-and-his-asherah-or-the-egyptian-god-bes/);
  [Wikipedia: Khirbet el-Qom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khirbet_el-Qom).
- The Ugaritic background: the texts from Ras Shamra.

*Cult, reform, and folk religion*
- [Biblical Archaeology Society: The Religious Reforms of Hezekiah and Josiah](https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/book/aspects-monotheism/the-religious-reforms-of-hezekiah-and-josiah/);

*Text, inscriptions, and history*
- The Documentary Hypothesis and its revisions (Wellhausen; and critics/updaters
  such as R. E. Friedman, *Who Wrote the Bible?*, and later fragmentary/supplementary
  models).
- [Wikipedia: Tel Dan Stele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Dan_stele);
  the Mesha Stele (Moabite Stone); [Wikipedia: Ketef Hinnom scrolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketef_Hinnom_scrolls).

*Note on sourcing:* Researched from current biblical scholarship, archaeology, and
epigraphy rather than memory alone, per the project standard. This chapter carefully
distinguishes the **historical** reconstruction from the **biblical** narrative, and
flags the live debates — the meaning of "his Asherah," the scale of the United
Monarchy, the origin of Yahweh, and the composition of the Torah — as disputes to be
reported, not resolved. Reviewer note: written with an emphasis on *how we know*
(method, inscriptions, comparative texts) to keep it educational; the exilic
transformation and the birth of Judaism proper are deliberately held for the
Second Temple chapter in the next era.
