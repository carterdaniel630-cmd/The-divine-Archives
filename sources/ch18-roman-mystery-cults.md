*Note on sourcing:* Researched from standard reference and scholarly sources (reference encyclopedias,
sourced overviews, and the mainstream scholarship of Cumont's critics, Ulansey, Smith, and others they
summarize) rather than memory alone, per the project standard. The chapter is careful about the **evidence
problem** peculiar to secret cults — flagging that Mithraic doctrine is reconstructed from images alone and
that the astronomical theory is unproven — corrects the widespread but overstated claim that **Christianity
copied the mysteries** (noting Mithras was no dying-rising god, the adverse chronology, the contested
"dying and rising god" category, and the danger of **parallelomania** and bias), distinguishes the Persian
*name* of Mithra from the largely new Roman cult, and cross-references the Greek mysteries (Chapter 8), Rome
(Chapter 13), and the era's rival salvation religions (Chapters 16–17). Written to be educational, and to
present the mysteries on their own terms as one of antiquity's great answers to the fear of death.
