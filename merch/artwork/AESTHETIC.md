**Movement name: *Gilded Vellum***

## Philosophy

*Gilded Vellum* treats each sacred symbol the way a monastic scriptorium treated
a single illuminated initial: as an object worth a week of a master's attention.
The mark is never merely printed onto the ground — it is **grown from it**, as
though the pigment were laid, burnished, and left to age into the fibre of the
skin. The result should read as a leaf lifted from a book that has survived
centuries: warm, quiet, and unmistakably made by hand.

**Ground.** Aged vellum — a warm ecru that is never flat. It carries the faint
mottling of hide, a soft foxing of age at the edges, and a gentle vignette that
draws the eye inward, the way candlelight falls on an open page. The parchment is
a living surface, not a backdrop.

**Line.** Bronze and copper drawn as if with a fine reed pen: tapering from a
confident trunk to hair-thin terminal twigs, every stroke bearing the slight
irregularity of a human hand rather than the dead uniformity of vector output.
Metal, not ink — the line should catch an implied light along its spine, darker
in the hollows, brighter on the rise, so the whole figure sits with the low sheen
of tarnished gilt.

**Symmetry & rhythm.** Each symbol is composed within a disciplined circle — the
manuscript roundel — with a mirror or radial order that feels inevitable, patient,
and calm. Ornament repeats the way a scribe's border repeats: enough to reward
sustained looking, never enough to shout.

**Restraint.** No drop shadows, no gradients that betray the screen, no clutter.
Two or three metallic values, one warm ground, and empty vellum given room to
breathe. The craftsmanship lives in the *precision of the line and the honesty of
the ground*, not in effects piled on top.

**The bar.** Every piece in this set must look meticulously crafted — the product
of countless hours and master-level execution — such that the fourteen symbols,
seen together, read as a single hand working a single book. If a mark looks
generated rather than gilded, it fails.
